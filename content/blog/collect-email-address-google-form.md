<p style="font-size: inherit;">Google Forms will not record anyone's email address unless you tell it to. The setting ships switched off, which catches out plenty of people who assumed every response arrived with a name attached.</p>

<p>Switching it on takes about four seconds. Choosing between the two collection modes is the part worth a moment's thought, because one of them makes a Google account mandatory for every single respondent.</p>

<blockquote><p>The control sits under <strong>Settings</strong>, in the <strong>Responses</strong> section. <strong>Verified</strong> takes the address from the account someone is signed in with, and forces that sign-in. <strong>Responder input</strong> lets them type it, with no account needed and no check that it is real.</p></blockquote>

<h2>Where the setting lives</h2>

<p>The email option is not in the question editor, which is where most people go looking. It sits in form-level settings, one section down.</p>

<ol>
<li>Click <strong>Settings</strong> in the top bar, beside Questions and Responses.</li>
<li>Expand the <strong>Responses</strong> section.</li>
<li>Open the <strong>Collect email addresses</strong> dropdown and pick a mode.</li>
</ol>

<p>Fresh out of the box it reads <strong>Do not collect</strong>. Look at the row directly beneath it: <strong>Send responders a copy of their response</strong> is greyed out, and Google spells out why with <em>Requires Collect email addresses</em>.</p>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_ce1_do_not_collect_default_e320bd5132.jpg" alt="Google Forms Settings Responses section with Collect email addresses set to Do not collect and Send responders a copy greyed out" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<p>There is no save button. Your choice applies the moment you make it, and the form itself picks up a line confirming what changed.</p>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_ce0_collecting_notice_543285f277.png" alt="Google Forms editor showing the notice this form is automatically collecting emails from all respondents with a Change settings link" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<p>That notice appears in the editor only. It is the quickest way to confirm collection is on without opening Settings a second time.</p>

<h2>Verified or Responder input: which one should you pick?</h2>

<p>Both modes put an address in your response sheet. What separates them is who vouches for that address.</p>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_ce2_three_options_3be2bc9e4a.jpg" alt="Collect email addresses dropdown open in Google Forms showing Do not collect, Verified and Responder input" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<tr><td>Where the address comes from</td><td>The signed-in Google account</td><td>Typed by the respondent</td></tr>

<p>Choose <strong>Verified</strong> and Google states the cost right under the dropdown: <em>Respondents will be required to sign in to Google</em>.</p>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_ce3_verified_signin_064d490149.jpg" alt="Collect email addresses set to Verified in Google Forms with the note respondents will be required to sign in to Google" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<p>That one line is the entire trade-off. Inside a school or a company already on Google Workspace, it buys you accurate addresses for nothing.</p>

<p>On a public signup form it becomes a wall. Anyone without a Google account, or signed into the wrong one, stops there and never submits.</p>

<p>Sign-in requirements ripple further than this one setting, and <a href="https://formester.com/blog/how-to-enable-access-for-google-forms/">how form access works in Google Forms</a> covers the rest of them.</p>

<h2>How to stop collecting email addresses</h2>

<p>Set the same dropdown back to <strong>Do not collect</strong>. Responses already in your sheet keep the addresses they captured, so turning it off does not reach backwards.</p>

<p>That detail matters if you promised anonymity partway through a survey.</p>

<p>The setting alone will not get you there either, since a name or an employee ID question identifies someone just as well. Our guide to <a href="https://formester.com/blog/how-to-make-anonymous-google-form-or-survey/">making a Google Form anonymous</a> covers the questions you need to strip out.</p>

<h2>Can people see your email address on Google Forms?</h2>

<p>Respondents cannot see each other's addresses. Response data is visible to people with edit access to the form or to the linked spreadsheet, and to nobody else.</p>

<p>Your own address is a separate question. Respondents do not see the form owner's email while filling anything in, but it does show at the top when you preview your own form, which is what convinces people it is public.</p>

<p>There is no toggle that collects an address and then hides it from your responses. Collection and visibility are the same switch, so if you need the submission without the identity, do not collect the address at all.</p>

<p>What you can control is who else gets to read it. Response data inherits the sharing permissions of the form and its spreadsheet, so the fix for exposure is access control rather than a hide button.</p>

<h2>Can you use the responses as a mailing list?</h2>

<p>Yes, and it is the most common reason people turn the setting on in the first place.</p>

<p>Addresses arrive in an Email address column alongside every other answer, ready to export. If responses are not reaching a spreadsheet yet, <a href="https://formester.com/blog/google-forms-to-google-sheets/">connecting Google Forms to Google Sheets</a> sorts that out.</p>

<p>Switching collection on also frees up the row underneath it, where you can send each respondent a receipt of what they submitted.</p>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_ce4_send_copy_options_ca06308d16.jpg" alt="Send responders a copy of their response dropdown in Google Forms showing Off, When requested and Always" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>When requested</strong> adds a checkbox to the form so respondents opt in to their own copy.</li>
<li><strong>Always</strong> sends the receipt on every submission, which is the closest Google Forms gets to a confirmation step.</li>
</ul>

<p>Two cautions before you treat any of this as a subscriber list. Google Forms gives you no consent checkbox and no unsubscribe link, so an explicit opt-in question is on you to add, worded specifically enough that people know what they agreed to.</p>

<h2>Where email collection in Google Forms runs out</h2>

<p>Turning the setting on gets you a column of text. It does not get you a column of working addresses, and the distance between those two things is where most collected lists quietly fall apart.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>A typo sails straight through.</strong> Responder input accepts anything shaped like an email, so <em>jhon@gmial.com</em> lands in your sheet looking perfectly respectable.</li>
<li><strong>Nothing proves ownership.</strong> Someone can type your address, a colleague's, or an invented one, and the submission counts all the same.</li>
<li><strong>Deduplication needs sign-in.</strong> The <em>Limit to 1 response</em> toggle sits under a heading that reads REQUIRES SIGN IN, so stopping repeat entries means making Verified compulsory.</li>
</ul>

<p>Verified solves the first two problems by demanding a Google account, which is the trade the whole feature revolves around. Accuracy or reach, and you only get to pick one.</p>

<h2>How to collect verified emails without a Google account</h2>

<p>Formester handles this on the field rather than the form, and that is precisely why no Google account is involved. Verification attaches to the email question itself.</p>

<p>Click the email field in the builder and open the <strong>Advanced</strong> tab.</p>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/formester_f1_email_advanced_tab_301a477d61.jpg" alt="Formester form builder with an email field selected and the Advanced tab open in the right panel" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<p>Three separate toggles live there, each aimed at a different one of the gaps above.</p>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/formester_f2_unique_confirmation_validation_5b5778e6c4.png" alt="Formester email field Advanced settings showing the Unique, Email Confirmation and Email Validation toggles" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Email Confirmation</strong> emails a one-time code and only accepts the submission once the code matches. This is the ownership check Google Forms has no equivalent for without sign-in.</li>
<li><strong>Email Validation</strong> tests deliverability while the respondent is still on the form, so dead domains get caught before they reach your results.</li>
<li><strong>Unique</strong> allows one submission per address, and it does that without asking anyone to log in anywhere.</li>
</ul>

<p>If everyone filling in your form already sits on the same Google Workspace domain, Verified in Google Forms does an equivalent job at no cost. Use that instead.</p>

<p>Paying for verification earns its keep when your audience is the general public, where a Google account cannot be assumed.</p>
