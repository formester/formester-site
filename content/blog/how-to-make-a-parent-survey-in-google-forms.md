<p style="font-size: inherit;">To make a parent survey in Google Forms, open a blank form, write a title and a description that says how long it takes and whether it is anonymous, add eight to twelve questions typed to the answers you want, leave Collect email addresses off so no parent needs a Google account, and publish with a close date.</p>

<p>It takes about twenty minutes, and the results come back as charts you can read the same evening.</p>

<p>The seven steps below build it. After that come 30 questions teachers actually ask, grouped by the point in the year, with the Google Forms question type for each, and the settings that decide whether parents answer honestly.</p>

<div class="tldr">
<p><strong>Quick answer</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The build:</strong> blank form, title and description, Multiple choice and Linear scale for anything you will count, one Paragraph question for the rest, a section per topic, then Publish.</li>
<li><strong>Anonymous by default:</strong> Collect email addresses on Do not collect and Limit to 1 response off. Parents answer on a phone with no sign-in, and you cannot see who said what.</li>
<li><strong>Questions by season:</strong> start of year is about the child. Conference time is about learning and communication. End of year is about what to keep and change.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 id="build">How do you make a parent survey in Google Forms?</h2>

<ol class="steps">
<li>
<h3>Open a blank form and write the title and description</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/30883db4ee7fd566_gforms-ps-step1-blank-title-description.gif" alt="A blank Google Form with the title changed to Parent Survey: Start of the Year and a description saying it takes five minutes, is anonymous and closes on a date" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Go to <a href="https://forms.google.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener">forms.google.com</a> and click <strong>Blank form</strong>. Name it for the moment in the year, and use the description for four facts: how long it takes, whether it is anonymous, one form per child and the closing date. Parents decide whether to start in those two lines.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Add the first questions and type them to the answers</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/1982fb8d13bf9dc1_gforms-ps-step2-first-question-options.gif" alt="Typing How does your child feel about school so far into a Multiple choice question in Google Forms and adding four options from Excited to go to Struggling" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Anything you want to count gets <strong>Multiple choice</strong>, with four or five options that cover the range. Anything that applies more than once gets <strong>Checkboxes</strong>. Save <strong>Paragraph</strong> for the two or three questions where you want the parent's own words, because you will read every one of those by hand.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Add a rating scale with labels at both ends</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/2cc580fce023f7f9_gforms-ps-step2b-linear-scale-labels.gif" alt="A Linear scale question from 1 to 5 in Google Forms with the labels Not at all and Very well typed at each end" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>For "how well" and "how often" questions, use <strong>Linear scale</strong> from 1 to 5 and type a label at each end, such as Not at all and Very well. Keep every scale on the same range, so the results chart the same way and you can compare a September answer with a June one.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Add an open question and split the form into sections</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/2a9dc9e7303af075_gforms-ps-step3-paragraph-add-section.gif" alt="Adding a Paragraph question asking what the teacher should know about the child, then clicking Add section in the Google Forms toolbar and naming it Communication" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Add a <strong>Paragraph</strong> question such as What should I know about your child that would help me teach them? Then click <strong>Add section</strong> in the side toolbar and name the next page, for example Communication. Sections turn a twelve-question form into three short pages, which is what makes it finishable on a phone at pickup.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Ask how parents want to hear from you</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/6060c8061532c744_gforms-ps-step4-checkboxes-contact.gif" alt="Adding a Checkboxes question in the Communication section of a Google Form asking how parents would like to hear from the teacher, with Email, Text message, Phone call and Printed note as options" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>In the Communication section, add a <strong>Checkboxes</strong> question: How would you like to hear from me? Email, text, a call and a printed note in the bag are the usual four. This is the one answer that changes what you do the next week, so it goes in every parent survey, whatever the season.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Keep it anonymous and allow a second child</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/d2cccaafa2fc2913_gforms-ps-step5-anonymous-settings-confirmation.gif" alt="The Google Forms Settings tab with Collect email addresses on Do not collect and Limit to 1 response off, then the confirmation message edited to mention a second form for another child" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>In <strong>Settings</strong>, leave <strong>Collect email addresses</strong> on Do not collect and <strong>Limit to 1 response</strong> off. Both are the defaults on a blank form, and both are what keeps the survey open to a parent without a Google account.</p>
<p>Under Presentation, keep <strong>Show link to submit another response</strong> on and edit the confirmation message to say a parent with two children in the class can fill in a second form.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Publish, copy the link and set the close date</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/7ea9f7b99241151f_gforms-ps-step6-publish-link-close-date.gif" alt="Publishing the parent survey in Google Forms, copying the shortened responder link, then opening Published options and choosing Set close date or response limit with a date picker" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Click <strong>Publish</strong>, check it says Anyone with the link, and copy the responder link with Shorten URL ticked. Then click the <strong>Published</strong> button again, choose <strong>Set close date or response limit</strong> and pick the date from the description. A closed survey gives you a fixed set of answers to report on rather than a moving one.</p>
</li>
</ol>

<p>Two wording rules make the results usable. Ask about one thing per question, so "How well did my updates and homework notes keep you informed?" becomes two questions. And do not lead: "How much did you enjoy the class newsletter?" assumes they did. "How useful was the class newsletter?" with a scale lets them say not very.</p>

<h2 id="anonymous">Should a parent survey be anonymous?</h2>

<p>For feedback about you, yes. Parents are kinder and vaguer when their name is attached, and the honest answer about homework or communication is the one you need. For information about a specific child, no, because you need to know whose child it is. Google Forms handles both, but with different settings, so they are usually two forms.</p>

<div class="table-wrap">
<table>
<thead><tr><th>You want</th><th>Collect email addresses</th><th>Limit to 1 response</th><th>Name question</th><th>What you get</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Honest feedback about the class</td><td>Do not collect</td><td>Off</td><td>None</td><td>Anonymous, no sign-in, a parent could answer twice</td></tr>
<tr><td>Information about each child</td><td>Do not collect</td><td>Off</td><td>Child's name, required</td><td>No sign-in, answers tied to the child, not the parent's account</td></tr>
<tr><td>One response per parent, verified</td><td>Verified</td><td>On</td><td>Optional</td><td>Requires a Google sign-in, which many parents do not have on their phone</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

<p>Say which one it is in the description. "Anonymous" in the first line of a survey changes the answers more than any question does. <a href="/blog/how-to-make-anonymous-google-form-or-survey/">Making a Google Form anonymous</a> goes through the settings in detail.</p>

<h2 id="responses">How do you get parents to answer?</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Make it a phone job:</strong> most parents open the link in a text or a school app on a phone. Twelve questions in three sections, no sign-in, no typing beyond the open questions.</li>
<li><strong>Give a deadline and mean it:</strong> the close date in the description, the same date in the reminder, and the form actually closing on that date.</li>
<li><strong>Put a QR code where parents stand:</strong> the pickup gate, the classroom door, the conference sign-in table. <a href="/blog/how-to-make-a-qr-code-for-a-google-form/">A QR code for a Google Form</a> takes two minutes.</li>
<li><strong>Send it twice:</strong> once on the day, once two days before it closes. The second send usually doubles the response.</li>
<li><strong>Offer the languages your families speak:</strong> Google Forms has no translation feature, so a second copy of the form in the other main home language is the honest option. Phone browsers can translate the page, but not reliably.</li>
<li><strong>Tell them what happened:</strong> a two-line note the following week saying what you heard and what you changed is why they answer the next one.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="results">How do you read the results?</h2>

<p>Open the form and click <strong>Responses</strong>. <strong>Summary</strong> shows a chart per question, which for Multiple choice, Checkboxes and Linear scale questions is the whole answer. <strong>Individual</strong> walks through one parent at a time, which is how you read the Paragraph answers. For anything you want to sort or keep, <strong>Link to Sheets</strong> puts every response in a spreadsheet.</p>

<p>Google Forms does not average a scale or compare September with June. Export both surveys to Sheets and put the two averages side by side, which takes one formula per question. <a href="/blog/how-to-make-pie-charts-in-google-forms/">Pie charts in Google Forms</a> covers what the Responses tab draws and how to get it into a slide for the staff meeting.</p>

<h2 id="limits">What Google Forms cannot do for a parent survey</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Translate the form:</strong> one language per form. A bilingual class means two forms and two sets of results.</li>
<li><strong>Remind people:</strong> no reminder emails. You send them.</li>
<li><strong>Stop a second vote without a sign-in:</strong> Limit to 1 response requires a Google account, so an anonymous survey is always open to a repeat.</li>
<li><strong>Know which child:</strong> with anonymity on, a parent of two has to say which child in the form, and there is no way to prefill it per family.</li>
<li><strong>Compare over time:</strong> each form is its own set of results. The trend lives in a spreadsheet you build.</li>
<li><strong>Look like the school:</strong> a header image and a theme colour, with Google's footer on every page.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="faq">Google Forms parent survey FAQ</h2>
<details><summary>Can I use Google Forms to create a parent survey?</summary><div>Yes. Open a blank form, add eight to twelve questions using Multiple choice and Linear scale for anything you will count and Paragraph for the parent's own words, leave Collect email addresses off so no Google account is needed, then publish and share the link. Results appear as charts in the Responses tab.</div></details>
<details><summary>Is a Google Forms parent survey really anonymous?</summary><div>It is when Collect email addresses is on Do not collect, Limit to 1 response is off and there is no name question. Google Forms then records no account, address or name with the answers. Switching on Limit to 1 response requires a sign-in and ends the anonymity.</div></details>
<details><summary>What are good questions for a parent survey?</summary><div>At the start of the year, ask about the child: strengths, worries, how they learn, home support and how parents want to hear from you. Mid-year, ask how informed parents feel and what they want to discuss. At the end, ask what to keep and what to change. The 30 questions above are grouped that way.</div></details>
<details><summary>How many questions should a parent survey have?</summary><div>Eight to twelve, split into two or three sections. Most parents answer on a phone at pickup or in the evening, and every extra screen loses a few of them. Keep the open questions to two or three, since you read each of those by hand.</div></details>
<details><summary>Do parents need a Google account to answer?</summary><div>Not unless you switch on Limit to 1 response or set Collect email addresses to Verified. With the blank form defaults, any parent can open the link in a phone browser and answer without signing in, which is what you want for a whole-class survey.</div></details>
<details><summary>How do I let a parent with two children in my class answer twice?</summary><div>Keep Show link to submit another response on under Presentation settings and say so in the confirmation message. If the survey is about each child, add a required Child's name question so you can tell the two forms apart.</div></details>
<details><summary>How do I see the parent survey results?</summary><div>Open the form and click Responses. Summary shows a chart per question, Individual shows one parent's answers at a time, and Link to Sheets puts every response in a spreadsheet for sorting and averages. Set a close date so the results stop changing while you read them.</div></details>
<details><summary>Does Google Forms have a parent survey template?</summary><div>No. The template gallery has feedback templates such as Event Feedback but nothing for parents, and a blank form with the questions above takes about the same time. Formester's parent feedback form template is a ready-made alternative that parents can answer without signing in.</div></details>
