<p style="font-size: inherit;">Google Forms is the free way to hand a class a quiz that grades itself, an exit ticket, a morning check-in or a permission slip. You build the form once, switch on quiz mode when it needs a score, and give it to students through Google Classroom or a link.</p>

<p>The tool is the same for every use. What changes is a handful of settings: whether the form grades, whether it knows who answered, whether a student can answer twice, and what they see afterwards. Get those right and the rest is writing questions.</p>

<p>This guide covers the seven uses that come up most, the setting each one needs, a recorded build of a self-grading quiz, the Google Classroom hand-off, and the things Google Forms cannot do for a class.</p>

<div class="tldr">
<p><strong>Quick answer</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Best for:</strong> quizzes with an answer key, exit tickets, check-ins, attendance, permission slips, homework hand-in and end-of-unit feedback. All free with any Google account.</li>
<li><strong>Hand it out:</strong> a Google Classroom quiz assignment if your school has Workspace for Education, otherwise the responder link or a QR code. Classroom adds locked mode on managed Chromebooks and grade importing.</li>
<li><strong>Cannot do:</strong> a timer, a random question bank, or locked mode on anything but a school-managed Chromebook. One response per student and file uploads both need a Google sign-in.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 id="uses">What can you use Google Forms for with students?</h2>

<p>Seven uses cover most of what a class needs. Each one is an ordinary form plus one or two settings, and the setting is what makes it work, so it is listed under each use.</p>

<h3>1. A quiz that grades itself</h3>

<p>Turn on <strong>Make this a quiz</strong> in Settings, set the answer key and points on each question, and Google Forms marks Multiple choice, Checkboxes, Dropdown, Short answer and grid questions the moment a student submits. Paragraph answers wait for you in the Responses tab.</p>

<p>The setting that matters is <strong>Release grades</strong>. Immediately after each submission suits practice. Later, after manual review keeps scores hidden until you have graded the written answers, and it needs Collect email addresses switched on.</p>

<h3>2. An exit ticket</h3>

<p>Three questions at the end of a lesson: one thing you learned, one question you still have, and a Linear scale from 1 to 5 on how confident you feel. Keep it under a minute so students finish it before the bell.</p>

<p>No special setting. Leave quiz mode off, collect the email address if you want to follow up with a named student, and read the Summary tab before the next lesson.</p>

<h3>3. A daily check-in</h3>

<p>A mood scale, a Multiple choice on how the week is going and an optional Paragraph for anything they want you to know. It is the same form every day, so make the title the check-in and the description the promise about who reads it.</p>

<p>The setting that matters is <strong>Collect email addresses</strong>. Verified records the school account automatically. Do not collect makes it anonymous. Say which one in the description, because the answers change with it.</p>

<h3>4. Attendance and sign-in</h3>

<p>A form with the student's name and a Dropdown for the class or period, opened from a QR code on the board. Every submission carries a timestamp, so the sheet behind the form is the register.</p>

<p><strong>Limit to 1 response</strong> stops a student signing in twice, but it forces a Google sign-in. Link the form to Sheets so the register updates as students arrive.</p>

<h3>5. Permission slips and parent information</h3>

<p>Field trip permission, emergency contacts, photo consent and the start-of-year information form all fit: the student's name, a Checkboxes question for the consents and a Short answer for the parent's name as a signature line.</p>

<p>Switch on <strong>Allow response editing</strong> so a parent can fix a phone number later, and set Collect email addresses to Responder input so the confirmation has somewhere to go. A typed name is not an e-signature, so check what your school accepts.</p>

<h3>6. Homework and project hand-in</h3>

<p>A File upload question lets students attach a document, a photo of their work or a video. Add a Dropdown for the assignment and a Short answer for the title, and every file lands in a folder in your Drive named after the form.</p>

<p>File upload questions require students to sign in to Google, and the files use your Drive storage, not theirs. Set the file count and size limit on the question.</p>

<h3>7. Course feedback</h3>

<p>At the end of a unit: a Linear scale on pace, a Multiple choice on what helped most, a Paragraph on what to change. Anonymous by default, which is the point. Students write more when the form does not know who they are.</p>

<p>Leave Collect email addresses on Do not collect and Limit to 1 response off, and say in the description that it is anonymous. The <a href="/blog/how-to-make-a-questionnaire-in-google-forms/">questionnaire guide</a> covers the question types for surveys.</p>

<h2 id="build">How do you make a Google Form for students?</h2>

<p>The recording below builds a short science quiz, because a quiz needs every setting the other uses need and two more. Skip step 2 for anything that is not graded.</p>

<ol class="steps">
<li>
<h3>Start from Blank form or an Education template</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/23766cf53a4e09cf_gforms-st-step1-gallery-blank.gif" alt="The Google Forms template gallery with the Education row of Blank Quiz, Exit Ticket, Assessment, Worksheet and Course Evaluation, then Blank form opening" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Open <a href="https://forms.google.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener">forms.google.com</a> and click <strong>Template gallery</strong>. The Education row has Blank Quiz, Exit Ticket, Assessment, Worksheet and Course Evaluation, each with the right settings already on. Blank form gives you one empty question and nothing switched on.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Switch on Make this a quiz</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/36095f1b217442a3_gforms-st-step2-make-quiz.gif" alt="Switching on Make this a quiz in the Google Forms Settings tab, which adds Release grades and Respondent settings" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Open the <strong>Settings</strong> tab and turn on <strong>Make this a quiz</strong>. Every question now gets an Answer key link and a points box, and the Responses tab gains a score summary. Do this before you write the questions, so you set the key as you go.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Write the questions and set the answer key</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/c94abc98e76d1216_gforms-st-step3-answer-key.gif" alt="Typing a Multiple choice question in Google Forms, opening Answer key, ticking Mercury as correct and setting 2 points" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Add a question, pick <strong>Multiple choice</strong>, type the options, then click <strong>Answer key</strong>, tick the correct option and set the points. Add answer feedback if a student who gets it wrong should see an explanation or a video. Short answer questions take a list of accepted answers.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Choose what students see</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/7706d80475eec0d2_gforms-st-step4-release-grades.gif" alt="Choosing Later, after manual review under Release grades and switching off Correct answers and Point values in Google Forms" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>In Settings, under <strong>Release grades</strong>, pick Immediately after each submission or Later, after manual review. Under <strong>Respondent settings</strong>, choose whether they see missed questions, correct answers and point values. For a test, hide all three until you release grades.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Decide who can respond</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/0d5a7a284ba3995d_gforms-st-step5-who-responds.gif" alt="The Responses settings in Google Forms with Collect email addresses on Verified and Limit to 1 response switched on" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Under Responses, set <strong>Collect email addresses</strong> to Verified so each score carries the student's account, and turn on <strong>Limit to 1 response</strong>. School accounts also see Restrict to users in your domain. Leave it on for a class and turn it off for parents.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Publish and hand it out</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/884db3967ae39737_gforms-st-step6-publish-link.gif" alt="Publishing the Google Forms quiz and copying the shortened responder link" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Click <strong>Publish</strong>, then copy the responder link with Shorten URL ticked, or make a <a href="/blog/how-to-make-a-qr-code-for-a-google-form/">QR code</a> for the board. If your school has Google Classroom, attach the form to a quiz assignment instead, which is the next section.</p>
</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="classroom">How do you assign a Google Form to students in Google Classroom?</h2>

<p>Google Classroom needs a Google Workspace for Education account, which comes from the school rather than a personal Gmail. With one, a Forms quiz becomes an assignment with a due date and two options a plain link never gets.</p>

<p>In Classroom open the class, click <strong>Classwork</strong>, then <strong>Create</strong> and <strong>Quiz assignment</strong>. Classroom creates a blank Google Forms quiz and attaches it. Click the attachment to build the quiz, or use Create and Assignment to attach a form you already built from Drive.</p>

<p><strong>Locked mode on Chromebooks:</strong> students on school-managed Chromebooks cannot open other tabs while the quiz is open, and you get an email if one of them leaves the quiz and comes back. Students on any other device cannot open the quiz at all, so switch it off for a class on mixed devices.</p>

<p><strong>Grade importing:</strong> switch it on when you create the assignment. After the due date, open the assignment, click <strong>Import grades</strong> and the Forms scores land in the Classroom gradebook. The two sets of grades stay separate, so regrade in Forms and import again to update them.</p>

<p>Without Classroom, the link route works for any student with the URL. Collect email addresses does the identifying, and a shortened link in the class chat does the handing out. <a href="/blog/how-to-share-google-form/">Sharing a Google Form</a> covers email, embedding and the other routes.</p>

<p>The type decides whether Google Forms can grade the answer and what the Summary tab shows. Pick it for the marking, not for how the question reads.</p>

<h2 id="results">How do you read what students answered?</h2>

<p>The Responses tab has three views. <strong>Summary</strong> shows the frequently missed questions, each question's chart with the correct answer marked, and the average, median and range of scores. It is the view to open before the next lesson.</p>

<p><strong>Question</strong> view grades one question across the whole class: mark a group of answers correct, give partial points, or attach feedback once for everyone who wrote the same thing. <strong>Individual</strong> view is per student, with a points box and a feedback field under each answer.</p>

<p>If grades were set to release later, the Individual view has a Release score button at the top of each response. Tick the students and click Send emails and release. <strong>Link to Sheets</strong> puts every response in a spreadsheet, which is the route into the <a href="/blog/how-to-turn-google-forms-into-student-assessment-dashboard/">assessment dashboard</a>.</p>

<h2 id="limits">What Google Forms cannot do for students</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>No timer:</strong> there is no countdown or time limit on a form. A close date ends the quiz for everyone at once. Per-student time needs an <a href="/blog/how-to-add-a-timer-to-your-google-quiz-includes-latest-alternative-works-without-add-ons-2023/">add-on or another tool</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Locked mode is Chromebook-only:</strong> it needs a school-managed Chromebook and a Classroom assignment. On a phone, a laptop or a personal Chromebook, the quiz opens like any web page next to every other tab.</li>
<li><strong>One response needs a sign-in:</strong> Limit to 1 response only works for students signed in to Google, which rules it out for a class answering from a shared device or a parent without an account.</li>
<li><strong>No question bank:</strong> every student gets the same questions. Shuffle question order in Presentation settings and Shuffle option order on a question are the only randomisation.</li>
<li><strong>File uploads use your Drive:</strong> every file a student uploads is stored in the form owner's Drive and needs the student to sign in, so a hand-in form cannot be anonymous.</li>
<li><strong>No partial credit in the answer key:</strong> a Checkboxes question is right only when every correct option is ticked and nothing else. Partial points are given by hand in the Question view.</li>
</ul>
