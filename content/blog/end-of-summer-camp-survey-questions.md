<p style="font-size: inherit;">Camp ends, the buses pull out, and the only way to know what actually worked is to ask. A short end-of-summer survey tells you which activities campers loved, where staff needed backup, and why families will (or won't) sign up again next year.</p>

<p>Retention is the whole game. <a href="https://www.acacamps.org/sites/default/files/resource_library/research/2021-Camp-Counts-Survey-Report.pdf">ACA data</a> puts average camper retention near 38% for day camps and 20% for overnight camps, so every returning family counts. Feedback is how you earn the next season.</p>

<p>Below are 30+ summer camp survey questions, grouped by who you are asking (campers, parents, staff) and when to send them. Copy the ones you need, or load the free template at the end and start collecting responses in minutes.</p>

<h2>Why Run a Summer Camp Survey</h2>

<p>A good survey does more than collect compliments. It tells you:</p>

<ul>
<li>What campers actually enjoyed, and what fell flat.</li>
<li>Which activities to keep, cut, or add next year.</li>
<li>Where staff or safety gaps slipped past you mid-season.</li>
<li>Which parent quotes you can use in next year's marketing.</li>
<li>Whether families trust you enough to rebook.</li>
</ul>

<p>Run it while camp is fresh in everyone's mind. The longer you wait, the fuzzier the answers.</p>

<h2>How to Write Camp Survey Questions That Get Answered</h2>

<ul>
<li>Keep it under 15 questions. Surveys under three minutes finish at <a href="https://lensym.com/blog/how-long-should-survey-be">85 to 95%</a>; long ones get abandoned.</li>
<li>Mix formats: rating scales for a quick read, open text for the gold.</li>
<li>Ask one thing per question. "Were the food and activities good?" hides two answers.</li>
<li>Match the language to the reader. Campers get plain words, parents get specifics.</li>
<li>Test it yourself first. If it takes you four minutes, it takes a 10-year-old eight.</li>
<li>Leave one open box for anything you forgot to ask.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Summer Camp Survey Questions for Campers</h2>

<p>Ask campers these at pickup or in a quick follow-up email. Keep it light and fast.</p>

<ul>
<li>How would you rate your overall time at camp? (1 to 5)</li>
<li>What was your favorite activity?</li>
<li>What is the one thing you would change for next year?</li>
<li>Did you feel safe and comfortable? (yes / mostly / no)</li>
<li>How helpful were the counselors? (1 to 5)</li>
<li>Did you make new friends? (yes / no)</li>
<li>What new skill did you pick up?</li>
<li>How was the food? (1 to 5)</li>
<li>What new activity or theme do you want next summer?</li>
<li>Would you come back? Why or why not?</li>
</ul>

<h2>Summer Camp Survey Questions for Parents</h2>

<p>Parents see what campers don't: the booking experience, communication, and value. Email this a few days after camp ends.</p>

<ul>
<li>How satisfied were you with your child's experience? (1 to 5)</li>
<li>How well did we communicate before and during camp? (1 to 5)</li>
<li>Did registration and payment go smoothly?</li>
<li>Did your child come home with positive things to say?</li>
<li>How did our staff handle your questions or concerns?</li>
<li>Was camp good value for the price? (1 to 5)</li>
<li>How likely are you to recommend us to other families? (0 to 10)</li>
<li>How likely are you to rebook next year? (1 to 5)</li>
<li>What would make you choose us again?</li>
<li>Anything we missed or could do better?</li>
</ul>

<h2>Summer Camp Survey Questions for Staff and Counselors</h2>

<p>Your staff ran the program hour by hour. They spot what's broken before parents do. Send this in the final week.</p>

<ul>
<li>How supported did you feel by camp leadership? (1 to 5)</li>
<li>Was your training enough for the job? (1 to 5)</li>
<li>Which activities or schedules need fixing?</li>
<li>Did you have the supplies and space you needed?</li>
<li>How clear were safety procedures and the chain of command?</li>
<li>What slowed you down most?</li>
<li>Would you work here again next summer? (yes / no)</li>
<li>What would make this a better place to work?</li>
<li>Any camper or safety concerns we should know about?</li>
<li>One idea that would improve next season?</li>
</ul>

<h2>When to Send Each Camp Survey</h2>

<p>Timing changes the answers you get.</p>

<ul>
<li>Pre-camp (parents): a week before camp, confirm expectations and any health or access needs.</li>
<li>Mid-session (campers, staff): a quick three-question pulse to fix problems while there is still time.</li>
<li>End-of-summer (everyone): the full survey within 48 hours of the last day, while memories are sharp.</li>
</ul>

<p>Send the end-of-summer survey too late and response rates fall off a cliff.</p>

<h2>What to Do With the Feedback</h2>

<p>Collecting responses is the easy part. Acting on them is what fills next year's roster.</p>

<ul>
<li>Sort by theme. Group comments into activities, staff, food, safety, and value.</li>
<li>Fix the loud problems first. If 30% of campers flag the same activity, that is your summer-one project.</li>
<li>Share wins with staff. Positive feedback keeps good counselors coming back.</li>
<li>Pull quotes for marketing. A parent's words sell better than any brochure line.</li>
<li>Close the loop. Email families one change you are making based on their input. It is the cheapest retention move you have.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Common Camp Survey Mistakes to Avoid</h2>

<ul>
<li>Too many questions. Past 15, people bail or click through at random.</li>
<li>Double-barreled questions. "Was the food and lodging good?" gives one answer for two things.</li>
<li>Leading questions. "How amazing were the counselors?" invites flattery, not truth.</li>
<li>No open box. Scales tell you what; open text tells you why.</li>
<li>Surveying campers only. Parents and staff see problems campers never notice.</li>
<li>Waiting two weeks. Memories fade and so do response rates.</li>
</ul>
