<p class="fmstr-blog-lead">Translation: the layout of your sign-up form is doing more for revenue than your hero animation. Below are the 12 form and website design trends defining 2026, with the brands using them well, the ones overdoing it, and what to copy into your own builds this quarter.</p>

<p class="fmstr-blog-lead">Form trends sit alongside website trends on purpose: a 2026 landing page that opens with a neo-brutalist hero and closes with a 2014 contact form will lose the lead at the last screen.</p>

<h2 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__h2" id="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps-h2">The 12 Website and Form Design Trends Shaping 2026</h2>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__intro">For each trend: who is doing it well, what to copy, what to skip, and where it fits.</p>
</div>
<ol class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__list">

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">1</span>AI-Personalized Forms and Pages</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Personalization in 2026 is not a "Hi, [First Name]" merge tag. It is the form rewriting itself based on who shows up. A B2B sign-up sees a hubspot.com email and skips the company-size question. A property site sees a London IP and replaces "ZIP code" with "postcode".</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">A pricing form reads UTM=enterprise and surfaces the SOC 2 section before the credit card. None of this needs custom code in 2026; conditional logic and prefill have moved from "advanced" to default.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__brands"><strong>Brands doing it well:</strong> Spotify (Discover Weekly), ASOS (auth-state-aware upsell), Notion (template gallery reorders by detected role).</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__triad">
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--copy">Copy</span><span>Hide every field that can be inferred, prefilled, or asked later. Build the path with <a href="https://formester.com/features/conditional-logic/">conditional logic</a> and pair it with <a href="https://formester.com/features/pre-fill-fields/">prefill from external data</a> so returning users do not re-type.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--skip">Skip</span><span>Surprise personalization. If the form prefills information the user has not given you yet, you have a privacy problem, not a UX win.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--best">Best for</span><span>SaaS sign-ups, B2B demo requests, multi-segment landing pages.</span></li>
</ul>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">2</span>Low-Light Mode as the New Default</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Dark mode is no longer a toggle in the footer. In 2026, the trend has shifted to a low-light default: muted greys, soft charcoals, dimmed highlights, lower contrast than pure dark mode. Easier on the eyes, moodier on the brand.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__brands"><strong>Brands doing it well:</strong> Silver Drive, Balenciaga, Bequant, <a href="https://linear.app/">Linear's marketing site</a>.</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__triad">
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--copy">Copy</span><span>Design the form for both light and low-light at the same time. Inputs need a visible border in low-light; relying on background contrast alone breaks accessibility. Test field focus states against WCAG 4.5:1 minimum in both modes.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--skip">Skip</span><span>Pure-black backgrounds with #FFFFFF text. The retina-burn era is over.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--best">Best for</span><span>Design-led brands, fintech, music and lifestyle products.</span></li>
</ul>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">3</span>Oversized, Expressive Typography</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Heros are still big in 2026, just less obedient. Variable fonts, oversized headlines, kinetic letter animations, and unconventional grid placements are now standard for design-forward brands. <a href="https://www.figma.com/resource-library/web-design-trends/">Figma's 2026 trend report</a> calls out bold typography as one of the load-bearing trends of the year.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__brands"><strong>Brands doing it well:</strong> CHRLS.DSGN, Chainzoku, Pact Media, the typographic experiments across <a href="https://www.awwwards.com/websites/typography/">Awwwards SOTD</a>.</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__triad">
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--copy">Copy</span><span>Pair an expressive display font in the hero with a quiet, neutral body font. Forms keep the neutral body font; do not put a 96px display weight on a field label.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--skip">Skip</span><span>Putting a kinetic animated headline above a form. The animation steals focus that the form needs.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--best">Best for</span><span>Agency sites, portfolios, brand-led landing pages.</span></li>
</ul>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">4</span>Bento Grid Layouts</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">The bento grid, popularized by Apple's product pages and now copied across every SaaS comparison page on the web, is the dominant feature-layout pattern of 2026. Modular cards, varied sizes, clear hierarchy without losing density.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__brands"><strong>Brands doing it well:</strong> Literal, Iconwerk, Procreate, Halo Branding, <a href="https://linear.app/changelog">Linear's changelog page</a>.</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__triad">
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--copy">Copy</span><span>Use the bento for "what you get" feature sections; pair big cards with hover micro-interactions instead of static images. Keep the longest card under three lines of copy.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--skip">Skip</span><span>Putting a form inside one bento card. Forms need vertical space; jammed into a card they collapse.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--best">Best for</span><span>SaaS feature pages, product launches, comparison pages.</span></li>
</ul>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">5</span>Experimental Navigation and Guided Scrolling</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Two threads collided in 2026. One is experimental navigation: parallax, horizontal scroll, interactive maps, hidden drawers, radial menus.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">The other is guided scrolling, what <a href="https://webflow.com/blog/web-design-trends-2026">Webflow's 2026 trend report</a> calls "turning scroll into a navigation tool" with progress indicators, sticky chapters, and visual wayfinding. The combination wins. Experimental gives novelty; guided scrolling stops users from getting lost in it.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__brands"><strong>Brands doing it well:</strong> Pixlspace, Gapsy Studio, Stripe's annual updates page (sticky chapter index).</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__triad">
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--copy">Copy</span><span>If you build an unusual scroll, build a progress indicator in the same sprint. Forms get the same treatment: long forms need a step counter at the top, not just a "Next" button.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--skip">Skip</span><span>Experimental navigation as the only way to find your pricing page. Always keep a standard nav available.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--best">Best for</span><span>Agency sites, product showcases, long-scroll storytelling pages.</span></li>
</ul>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">6</span>Conversational Forms</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Conversational forms swap labels and validation errors for human language.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">"Where to?" instead of "Enter location." "Whoops, that doesn't seem right" instead of "Invalid input." The numbers matter here. <a href="https://formflux.io/blog/form-completion-rates-guide">Tools with conversational, one-question-per-screen modes see 15-25% higher completion rates than traditional layouts</a>, and <a href="https://surveysparrow.com/blog/how-can-conversational-forms-increase-survey-response-rates/">some studies report up to 40% uplift on longer lead-gen flows</a>.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__brands"><strong>Brands doing it well:</strong> ASOS, Mailchimp (brand voice carried into validation copy), Calm (encouragement framing).</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__triad">
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--copy">Copy</span><span>Rewrite every field label and every error message in the brand voice. Treat form copy as marketing copy, not engineering copy. Build it with <a href="https://formester.com/blog/how-to-create-conversational-forms-for-your-website/">Formester's conversational form patterns</a> for one-question-per-screen out of the box.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--skip">Skip</span><span>Conversational tone on regulated forms. A medical intake form using "Whoops!" is the wrong register.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--best">Best for</span><span>Lead capture, surveys, onboarding flows, anything emotional or qualitative.</span></li>
</ul>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">7</span>Autofill, Prefill, and AI Suggestions</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Typing is the friction. Every input field is a chance to lose the user. In 2026, "do not make them type" is design law.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Three layers are now standard: browser autofill (HTML autocomplete attributes wired correctly), server-side prefill (URL parameters or session data), and AI suggestions that learn from the user's previous answers and propose the next one, the way Gmail proposes a reply.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__brands"><strong>Brands doing it well:</strong> Google's address autocomplete, HubSpot's progressive profiling, <a href="https://stripe.com/link">Stripe Checkout's Link autofill</a>.</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__triad">
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--copy">Copy</span><span>Audit every form for autocomplete attributes. Add <a href="https://formester.com/features/auto-fill-forms-with-external-data/">Formester's prefill from external data</a> for any logged-in or returning-user flow. The 30 seconds saved per user compounds into measurable conversion.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--skip">Skip</span><span>Blind AI suggestions on sensitive fields (medical history, financial details). Suggest in a non-binding way or not at all.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--best">Best for</span><span>Checkout, address entry, repeat-customer flows, B2B sign-ups.</span></li>
</ul>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">8</span>Micro-Interactions and Inline Feedback</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">A 200ms checkmark when the email validates. A subtle shake when the password is wrong. A bar that fills as the user moves through a multi-step form. None of these are decoration; each one is a status update the user reads without thinking.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__brands"><strong>Brands doing it well:</strong> Headspace, Remembear, Stripe's inline card-validation animations, <a href="https://1password.com/">1Password's strength meter</a>.</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__triad">
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--copy">Copy</span><span>Validate inline, not on submit. The user types their email, the field shows a tick or a hint the moment they leave it. Add a progress bar to every form over 3 steps; <a href="https://voiceforms.anvevoice.app/blog/multi-step-form-best-practices/">progress indicators on multi-step forms reduce abandonment by 20-25%</a>.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--skip">Skip</span><span>Long animations that block the next action. A celebration GIF on form submit is fine; a 4-second loading animation is not.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--best">Best for</span><span>Multi-step forms, sign-ups, checkout, anywhere users need a sense of progress.</span></li>
</ul>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">9</span>Accessibility-First, Not Accessibility-Added</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Accessibility used to be the audit you ran two weeks before launch.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">In 2026 it is the constraint you design against from the first wireframe, partly because the <a href="https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-a-european-green-deal/file-european-accessibility-act">European Accessibility Act enforcement window opened on 28 June 2025</a> and dragged most B2B SaaS into WCAG 2.1 AA compliance by default.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">What this looks like on a form: contrast ratios above 4.5:1 for all input borders and label text, visible focus states (not just outline:none), labels associated with inputs via for=, error messages that announce themselves to screen readers, and keyboard navigation that works without a mouse.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__brands"><strong>Brands doing it well:</strong> Scope, RNID, GOV.UK (the canonical accessible form system).</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__triad">
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--copy">Copy</span><span>Every form ships with a screen-reader pass. If the form cannot be completed with keyboard only, it is broken, not "not yet ready."</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--skip">Skip</span><span>Accessibility overlays. The widgets that promise "WCAG compliance in 1 line of JavaScript" are largely <a href="https://overlayfactsheet.com/">discredited</a> and increase legal risk.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--best">Best for</span><span>Every form. Non-negotiable in 2026.</span></li>
</ul>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">10</span>Visible Trust Signals on Every Form</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Forms ask for personal data. Users are warier than they were in 2020. The lift comes from making the security visible, not just real.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">What goes on a high-trust form: a one-line privacy note next to the email field, a padlock icon near payment inputs, a "we will never sell your data" link to a real policy, <a href="https://formester.com/features/gdpr-compliant-forms/">GDPR-compliant data handling</a> for any EU user, and a clear sender identity if you are emailing them after submission.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__brands"><strong>Brands doing it well:</strong> PayPal (badge stack on checkout), Stripe (compliance badges at every payment point), GOV.UK ("we collect this to..." inline copy).</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__triad">
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--copy">Copy</span><span>Tell users what each field is for in a sentence under it. The phone number on a contact form should say "we use this only to schedule the demo, not for marketing."</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--skip">Skip</span><span>Paranoia-inducing copy. "Your data is 100% safe!" reads as "we just got breached."</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--best">Best for</span><span>Payment forms, lead capture, anything collecting an email.</span></li>
</ul>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">11</span>Neo-Brutalism in Forms and Dashboards</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Hard borders. Flat colors. No shadows. Sans-serifs with too much weight. Neo-brutalism started on agency portfolios in 2023 and made it to mainstream SaaS in 2026: Gumroad, Vercel's marketing pages, and a growing list of B2B tools have adopted some flavor of it.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">The look reads as confidence. "We do not need to soften ourselves with gradients." It also performs well in dense interfaces because the high contrast carries information cheaply.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__brands"><strong>Brands doing it well:</strong> <a href="https://gumroad.com/">Gumroad</a>, Vercel, Figma's developer-mode pages, <a href="https://www.notion.so/product/calendar">Notion Calendar's launch site</a>.</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__triad">
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--copy">Copy</span><span>Use neo-brutalism on the elements that need attention (primary CTAs, error states, plan-selector cards). Forms can adopt the language with thick input borders and zero-radius buttons.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--skip">Skip</span><span>Long-form reading surfaces. A 2,000-word landing page in pure neo-brutalism is exhausting.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--best">Best for</span><span>Developer tools, design-led SaaS, agency sites, plan selectors.</span></li>
</ul>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">12</span>Single-Question-Per-Screen and Voice Input</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">The most consequential form pattern of 2026 is also the simplest. Show one question. Wait for the answer. Show the next one. <a href="https://voiceforms.anvevoice.app/blog/multi-step-form-best-practices/">Multi-step forms achieve 86% higher completion rates than equivalent single-page forms</a> when the steps are clean and the progress is visible.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">On mobile, the single-question pattern is close to mandatory: 30-field forms on a 5-inch screen lose 60-80% of submissions before the user reaches the second screen. Voice input is the natural extension.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Phones already have it; long-form fields ("describe your project") convert better when the user can talk instead of thumb-type.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__brands"><strong>Brands doing it well:</strong> Typeform (the canonical single-question form), <a href="https://pets.byspotify.com/">Spotify Pets</a>, Headspace onboarding.</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__triad">
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--copy">Copy</span><span>Any form over 8 fields should be multi-step with one question per screen and a progress bar. Long descriptive fields get a "tap to speak" affordance on mobile.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--skip">Skip</span><span>Turning a 3-field contact form into a 3-screen wizard. The pattern is for length, not branding.</span></li>
<li><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tag--best">Best for</span><span>Lead generation, surveys, onboarding, anything with more than 8 fields.</span></li>
</ul>
</li>

</ol>
</div>

<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__container">
<h2 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__h2" id="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq-h2">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__intro">The questions teams actually ask when planning a 2026 form or website refresh.</p>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__list">

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>What is the biggest form design trend of 2026?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">AI-personalized forms. In 2026, the form rewrites itself based on who shows up: detected email domain, UTM source, returning-user status, geolocation. The form a logged-in enterprise admin sees is materially different from the form a cold visitor sees, and conditional logic builders make this a config change, not an engineering project.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>Are conversational forms still worth using in 2026?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Yes. Conversational, one-question-per-screen forms see 15-25% higher completion rates than traditional layouts, and up to 40% uplift on longer flows. The pattern stopped being a novelty around 2022 and is now the default for any form over 8 fields.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>Is dark mode still a 2026 trend?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Dark mode is now a baseline expectation, not a trend. The 2026 evolution is "low-light" mode: muted greys, dimmed highlights, lower contrast than pure dark. Easier on the eyes for long sessions, and a richer brand canvas than pure black.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>How does AI change form design in 2026?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Three ways. AI prefills more fields than browser autofill can. AI generates the form from a prompt for non-designers. AI personalizes the question order and field set per user. The biggest practical shift is that designers no longer have to draft every form; they curate AI-generated drafts.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>What is the best form length in 2026?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">As short as the data you need. The average ecommerce checkout has 23 fields when the optimum is 12-14, so most teams can cut a third of their form fields and lose nothing. For lead capture, 3-5 fields outperform every other length on initial submission; ask the rest progressively after the user is in.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>Should every form be accessible by default in 2026?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Yes. The European Accessibility Act enforcement window opened in mid-2025 and most B2B SaaS now ship WCAG 2.1 AA by default. Beyond compliance, accessible forms have higher completion rates because keyboard navigation, visible focus states, and clear error messages help every user, not only assistive-tech users.</div>
</details>
