<h2 class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-body__h2" id="why-run-survey">Why run a social media survey</h2>
<p>Four things you can't get from analytics alone:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Intent behind the scroll.</strong> Analytics tells you a Reel got 12,000 views. A survey tells you 38% saved it for later because it taught them something, 22% sent it to a friend as a joke. Those are two different content engines.</li>
<li><strong>Platform drift.</strong> <a href="https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/">Pew Research's 2025 social media fact sheet</a> shows TikTok use among US adults climbed to 33% while Facebook held flat at 68%. Your audience may have moved without telling you.</li>
<li><strong>Ad fatigue thresholds.</strong> Most brands run the same creative until CTR halves. A survey tells you the cutoff before that happens.</li>
<li><strong>Buying triggers.</strong> "Did you ever buy something because of a social post" has a yes-rate you can benchmark. The follow-up, <em>what kind of post</em>, is the real data.</li>
</ul>
<p>Run one quarterly. Twenty respondents is enough to spot a pattern. Two hundred is enough to act on it.</p>

<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__head">
<h2 class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__h2" id="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method-h2">How to design a survey that actually gets responses</h2>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__intro">The five steps that separate a 4% response rate from a 22% one.</p>
</div>

<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__list">

<article class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__step">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__h3"><span class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__num" aria-hidden="true">1</span><span>Pick one objective per survey</span></h3>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__body">
<p class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__p">&ldquo;Understand my audience&rdquo; is too broad to design around. &ldquo;Decide whether to launch a YouTube channel this quarter&rdquo; is one good survey. So is &ldquo;find out why our Instagram engagement dropped 30%.&rdquo; If you have two goals, run two short surveys, not one long one.</p>
</div>
</article>

<article class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__step">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__h3"><span class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__num" aria-hidden="true">2</span><span>Pick the audience before the questions</span></h3>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__body">
<p class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__p">A survey aimed at your own followers reads differently from one going to a cold segment. Followers will answer 20 questions. Cold audiences will quit at 8. Researchers studying social media effects need Likert scales and a consent line; HR teams running a policy pulse need anonymity. Decide who is filling this out before you draft a single question.</p>
</div>
</article>

<article class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__step">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__h3"><span class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__num" aria-hidden="true">3</span><span>Match the question mix to the question</span></h3>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__body">
<p class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__p">Categorical (multiple choice) for fast, easy questions. Likert scales for attitudes and frequencies. One or two open-ended boxes where the <em>why</em> matters more than the <em>what</em>. The single biggest fatigue trigger is back-to-back open-text prompts. Keep them to two, max.</p>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__callout">Rule of thumb: 70% multiple choice, 20% Likert, 10% free text. Adjust for researcher use-cases.</div>
</div>
</article>

<article class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__step">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__h3"><span class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__num" aria-hidden="true">4</span><span>Cap it at 10 questions for cold audiences, 20 for your own list</span></h3>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__body">
<p class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__p">Past 10 questions, completion rates start dropping in the 5-15% range for cold audiences. Your own followers will go to 20 because they already trust the brand. Past 20, every audience drops off. Tell respondents the time upfront: &ldquo;Takes 90 seconds&rdquo; outperforms &ldquo;Quick survey&rdquo; on start-rate.</p>
</div>
</article>

<article class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__step">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__h3"><span class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__num" aria-hidden="true">5</span><span>Distribute where the audience already is, then close the loop</span></h3>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__body">
<p class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-method__p">Email list gets the highest completion rate. Story link sticker is second. Bio link is third. DM to engaged followers is fourth (highest response quality, lowest reach). Paid promotion only if you need a representative sample for research. Then post one screenshot, chart, or quote from the results within two weeks. Response rates on your <em>next</em> survey double when people know their answers shaped a decision.</p>
</div>
</article>

<h2 class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-body__h2">Tips for higher response rates</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Keep it short.</strong> 5-10 questions for a cold audience, up to 20 for your own followers. Past that, completion drops off a cliff.</li>
<li><strong>Lead with the easiest question.</strong> Multiple choice or a single-tap rating. The hardest free-text question goes last.</li>
<li><strong>Promise something real.</strong> A discount works. A summary of the results works better, people are more curious about what their peers think than they admit.</li>
<li><strong>Distribute on three channels, not one.</strong> Email + story sticker + bio link beats any single channel by 30-40%.</li>
<li><strong>Mention the time it takes.</strong> "Takes 90 seconds" in the headline lifts start-rate. Be honest; if it's 3 minutes, say 3.</li>
<li><strong>Close the loop in public.</strong> Post the top insight within two weeks. Builds trust for the next survey.</li>
</ul>
<p>See <a href="https://formester.com/blog/5-things-you-can-do-with-survey-forms/">5 things you can do with survey forms</a> for the playbook on what to actually do with the responses.</p>

<h2 class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__h2" id="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq-h2">Social media survey FAQ</h2>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__intro">Answers that mirror the FAQPage JSON-LD on the live page.</p>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__list">

<details class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__item">
<summary>How many questions should a social media survey have?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__answer">5 to 10 for a cold audience, 10 to 20 for your own followers. Past 20, drop-off accelerates. The <a href="https://formester.com/templates/social-media-survey-form-2114/">free template</a> ships with 50 questions because they&rsquo;re optional. Cut what doesn&rsquo;t apply.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__item">
<summary>What are the best questions to ask in a social media usage survey?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__answer">For usage studies (versus marketing studies), lead with platform mix, daily time spent, time-of-day patterns, and primary device. Then layer in content-type preferences and one effects question (mood change after use). The &ldquo;Platform and usage&rdquo; and &ldquo;Social media effects&rdquo; blocks above cover the core 15.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__item">
<summary>What questions do students or researchers studying social media effects usually ask?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__answer">Likert-scale items covering mood after use, social comparison, sleep impact, attention or scroll loss, sense of connection, and exposure to harmful content. The 8-question block in &ldquo;Social media effects&rdquo; above is the standard structure. Pair it with demographics and a free-text &ldquo;what would you change&rdquo; closer. <a href="https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/">Pew Research&rsquo;s 2025 social media fact sheet</a> is a solid benchmark to cite in the write-up.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__item">
<summary>How long should a social media survey take to complete?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__answer">Aim for under 3 minutes. Test it on one team member first. If it takes you more than 4 minutes, cut questions. Tell respondents the time upfront. &ldquo;Takes 90 seconds&rdquo; outperforms &ldquo;Quick survey&rdquo; on start-rate.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__item">
<summary>What&rsquo;s the best free tool to create a social media survey?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__answer">For a one-off, Google Forms works. For branded, shareable, and analytics-tracked, use <a href="https://formester.com/templates/social-media-survey-form-2114/">Formester&rsquo;s free template</a>. For a faster start, the <a href="https://formester.com/ai-survey-generator/">AI Survey Generator</a> drafts 10 questions from a one-line brief.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__item">
<summary>How do I get a higher response rate?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-smsq-faq__answer">Short survey, three distribution channels (email plus story sticker plus bio link), state the time upfront, and post one result publicly within two weeks. Most marketers see 8 to 15% on cold audiences and 25 to 40% on warm. If you&rsquo;re under 5%, the survey is too long or the ask is unclear. For more ideas, see <a href="https://formester.com/blog/5-things-you-can-do-with-survey-forms/">5 things you can do with survey forms</a>.</div>
