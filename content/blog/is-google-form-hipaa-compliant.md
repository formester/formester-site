![Cover art for a guide to whether Google Forms is HIPAA compliant](https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/a120e5652528d3e1_is-google-form-hipaa-compliant-cover.jpg)

<p style="font-size: inherit;">Yes, with conditions that most answers to this question leave out. Google Forms sits inside Google Drive on Google's own list of functionality covered by its HIPAA Business Associate Addendum, so the tool itself is not the obstacle. What decides whether your form is compliant is the account it lives on, whether an administrator has signed the BAA, and what you have installed on it.</p>

<div class="tldr">
<p><strong>Key facts</strong></p>
<p>Google Forms is covered by the BAA as part of Google Drive. That coverage applies only on a Google Workspace account where an admin has accepted the Business Associate Addendum. Free and personal Google accounts have no BAA path at all, and any add-on you install on the form falls outside the agreement.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Google Forms is Included Functionality.</strong> Google lists it inside Google Drive, alongside Docs, Sheets, Slides and Vids (<a href="https://workspace.google.com/terms/2015/1/hipaa_functionality.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google Workspace</a>)</li>
<li><strong>A signed BAA is mandatory.</strong> Google states that customers who have not signed one must not use PHI in Workspace services (<a href="https://support.google.com/a/answer/3407054" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google Workspace Admin Help</a>)</li>
<li><strong>Add-ons are excluded.</strong> Third-party applications including add-ons are not covered by the BAA (<a href="https://support.google.com/a/answer/3407054" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google Workspace Admin Help</a>)</li>
<li><strong>Free Google accounts: no BAA.</strong> A personal account cannot be brought into scope by any setting</li>
<li><strong>The BAA covers Google's side only.</strong> Sharing, retention and staff access remain yours to control</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 id="covered">Is Google Forms actually covered by Google's BAA?</h2>

<p>It is, and this is the part most articles on this question get wrong. Google publishes a list of what its HIPAA Business Associate Addendum covers, and Google Drive appears on it with its editors named explicitly: Docs, Forms, Sheets, Slides and Vids. Forms is not a grey area or an oversight. It is written into the list.</p>
<p>The confusion comes from conflating two different things. "Is Google Forms HIPAA compliant" reads like a question about the product, but HIPAA compliance is never a property of a tool on its own. It is a property of how an organisation uses that tool under an agreement. Google can offer the covered service; whether your particular form is compliant depends on the four conditions below.</p>

<h2 id="conditions">What has to be true for a Google Form to be compliant?</h2>

<div class="table-wrap">
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Condition</th><th>What it means</th><th>If it is missing</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>A Workspace account</td><td>Free and personal Google accounts have no BAA path</td><td>No coverage, at any settings</td></tr>
<tr><td>A signed BAA</td><td>An admin reviews and accepts it in the Admin console</td><td>Google says PHI must not be used</td></tr>
<tr><td>No add-ons on the form</td><td>Third-party apps sit outside Included Functionality</td><td>That data leaves the agreement</td></tr>
<tr><td>Controlled access</td><td>Responses and the linked Sheet shared with named people</td><td>Coverage intact, your safeguards are not</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table></div>

<p>The fourth row is the one that catches organisations that did everything else right. A signed BAA does not stop a linked Google Sheet being shared with anyone who has the link, and Google's implementation guide is direct about limiting access to PHI within a domain rather than relying on the agreement to do it for you.</p>

<h2 id="addons">Why do add-ons break HIPAA coverage?</h2>

<p>Because the BAA covers Google's own functionality, and an add-on is somebody else's software reading your responses. Google's documentation states it plainly: third-party applications including add-ons are not included in the Included Functionality covered by the BAA.</p>
<p>This matters more than it sounds, because add-ons are exactly how teams paper over the gaps in Google Forms. A form limiter, a PDF generator, a notification tool, a signature add-on: each one is a third party now handling protected health information under its own terms rather than Google's. If a form touches PHI, it should have nothing installed on it, which also removes most of the reasons people reach for Google Forms in a clinical setting.</p>

<h2 id="settings">Which Google Forms settings still matter?</h2>

<p>Coverage is an account-level question, but a few form-level settings decide how much identifying data you collect in the first place. Collecting less is the simplest safeguard available.</p>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/46ee6f6816ea9e6c_anon-01-do-not-collect.jpg" alt="Google Forms Settings with Collect email addresses set to Do not collect, marked with a red arrow" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:790px;height:auto;border-radius:10px;border:1px solid #d8dce3;box-shadow:0 1px 3px rgba(16,24,40,0.08);margin:18px 0;" /></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Collect email addresses.</strong> Set to Do not collect unless you genuinely need to know who submitted. An email address attached to a health question is itself identifying.</li>
<li><strong>Limit to 1 response.</strong> This forces a Google sign-in, which ties every submission to an account. Useful for controlling duplicates, the opposite of minimising data.</li>
<li><strong>The linked Google Sheet.</strong> It inherits none of your carefulness. Check who it is shared with, because it is usually the least locked-down copy of the data.</li>
<li><strong>Response receipts.</strong> Sending a copy of the answers by email puts PHI into an inbox you do not control.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="alternatives">When is Google Forms the wrong tool for PHI?</h2>

<p>When the administrative overhead outweighs what you get. Running a compliant Google Form means holding a Workspace account, keeping the BAA current, resisting every add-on that would make the form better, and auditing sharing on both the form and its Sheet. Practices that do this successfully tend to be ones already standardised on Workspace.</p>
<p>If you are not, a purpose-built intake tool that signs a BAA and handles the safeguards natively is usually less work than administering Google Forms carefully enough to be safe.</p>
