<p style="font-size: inherit;">Google Forms keeps your responses in five separate places. Most guides list them and stop there, which is why people still end up in the wrong tab. Each one answers a different question, so this covers what each one shows and the point where it runs out.</p>

<blockquote><p>Open your form and click <strong>Responses</strong>. Three tabs sit there: <strong>Summary</strong> for charts, <strong>Question</strong> for one question at a time, and <strong>Individual</strong> for one person at a time. Two more routes live outside those tabs: the <strong>linked Google Sheet</strong> for raw rows, and <strong>Download responses (.csv)</strong> for a file you can hand to something else.</p></blockquote>

<h2>Which of the five you actually want</h2>

<p>Pick the row that matches the question you are trying to answer, then jump to it.</p>

<h2 id="walkthrough">All five, in one minute</h2>

<p>Summary, Question, Individual, the linked Sheet, then CSV. The detail on each one follows.</p>

<h2 id="summary-tab">1. Summary tab</h2>

<p>The default view when you open Responses. Google builds a chart per question: bar charts for multiple choice, a list for short answers, an average for linear scales.</p>
<p><img src="/blog/google-forms-responses/01-summary-tab.jpg" alt="Google Forms Summary tab showing a bar chart of satisfaction responses with the Link to Sheets button above it" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:791px;height:auto;border-radius:10px;border:1px solid #d8dce3;box-shadow:0 1px 3px rgba(16,24,40,0.08);margin:18px 0;" /></p>
<p><strong>How to get there.</strong> Open your form, click <strong>Responses</strong>, then <strong>Summary</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Where it stops.</strong> There is no filter. You cannot look at only the responses from one team, one date range or one answer choice, so a survey with two distinct audiences reads as one blurred average.</p>

<h2 id="question-tab">2. Question tab</h2>

<p>Shows a single question with every answer given to it, and a pair of arrows to step through the rest.</p>
<p><img src="/blog/google-forms-responses/02-question-tab.jpg" alt="Google Forms Question tab with a question picker showing 1 of 10 and the answers to a single question" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:791px;height:auto;border-radius:10px;border:1px solid #d8dce3;box-shadow:0 1px 3px rgba(16,24,40,0.08);margin:18px 0;" /></p>
<p><strong>How to get there.</strong> Click <strong>Responses</strong>, then <strong>Question</strong>, then use the arrows or the dropdown to pick a question.</p>
<p><strong>Where it stops.</strong> It isolates the question by design, so you cannot see who gave a particular answer without switching to the Individual tab and hunting.</p>

<h2 id="individual-tab">3. Individual tab</h2>

<p>One respondent's whole submission on screen, laid out like the form they filled in. This is the view to use before replying to someone.</p>
<p><img src="/blog/google-forms-responses/03-individual-tab.jpg" alt="Google Forms Individual tab showing response 1 of 3 with the print and delete icons above it" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:791px;height:auto;border-radius:10px;border:1px solid #d8dce3;box-shadow:0 1px 3px rgba(16,24,40,0.08);margin:18px 0;" /></p>
<p><strong>How to get there.</strong> Click <strong>Responses</strong>, then <strong>Individual</strong>, then page through with the arrows.</p>
<p><strong>Where it stops.</strong> Paging is the only navigation. With 300 submissions there is no search, so finding one person means clicking or going to the Sheet instead.</p>

<h2 id="linked-sheet">4. Linked Google Sheet</h2>

<p>Sends every submission into a Google Sheet, one row per response, updating as new ones arrive. This is where sorting, filtering and formulas become possible.</p>
<p><img src="/blog/google-forms-responses/05-linked-sheet.jpg" alt="The linked Google Sheet showing three form responses with a Timestamp column and one column per question" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:10px;border:1px solid #d8dce3;box-shadow:0 1px 3px rgba(16,24,40,0.08);margin:18px 0;" /></p>
<p><strong>How to get there.</strong> In the Responses tab, click the Sheets icon, then either create a new spreadsheet or pick an existing one.</p>
<p><strong>Where it stops.</strong> It is a second file to manage, with its own sharing settings. Anyone you give edit access to the Sheet can see every response, which catches people out on anonymous surveys.</p>

<h2 id="csv-download">5. CSV download</h2>

<p>A one-off file of every response, for a tool that will not read a Google Sheet.</p>
<p><img src="/blog/google-forms-responses/04-download-csv.jpg" alt="The Google Forms Responses three-dot menu open, showing Download responses as CSV, Print all responses and Delete all responses" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:791px;height:auto;border-radius:10px;border:1px solid #d8dce3;box-shadow:0 1px 3px rgba(16,24,40,0.08);margin:18px 0;" /></p>
<p><strong>How to get there.</strong> In the Responses tab, open the three-dot menu and choose <strong>Download responses (.csv)</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Where it stops.</strong> It is a snapshot. The file is out of date as soon as the next response arrives, so a weekly report means a weekly re-download.</p>

<h2 id="see-your-own-response">Can you see your own response after you submit?</h2>

<p>This is the question behind a lot of searches for Google Forms responses, and the answer is usually no. As a respondent you get your answers back only if the form owner set it up in advance, and there is no list of forms you have submitted anywhere in Google Forms.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>If the owner turned on response receipts.</strong> Google emails you a copy of your answers, but only when the form collects respondent emails and the owner enabled receipts in <strong>Settings</strong>. Search your inbox for the form name.</li>
<li><strong>If the owner allowed editing.</strong> That receipt email carries an <strong>Edit your response</strong> link, which reopens your submission with your answers still in it.</li>
<li><strong>If neither was switched on.</strong> There is no way to retrieve it yourself. Ask whoever owns the form to look you up in their Individual tab.</li>
</ul>

<p>If you are the one building forms and want people to keep a copy without depending on a setting they will never find, that is worth designing in from the start rather than bolting on afterwards.</p>

<h2 id="delete-a-response">How to delete a response</h2>

<p>Deleting is per response and it is permanent, so read the row before you click.</p>
<p><img src="/blog/google-forms-responses/06-delete-all-responses.jpg" alt="The Google Forms Responses three-dot menu with Delete all responses at the bottom" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:410px;height:auto;border-radius:10px;border:1px solid #d8dce3;box-shadow:0 1px 3px rgba(16,24,40,0.08);margin:18px 0;" /></p>
<ol>
<li>Click <strong>Responses</strong>, then <strong>Individual</strong>.</li>
<li>Page to the submission you want gone.</li>
<li>Click the bin icon, then confirm.</li>
</ol>
<p>To clear everything, use <strong>Delete all responses</strong> in the three-dot menu. Neither action touches rows already written to the linked Sheet, so a deleted response still sits in the spreadsheet until you remove it there too.</p>

<h2>Google Forms responses FAQ</h2>
<details><summary>How do I view responses in Google Forms?</summary><p>Open the form and click <strong>Responses</strong>. Use the Summary tab for charts, the Question tab to read every answer to one question, and the Individual tab to see one person's full submission.</p></details>
<details><summary>How do I see who responded to my Google Form?</summary><p>Only if the form collects respondent emails. Turn that on in Settings before you share the form, then each row in the Individual tab and the linked Sheet carries an email address. Without it, submissions are anonymous and cannot be attributed afterwards.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can I see my own response to a Google Form after submitting?</summary><p>Not on your own. You get a copy only if the owner enabled response receipts and the form collects emails, in which case Google emails your answers to you. If the owner also allowed editing, that email has an Edit your response link. Otherwise ask the form owner to look you up.</p></details>
<details><summary>Where can I find my Google Form responses?</summary><p>In the Responses tab of the form itself, and in the linked Google Sheet if you created one. There is no separate archive, so if you cannot open the form you cannot reach its responses.</p></details>
<details><summary>How do I export Google Forms responses to Excel?</summary><p>Open the Responses tab, use the three-dot menu and choose Download responses (.csv), then open the file in Excel. To keep it updating instead, link a Google Sheet and export from there.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can I view Google Forms responses on mobile?</summary><p>Yes, through a browser. Google Forms has no dedicated mobile app for viewing responses, so open forms.google.com in your phone browser. The Summary tab reads well on a small screen; the Individual tab is cramped.</p></details>
<details><summary>How do I filter Google Forms responses by date or category?</summary><p>Not inside Google Forms, which has no filtering at all. Link a Google Sheet and filter there with a formula or a pivot table, or use a builder with filtering in its own dashboard.</p></details>
<details><summary>How do I delete a response in Google Forms?</summary><p>Open Responses, then Individual, page to the submission and click the bin icon. Deletion is permanent and does not remove the matching row from a linked Sheet, so clear that separately.</p></details>
