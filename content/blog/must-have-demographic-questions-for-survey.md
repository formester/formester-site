<p class="fmstr-blog-lead">Demographic survey questions describe who answered your survey: age, gender, location, income, employment, education, ethnicity, language, disability, and household. They are how aggregate numbers turn into segments you can act on.</p>

<p class="fmstr-blog-lead">Get them wrong and people abandon the form or pick a random answer. Research by <a href="https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.133.5.859">Tourangeau and Yan in Psychological Bulletin</a> found that the more sensitive a question feels and the earlier it appears, the more it drags down both response rate and accuracy.</p>

<p class="fmstr-blog-lead">Below are 15 demographic survey questions with copy-ready answer options, inclusive 2026 phrasing, and a note on when to skip each one. Use them as a base and cut whatever does not directly serve your analysis.</p>

<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-tldr__list">
<li><strong>Most analyses need 3 to 5 demographic questions, not 15.</strong> Cut to what your analysis plan actually uses.</li>
<li><strong>Place them at the end.</strong> Exceptions: language and accessibility go first; screening questions go first.</li>
<li><strong>Always include "Prefer not to say".</strong> Forced answers on sensitive items either drop respondents or fill cells randomly.</li>

<section class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps" aria-labelledby="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps-h2">
<div class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__container">
<div class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__head">
<h2 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__h2" id="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps-h2">15 demographic survey questions to ask (with examples)</h2>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__intro">Copy-ready answer options, inclusive 2026 phrasing, and a clear note on when to skip each one.</p>
</div>
<ol class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__list">

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">1</span>Age range survey question</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Age is the most analytically useful demographic question for almost every survey. It segments cleanly, generalises across industries, and rarely feels invasive when offered as ranges.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Ask: What is your age?</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Under 18</li>
<li>18 to 24</li>
<li>25 to 34</li>
<li>35 to 44</li>
<li>45 to 54</li>
<li>55 to 64</li>
<li>65 or older</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Ranges feel less identifying, reduce drop-off, and roll up cleanly when you cross-tab against satisfaction or NPS. If you need an exact age (medical, financial, regulated industries), state why in a single line above the field.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__skip"><strong>Skip if:</strong> your survey already pulls age from a verified source (employee HRIS, customer profile, ID verification).</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">2</span>Gender identity survey question</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Use 2026 inclusive phrasing. The standard pattern is a closed list with a self-describe text field, kept separate from sex assigned at birth (which most surveys do not need at all).</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Ask: Which best describes your gender?</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Woman</li>
<li>Man</li>
<li>Non-binary or genderqueer</li>
<li>Prefer to self-describe (text field)</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Always include the self-describe option and always include "Prefer not to say". The <a href="https://go.arizona.edu/assessment-research/assets/Inclusive-and-Functional-Demographic-Questions_2019.pdf">University of Arizona Inclusive Demographic Questions guide</a> and the <a href="https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/7541739987355-U-S-Standard-demographic-questions">Greenhouse U.S. Standard demographic questions reference</a> both treat these as defaults.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__skip"><strong>Skip if:</strong> you cannot show how gender will be used in the analysis. Collecting it for completeness without a plan to segment by it adds friction without value.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">3</span>Ethnicity survey question</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Ethnicity belongs in a survey only when you plan to segment by it. Equity studies, representativeness checks, market research across regions, and DEI reporting all qualify. Customer-experience surveys for most B2B products do not.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Ask: Which of the following best describes you? Select all that apply.</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>American Indian or Alaska Native</li>
<li>Asian</li>
<li>Black or African American</li>
<li>Hispanic, Latino, or Spanish origin</li>
<li>Middle Eastern or North African</li>
<li>Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander</li>
<li>White</li>
<li>Prefer to self-describe (text field)</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Multi-select is the inclusive default. Forcing a single answer on multi-ethnic respondents either drops them out or skews data. The <a href="https://www.usda.gov/sites/default/files/documents/surveys-standard-optional-questions.pdf">USDA Standardized Optional Questions guidance</a> and U.S. Census categories are the public-sector references most readers expect.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__skip"><strong>Skip if:</strong> you are outside the use cases above. In the EU, UK, and most of Asia, ethnicity is treated as special-category data under <a href="https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/">GDPR Article 9</a> and needs explicit consent plus a documented purpose.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">4</span>Income survey question</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Income is the highest-friction demographic question on any survey. Use ranges, not free text. Decide upfront whether you need household income (consumer surveys) or individual income (employment, financial-services surveys). Mixing the two is a data-quality killer.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Ask: What is your annual household income?</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Under $25,000</li>
<li>$25,000 to $49,999</li>
<li>$50,000 to $74,999</li>
<li>$75,000 to $99,999</li>
<li>$100,000 to $149,999</li>
<li>$150,000 to $199,999</li>
<li>$200,000 or more</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">If your respondent pool is global, switch to currency-neutral bands (quintiles or local-currency tiers per country). State the time period (annual, monthly) and the unit explicitly in the question stem.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Overlapping numbers like "$20,000 to $40,000" and "$40,001 to $60,000" force respondents at the boundary to pick arbitrarily, which is why every range above uses "to" with a clear cap.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__skip"><strong>Skip if:</strong> you can infer the answer from a transactional dataset you already own.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">5</span>Employment survey question</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Employment status maps to spending power, life stage, and product fit. Consolidate "looking for a job" into this section rather than asking it as a separate yes / no.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Ask: Which best describes your current employment status?</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Employed full-time</li>
<li>Employed part-time</li>
<li>Self-employed or freelance</li>
<li>Unemployed and looking for work</li>
<li>Unemployed and not looking</li>
<li>Student</li>
<li>Homemaker or caregiver</li>
<li>Retired</li>
<li>Unable to work</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">This wording follows the <a href="https://www.bls.gov/cps/definitions.htm">U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics labour-force categories</a>, which is the cleanest segmentation if you ever need to compare your data against national benchmarks.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__skip"><strong>Skip if:</strong> you are not running a recruitment, careers, or workforce-development study; the options above already capture job-search status.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">6</span>Industry and job role survey question</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">If your survey is for B2B research, customer feedback, or workforce studies, role and industry are more useful than a free-text job title. Free text creates dirty data (147 variants of "Manager") and is hard to segment.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Ask: Which industry do you work in?</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Technology and software</li>
<li>Financial services</li>
<li>Healthcare</li>
<li>Education</li>
<li>Retail and ecommerce</li>
<li>Manufacturing</li>
<li>Government or public sector</li>
<li>Non-profit</li>
<li>Media and entertainment</li>
<li>Hospitality and travel</li>
<li>Construction or real estate</li>
<li>Other (text field)</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Then ask: Which best describes your role?</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Individual contributor</li>
<li>Team lead or manager</li>
<li>Director</li>
<li>VP or head of function</li>
<li>C-level or owner</li>
<li>Student or intern</li>
<li>Not currently working</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">The two-question pattern beats a free-text "job title" field on data quality every time.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__skip"><strong>Skip if:</strong> you already have firmographic data from a CRM or signup flow.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">7</span>Education survey question</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Ask for the highest level completed, not the level a respondent is currently working toward.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Ask: What is the highest level of education you have completed?</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Less than high school</li>
<li>High school diploma or equivalent</li>
<li>Some college, no degree</li>
<li>Trade or technical certificate</li>
<li>Associate degree</li>
<li>Bachelor's degree</li>
<li>Master's degree</li>
<li>Professional degree (MD, JD, etc.)</li>
<li>Doctoral degree (PhD, EdD)</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">For non-US audiences, swap the labels to local equivalents (A-levels, baccalauréat, gymnasium). Education systems do not translate cleanly, so build the localisation step into the survey rather than shipping a US-only list to a global panel.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__skip"><strong>Skip if:</strong> education does not change how you would interpret the answer to the rest of the survey.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">8</span>Relationship status survey question</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">"Marital status" alone misses the way most adults actually describe their relationships in 2026. Use "relationship status" as the headline and include the longer-form options.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Ask: Which best describes your relationship status?</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Single, never married</li>
<li>In a relationship, not living together</li>
<li>In a relationship, living together</li>
<li>Married or in a civil partnership</li>
<li>Separated</li>
<li>Divorced</li>
<li>Widowed</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__skip"><strong>Skip if:</strong> this is a customer-experience or product-feedback survey. Use it only when household structure, family planning, or relationship stage actually affects how someone uses the product or service.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">9</span>Household composition survey question</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Two short questions beat one. The first captures size, the second captures whether children are in the picture, which is what most consumer segmentation actually needs.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Ask: How many people live in your household, including you?</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>1 (just me)</li>
<li>2</li>
<li>3</li>
<li>4</li>
<li>5 or more</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Then ask: Do any children under 18 live in your household?</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Yes</li>
<li>No</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__skip"><strong>Skip if:</strong> you are not segmenting by household structure. For most B2B surveys this is dead weight.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">10</span>Location survey question</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Decide the granularity before you build the field. Country is enough for most market-research surveys. State or province is useful for regional rollouts. City is rarely worth the privacy cost.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Country only (most surveys):</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Dropdown listing all countries, with "Prefer not to say" at the top.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Country + region (regional studies):</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Two dependent dropdowns. Country first, then the state or province list refreshes based on the country.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">City and country (only when you ship something physical):</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Open city field plus country dropdown. State the use ("to estimate shipping zones") in one line above the field.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">For mobile-first surveys, use a country dropdown with type-ahead. A long radio list of 195 countries crashes form completion on a phone.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">11</span>Language survey question</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Language matters for global product research, accessibility audits, and content localisation studies.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Ask: Which language do you speak most often at home?</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>English</li>
<li>Spanish</li>
<li>Mandarin</li>
<li>Hindi</li>
<li>Arabic</li>
<li>French</li>
<li>Portuguese</li>
<li>Other (text field)</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">For multilingual respondents, follow up with "Which other languages do you speak fluently?" as a multi-select. If you are localising the survey itself, ask the language question first and route the respondent into a translated version.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">The <a href="https://www.surveymonkey.com/learn/survey-best-practices/gathering-demographic-information-from-surveys/">SurveyMonkey demographic best-practices guide</a> recommends placing language and accessibility questions before the rest of the demographics so the survey can adapt.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">12</span>Disability survey question</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">The <a href="https://www.washingtongroup-disability.com/question-sets/wg-short-set-on-functioning-wg-ss/">Washington Group Short Set on Functioning</a> is the global standard. It avoids the word "disability" (which respondents under-report) and asks about functioning in six domains instead. Use it directly.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Ask each of the following with the same answer scale:</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Do you have difficulty seeing, even if wearing glasses?</li>
<li>Do you have difficulty hearing, even if using a hearing aid?</li>
<li>Do you have difficulty walking or climbing steps?</li>
<li>Do you have difficulty remembering or concentrating?</li>
<li>Do you have difficulty with self-care, such as washing all over or dressing?</li>
<li>Using your usual language, do you have difficulty communicating?</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Answer scale (same for all six):</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>No, no difficulty</li>
<li>Yes, some difficulty</li>
<li>Yes, a lot of difficulty</li>
<li>Cannot do it at all</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__skip"><strong>Skip if:</strong> you are outside accessibility audits, equity studies, healthcare research, or product research focused on assistive technology. Asking it casually feels invasive.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">13</span>Sexual orientation survey question (optional)</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">This is a sensitive question. Ask only when you have a clear analytical reason (DEI study, healthcare research, LGBTQ+ market research) and you can show it in a privacy note above the field.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Ask: Which best describes your sexual orientation?</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Straight or heterosexual</li>
<li>Gay or lesbian</li>
<li>Bisexual or pansexual</li>
<li>Asexual</li>
<li>Queer</li>
<li>Prefer to self-describe (text field)</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">The <a href="https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/7541739987355-U-S-Standard-demographic-questions">Greenhouse U.S. Standard demographic questions reference</a> uses an equivalent list and the same self-describe + opt-out pattern.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__skip"><strong>Skip if:</strong> you cannot show a clear analytical use. Under <a href="https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/">GDPR Article 9</a>, sexual orientation is special-category data and EU respondents need explicit consent and a documented purpose.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">14</span>Technology and digital access survey question</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Useful for product research, digital-equity studies, and any survey where the answer might be shaped by what device the respondent is using.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Ask: Which devices do you use to access the internet? Select all that apply.</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Smartphone</li>
<li>Tablet</li>
<li>Laptop</li>
<li>Desktop computer</li>
<li>Smart TV</li>
<li>Game console</li>
<li>None of the above</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Then ask: How would you describe your home internet connection?</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Reliable broadband</li>
<li>Spotty broadband</li>
<li>Mobile data only</li>
<li>No home connection</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__skip"><strong>Skip if:</strong> device or connection quality has no bearing on your analysis.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">15</span>Religion and political affiliation (optional, sensitive)</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">Both are special-category data under <a href="https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/">GDPR Article 9</a> and both are flagged as sensitive in the <a href="https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.133.5.859">Tourangeau and Yan sensitive-question research</a>. Ask only when essential.</p>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Religion (use only when relevant):</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Christian</li>
<li>Muslim</li>
<li>Hindu</li>
<li>Buddhist</li>
<li>Jewish</li>
<li>Sikh</li>
<li>Spiritual but not religious</li>
<li>Atheist or agnostic</li>
<li>Other (text field)</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__ask">Political affiliation (use only when relevant):</p>
<ul class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__options">
<li>Liberal or progressive</li>
<li>Moderate</li>
<li>Conservative</li>
<li>Libertarian</li>
<li>Other (text field)</li>
<li>Prefer not to say</li>
</ul>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-steps__body">For US-specific political surveys, switch to party affiliation (Democrat, Republican, Independent, Other) with the same opt-out. Make both questions optional and place them last.</p>
</li>

</ol>
</div>
</section>

<div class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__container">
<h2 class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__h2" id="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq-h2">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__intro">Common questions about writing, ordering, and analysing demographic survey questions.</p>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__list">

<details class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__item">
<summary>What are the most important demographic survey questions to include?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__answer">Age, gender, location, and income are the four most-used demographic questions across consumer, employee, and academic surveys. Add ethnicity, language, employment status, and education when your analysis depends on them. Skip the rest. Most analyses need three to five demographic questions, not fifteen.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__item">
<summary>Where should demographic questions go in a survey?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__answer">Place them at the end. The SurveyMonkey best-practices guide and most academic guidance agree: asking sensitive identity questions upfront raises drop-off and depresses accuracy. The exception is language and accessibility, which go first so the survey can adapt to the respondent.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__item">
<summary>Should demographic questions be mandatory?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__answer">No. Every demographic question should include "Prefer not to say" and should not block submission. Forcing answers on income, ethnicity, or gender produces either drop-offs or random selections, both of which hurt your data.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__item">
<summary>How do I write inclusive gender questions in 2026?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__answer">Use a closed list with a self-describe text field and a "Prefer not to say" option. Keep gender identity separate from sex assigned at birth (most surveys do not need the second one at all). Avoid binary-only ("Male / Female") and avoid stand-alone "Other".</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__item">
<summary>How many demographic questions should I include?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__answer">Three to five. If your core survey has ten questions and your demographic section has twelve, the demographic section is the survey. Cut to the minimum that supports your analysis plan.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__item">
<summary>Is it legal to ask about income and ethnicity in a survey?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__answer">Yes, in most jurisdictions, but with conditions. Under GDPR Article 9, ethnicity is special-category data and needs explicit consent plus a documented purpose. In the US, the EEOC has specific guidance on collecting demographic data for hiring purposes. Income is not legally restricted but is socially sensitive: always use ranges and an opt-out.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__item">
<summary>What is the difference between demographic and psychographic survey questions?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__answer">Demographic questions describe who someone is (age, income, location). Psychographic questions describe how someone thinks (values, lifestyle, attitudes). Both segment your audience, but they answer different questions. Use demographic data for representativeness and equity checks, psychographic data for messaging and positioning.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__item">
<summary>When should I not ask demographic questions?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-demo-faq__answer">Skip a demographic question when you cannot point to a specific cross-tab in your analysis plan that uses it. "Just in case" demographic data is the most common reason surveys feel invasive and the most common reason respondents bail before submitting.</div>
</details>
