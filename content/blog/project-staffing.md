<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__container">
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__head">
<h2 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__h2" id="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps-h2">The project staffing process: 7 steps</h2>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__intro">Run this in order. Skipping a step does not save time; it moves the cost to the back of the project.</p>
</div>
<ol class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__list">

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">1</span>Capacity intake</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Before scoping anyone in, document who is already booked and at what percentage. Pull current allocations from your PM tool or use a <a class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__inline" href="https://formester.com/templates/project-resource-engagement-form-1916/">staffing request form</a> to collect availability from team leads in one pass.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">2</span>Scope-to-skill mapping</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Break the project into work packages. For each package, list the skill required, the seniority needed, and the estimated hours. Skill, not job title. "React with hooks experience" beats "frontend engineer."</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">3</span>Resource forecasting</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Add up hours per skill per week across the project length. This is the demand curve. Compare it against the supply curve from step 1. The gaps are what you actually need to staff.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">4</span>Role assignment</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Match named people to work packages, starting with the highest-risk skill first. Document allocation as a percentage (50%, 75%, 100%), not as a binary "assigned."</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">5</span>Kickoff and onboarding</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Share the staffing plan with the team in week one. Every person should see their role, allocation, start, end, and dependencies. Most onboarding failures are documentation failures, not introduction failures.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">6</span>Mid-project rebalance</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Re-check actual vs planned allocation at 30% and 70% of the timeline. If utilization is over 90% on any individual, rebalance before they burn out. If it's under 50%, free that person for the next intake.</p>
</li>

<li class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__item">
<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__title"><span class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__num" aria-hidden="true">7</span>Post-project review</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__body">Within two weeks of project close, log what actually happened: estimated hours vs actual, skill gaps you hit, who delivered above plan. This data is the input to your next forecast.</p>
</li>

</ol>

<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__sub">Project staffing plan template: 9 fields</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__lede">Copy these nine columns into a sheet, or use the <a class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__inline" href="https://formester.com/templates/project-intake-form-12251/">project intake form</a> template to collect them as structured submissions instead of free-text rows.</p>

<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__rules">
<ul>
<li><strong>Allocation is a percentage, not a yes/no.</strong> "75%" tells the next PM what's left.</li>
<li><strong>Status updates weekly, not at milestones.</strong> By milestone, the slip is already real.</li>
<li><strong>Skills column lists the tool or technique, not the seniority label.</strong> "React Server Components" not "senior frontend."</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__sub">Project staffing models compared</h3>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__lede">Two filters decide between them: how core the work is to your IP, and how stable the demand is. Core plus stable picks in-house. Non-core plus spiky picks staff augmentation. Most companies end up hybrid.</p>

<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__tablewrap">
<table class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-steps__table">
<thead><tr><th>Model</th><th>Best for</th><th>Cost band</th><th>Ramp time</th><th>Control</th><th>Exit cost</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>In-house</td><td>Core IP work, recurring projects</td><td>Highest fixed</td><td>Slow (hire cycle)</td><td>Highest</td><td>Severance</td></tr>

<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__container">
<h2 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__h2" id="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq-h2">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__intro">Common questions about the project staffing process, plan, and models.</p>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__list">

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>What are the 7 steps of the project staffing process?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Capacity intake, scope-to-skill mapping, resource forecasting, role assignment, kickoff and onboarding, mid-project rebalance, and post-project review. The first three happen before the project starts; the last four span the project lifecycle.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>What should be included in a project staffing plan?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Nine fields: role, department, employment type, headcount, specific skills required, start date, end date, percentage allocation, and current status (filled, open, or at risk). Allocation as a percentage matters more than a binary assigned or not assigned.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>What is the difference between project staffing and resource planning?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Resource planning is the long view: how many people with which skills your organisation will need across all projects over the next 6 to 12 months. Project staffing is the short view: who, specifically, is on this project, at what percentage, from when to when.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>How do you know if your project staffing plan is working?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Three signals. Actual hours per role land within 10% of planned hours. No individual stays over 90% allocated for more than two consecutive weeks. Skill gaps are caught 4+ weeks before the work is due, not at the work's due date.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>What is staff augmentation in project staffing?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Staff augmentation is bringing in contractors, freelancers, or vendor consultants to fill a skill gap or absorb a surge in demand on an existing project team. Used when the skill is short-term, the gap is small, or in-house hiring lead time is too slow.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>Which project staffing model is best?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">There is no single best. Use in-house for core IP and recurring work, outsourced for defined-scope non-core work, staff augmentation for skill gaps and surge capacity, and hybrid (a mix) when work spans both core and routine. Most companies running 50+ people end up hybrid.</div>
</details>
