<p style="font-size: inherit;">A QR code turns your form into something people can open by pointing a phone at it. No link to type, no URL to remember. Scan, and the form loads.</p>

<p>That makes QR codes the simplest way to collect responses off a poster, a table tent, a product label, or a slide at the back of a room. This guide shows you how to generate one for a form or survey, customize it, download it, and place it where people will actually scan.</p>

<p>If you already build forms in Formester, the QR code lives in the Publish tab and points straight at your live form, so it keeps working even after you edit the questions.</p>

<h2>Why a QR Code Beats Sharing a Link</h2>

<ul>
<li>No typing. People scan and land on the form instead of copying a long URL off a screen or flyer.</li>
<li>Works anywhere a phone goes. A QR code on a table, badge, or receipt collects responses in the moment, before the intent fades.</li>
<li>Fewer dead ends. A scanned link cannot be mistyped, so you lose fewer people to a 404 or a wrong address.</li>
<li>Higher response rates on print. A code on a poster or product turns a passive glance into a tap, which is why retail and events lean on them for feedback.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Where QR Codes for Forms Actually Pay Off</h2>

<h3>Events and conferences</h3>

<p>Print the code on badges, signage, and slides so attendees register or leave feedback without queueing at a laptop.</p>

<h3>Surveys and customer feedback</h3>

<p>Put a QR code on a receipt, table tent, or packaging and you capture opinions while the experience is fresh. Build the survey first with Formester's <a href="https://formester.com/online-survey-maker/">online survey maker</a>, then generate the code from it.</p>

<h3>Marketing campaigns</h3>

<p>Add the code to flyers, ads, and product inserts to capture leads or run a giveaway. One scan replaces a typed landing-page URL.</p>

<h3>Product registration</h3>

<p>Link the code on the box straight to a <a href="https://formester.com/blog/how-to-make-a-registration-form/">registration form</a> so buyers activate the product in seconds.</p>

<h3>Classrooms and training</h3>

<p>Share quizzes and course-evaluation forms by code so students open them on their own phones instead of passing a link around.</p>

<h2>What About a QR Code for a Google Form?</h2>

<p>Google Forms has no built-in QR generator. The workaround is manual: open your form, click Send, copy the link, then paste it into a separate QR code generator and download the image.</p>

<p>It works, but you juggle two tools and the code is a flat image you cannot recolor or track inside the form. If you want the QR code, the form, and the responses in one place, build the form where the QR code is already part of publishing.</p>

<p>For the step-by-step Google Forms route, see <a href="https://formester.com/blog/how-to-make-a-qr-code-for-a-google-form/">how to make a QR code for a Google Form</a>. The rest of this guide shows the one-tool version in Formester.</p>

<h2>Best Practices for Form QR Codes</h2>

<ul>
<li>Say what the scan is for. "Scan to register" or "Scan to give feedback" beats a bare code every time.</li>
<li>Keep it big and high-contrast. Aim for at least 2 by 2 cm on print, larger if people scan from a distance.</li>
<li>Test on two phones before you print. Check one iPhone, one Android, in the lighting where the code will live.</li>
<li>Place it at eye level. A code low on a wall or buried in fine print gets skipped.</li>
<li>Give people a reason. A discount, early access, or a prize draw lifts scan rates on a campaign.</li>
</ul>

<h2>QR Code Not Scanning? Fix These First</h2>

<h3>The code will not scan at all</h3>

<p>Usually it is too small or too low contrast. Make it bigger, use dark code on a light background, and remove any logo that covers the corner squares.</p>

<h3>It scans but the form does not open</h3>

<p>The link is broken or the form is unpublished. Check the form is live and the link still resolves, then regenerate the code.</p>

<h3>Low scan numbers</h3>

<p>Either people cannot see the code or they have no reason to act. Move it to eye level and add a clear, benefit-led prompt.</p>

<h3>Glare on a printed code</h3>

<p>Glossy stock reflects light and blinds the camera. Print on matte where you can, and keep the code off curved or wrinkled surfaces.</p>
