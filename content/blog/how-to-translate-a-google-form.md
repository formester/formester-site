<p style="font-size: inherit;">Google Forms has no translate button. There is no language setting in the editor and no way to offer one form in two languages, which is why this question keeps ending up in forums rather than in Google's documentation. Three routes get you there, and they do quite different things to the data you end up with.</p>

<blockquote><p>There is no built-in translation in Google Forms. Your options are to let the respondent's <strong>browser</strong> translate the page, install a Marketplace <strong>add-on</strong> that rewrites the form, or <strong>duplicate</strong> the form and translate it by hand. Browser translation changes only what is on screen. The add-on changes the form itself.</p></blockquote>

<h2>Which route to pick</h2>

<p>The last column is the part most guides leave out, and it is usually what decides the answer.</p>

<div class="table-wrap">
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Route</th><th>What it gives you</th><th>What lands in your responses</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><a href="#browser">Browser translation</a></td><td>Read-only translation, per respondent</td><td>Original language, because nothing in the form changed</td></tr>
<tr><td><a href="#add-on">Marketplace add-on</a></td><td>The form itself, rewritten</td><td>The translated language</td></tr>
<tr><td><a href="#duplicate">A duplicated form</a></td><td>One controlled form per language</td><td>A separate response sheet per language</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<h2 id="browser">1. Let the browser translate it</h2>

<p>Anyone opening the form can read it in their own language, without you changing anything.</p>
<ol>
<li>Send the form link as normal.</li>
<li>The respondent opens it in Chrome, Edge or Safari and accepts the translate prompt, or right-clicks and picks <strong>Translate</strong>.</li>
<li>The questions render in their language straight away.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Where it stops.</strong> Nothing about the form changes, so this is display only. It relies on each respondent noticing the prompt, and it does not translate images or text inside images.</p>

<h2 id="add-on">2. Use a Marketplace add-on</h2>

<p>One click rewrites the whole form into another language.</p>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/48aa22f37b3668c1_01-get-add-ons.jpg" alt="The Google Forms editor three-dot menu with Get add-ons, the route to installing a translation add-on" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:292px;height:auto;border-radius:10px;border:1px solid #d8dce3;box-shadow:0 1px 3px rgba(16,24,40,0.08);margin:18px 0;" /></p>
<ol>
<li>In the form editor, open the three-dot menu and choose <strong>Get add-ons</strong>.</li>
<li>Search for <strong>Forms Translator</strong> and install it.</li>
<li>Open it from the add-ons menu, choose your target language and run it.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Where it stops.</strong> It is a third party with edit access to your forms, which some IT departments will not approve. Translation quality is machine translation, so labels that matter need a human read afterwards.</p>

<h2 id="duplicate">3. Duplicate the form per language</h2>

<p>A separate, fully controlled form for each language.</p>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/3765dfe7d36f968e_02-make-a-copy.jpg" alt="The Google Forms editor three-dot menu with Make a copy at the top, the route to duplicating a form per language" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:292px;height:auto;border-radius:10px;border:1px solid #d8dce3;box-shadow:0 1px 3px rgba(16,24,40,0.08);margin:18px 0;" /></p>
<ol>
<li>Open the form, use the three-dot menu and choose <strong>Make a copy</strong>.</li>
<li>Translate every question, option and description by hand, or paste in translations you already have.</li>
<li>Share the right link with the right audience.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Where it stops.</strong> Every language becomes its own form with its own response sheet, so a five-language campaign means five forms to keep in sync and five sets of results to merge.</p>

<h2 id="add-on-detail">What the Forms Translator add-on actually does</h2>

<p>It is the top result for this search, so it is worth being specific about it. <strong>Forms Translator</strong> is published by Translator for Forms and sits on the Google Workspace Marketplace with more than 191,000 installs, free of charge at the time of writing.</p>
<ul>
<li>Translates a whole form in one action rather than field by field</li>
<li>Advertises 130+ languages, running on Google Translate underneath</li>
<li>States that your data stays in your Google account and is not copied to its own database</li>
</ul>
<p>Because it edits the form, the questions your respondents see are the translated ones, and so are the answers that come back. That is the opposite of browser translation, and it is the reason to decide which you want before you send the link.</p>

<h2 id="on-phone">Translating a Google Form on a phone</h2>

<p>There is no Google Forms mobile app, so a form on a phone is a web page in a mobile browser, and the browser does the work.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Chrome on Android.</strong> Open the form, tap the three-dot menu, then <strong>Translate</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Safari on iOS.</strong> Tap the page settings icon in the address bar, then <strong>Translate to</strong>.</li>
</ul>
<p>Editing a form on a phone is a different matter. The add-on route needs the desktop editor, so build and translate on a computer and treat the phone as the respondent's device.</p>

<h2 id="spanish">Translating a Google Form to Spanish, or any single language</h2>

<p>None of the three routes has a special case for a particular language. Pick Spanish in the browser prompt, in the add-on's target dropdown, or in your own translation of a duplicated form.</p>
<p>What does change by language is how much a machine translation can be trusted. Formal register, gendered nouns and anything legal are the places a machine gets it close but not right, so have a speaker read the output before it goes to a real audience.</p>

<h2 id="what-none-fix">What none of the three routes fix</h2>

<p>Every route above solves the reading problem. None of them solves the collecting problem.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>No language switcher.</strong> A respondent cannot choose their language on the form. Either their browser offers it or they read whatever you sent.</li>
<li><strong>Mixed-language free text.</strong> Whatever route you pick, people type paragraph answers in their own language, so open questions arrive multilingual.</li>
<li><strong>Duplicated forms fragment your data.</strong> Five languages means five response sheets to merge before you can report on anything.</li>
<li><strong>Nothing tells you which language someone used.</strong> There is no field recording it, so you cannot segment by language afterwards.</li>
</ul>
