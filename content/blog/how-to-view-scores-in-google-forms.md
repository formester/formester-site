<p style="font-size: inherit;">You built the quiz. The responses are in. Now you want the scores, and Google Forms has hidden them in three different places.</p>

<p>There's also one setting that decides whether your respondents ever see their own score. Pick the wrong option when you build the form and every score goes out the second someone hits submit, before you've checked a single answer.</p>

<blockquote><p>Scores live under <strong>Responses</strong>, in three tabs. Summary gives you the class picture. Question marks one question across everyone. Individual shows a single person's paper. Whether they see their score is a separate setting, <strong>Release grades</strong>, in Settings.</p></blockquote>

<h2>First, the form has to be a quiz</h2>

<p>Google Forms won't calculate a score unless quiz mode is on. A plain form collects answers and stops there. That's why people open Responses looking for scores and find nothing but a list of submissions.</p>

<ol>
<li>Open the form and click <strong>Settings</strong>.</li>
<li>Turn on <strong>Make this a quiz</strong>.</li>
</ol>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_s1_make_this_a_quiz_27ecc2c2c2.jpg" alt="Google Forms Settings with Make this a quiz switched on, revealing the Release grades and Respondent settings options" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<p>One choice here matters more than it looks. If you decide to hold scores back for review, Google switches on <strong>Collect email addresses</strong> for you and sets it to <strong>Verified</strong>.</p>

<p>Verified means everyone has to sign in to a Google account. That's fine in a school on Workspace. It quietly locks out anyone who doesn't have a Google login.</p>

<p>If your respondents are the public, switch it:</p>

<ol>
<li>Open <strong>Settings</strong> and expand <strong>Responses</strong>.</li>
<li>Set <strong>Collect email addresses</strong> to <strong>Responder input</strong>.</li>
</ol>

<p>They type an address instead of signing in, and manual release still works.</p>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_s4b_collect_email_verified_7fdff40d20.jpg" alt="Collect email addresses set to Verified, labelled Required to release grades manually and Respondents will be required to sign in to Google" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<p>Building the quiz itself is a separate job. If you're starting from an empty form, our walkthrough on <a href="https://formester.com/blog/how-to-create-a-quiz-in-google-forms-the-only-guide-youll-need-in-2023/">how to create a quiz in Google Forms</a> covers question setup.</p>

<h2>Set the answer key and the points</h2>

<p>A score is just the sum of the points you attached to correct answers. Nothing shows up until you tell Google which answers are right.</p>

<ol>
<li>Click a question, then click <strong>Answer key</strong> at the bottom left.</li>
<li>Pick the correct answer.</li>
<li>Set the point value in the top right.</li>
</ol>

<p>Add answer feedback attaches an explanation or a YouTube video that appears once grading is done. Worth the extra minute on questions people reliably get wrong.</p>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_s2_answer_key_points_427e99112a.jpg" alt="Answer key panel on a multiple choice question with Mitochondrion marked correct and the question set to 2 points" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<p>Two things will catch you out. New questions default to 0 points, so anything you skipped contributes nothing to the total.</p>

<p>And the points box only takes whole numbers. Type 0.5 and it'll sit there looking accepted. Reload the form and it's back to 0, with no warning. There are no half marks, so scale up instead: make the question worth 2 and award 1 for a partial answer.</p>

<p>Answer keys don't work on every question type. Six of them grade themselves:</p>

</tbody>

<p>You can still put points on any question type, a paragraph answer included. What you lose is the automatic part. Someone has to read it and award the marks.</p>

<p>On a paragraph question the panel even renames itself to "Edit feedback and points", because there's no correct answer to set.</p>

<p>Short answer grading has a trap worth knowing before you send a quiz to thirty people. The match is exact, capitalisation and all. A near miss isn't marked wrong. It's left ungraded.</p>

<p>Answer "photosynthesis" against a key of "Photosynthesis" and you get an empty points box and a quiet "1 ungraded response" label. That person's total under-reports until somebody grades it by hand.</p>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_s8_ungraded_case_mismatch_b8838b5419.jpg" alt="Question view showing Photosynthesis graded 2 out of 2 while the lowercase photosynthesis sits as one ungraded response with an empty points box" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<p>Two ways out. List every spelling and capitalisation you'll accept as separate correct answers, or tick <strong>Mark all other answers incorrect</strong> so a miss scores a real zero instead of sitting in limbo.</p>

<h2>Where scores appear once responses come in</h2>

<p>Click <strong>Responses</strong> at the top of the quiz. The three tabs answer different questions, and picking the wrong one is why grading takes longer than it should.</p>

<p>Summary is the one to open first. It reads out the average, median and range against the total, and the distribution chart shows the shape of the class in a glance.</p>

<p>Google also surfaces frequently missed questions here, once a quiz has enough responses for the pattern to mean anything. <h2>Release scores automatically after each submission</h2>

<p>This is the default, and it catches people out. Unless you changed it, every respondent saw their score the moment they submitted.</p>

<p>To check which mode you're on:</p>

<ol>
<li>Open <strong>Settings</strong>.</li>
<li>Under <strong>Release grades</strong>, pick <strong>Immediately after each submission</strong>.</li>
</ol>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_s4_release_grades_manual_52e4dcee67.jpg" alt="Release grades set to Later, after manual review, with the note that it turns on Collect email addresses" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<p>Instant release suits practice quizzes and revision, or anything where the score is feedback rather than a grade. It only works cleanly when every question has an answer key.</p>

<p>A paragraph question in an instant-release quiz shows up ungraded, which reads to the respondent as a zero they didn't earn.</p>

<h2>Release scores later, after you review</h2>

<p>Pick <strong>Later, after manual review</strong> in the same setting and Google holds every score until you send it. Respondents see a confirmation that their response was recorded and nothing about how they did.</p>

<p>There are two ways to hand the scores back. For one person at a time:</p>

<ol>
<li>Open <strong>Responses</strong> and click <strong>Individual</strong>.</li>
<li>Click <strong>Release score</strong> at the top right.</li>
<li>Tick the people you want, then confirm with <strong>Send emails and release</strong>.</li>
</ol>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_s5_individual_release_score_f0eada3b11.jpg" alt="Individual response view showing 4 of 8 points, a padlock reading Score not released, and the Release score button" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<p>To release in bulk, stay on <strong>Summary</strong>. The Scores panel lists every respondent with a "Score released" column and a single <strong>Release scores</strong> button covers the lot.</p>

<p>That button only appears on responses that have an email address attached. If a response came in before email collection was switched on, Google Forms gives you no way to hand that person a score. You can't fix it retroactively.</p>

<h2>What respondents see, and what they cannot</h2>

<p>What lands on a respondent's screen is controlled separately from the release mode. In <strong>Settings</strong>, under <strong>Respondent settings</strong>, three toggles decide what they get:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Missed questions.</strong> Whether they can see which ones they got wrong.</li>
<li><strong>Correct answers.</strong> Whether the right answers are revealed after grades go out.</li>
<li><strong>Point values.</strong> Whether they see the total and the points per question.</li>
</ul>

<p>They're independent, so you can show someone their total while hiding the correct answers. That's the setup you want if the same quiz goes to a second group later.</p>

<p>Two limits are worth stating plainly, because they're what respondents actually ask about.</p>

<p>Until you release, they see nothing at all. The confirmation page says only "Your response has been recorded", with no score and no hint that one exists. That single line is the honest answer to most "why can't I see my score" searches.</p>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_s7_response_recorded_no_score_e29ed37043.jpg" alt="Google Forms confirmation page reading Your response has been recorded, with no score shown to the respondent" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<p>Respondents also can't browse back to an old score. Google Forms emails it or shows it once at submission. There's no student dashboard to log into, so tell people to keep the email.</p>

<p>And you can't see results for a form that isn't yours. Response data belongs to the owner. Unless they gave you editing access or published a summary link, the answer is no. No add-on gets around it.</p>

<h2>How to stop showing scores</h2>

<p>To take the score away after the fact:</p>

<ol>
<li>Open <strong>Settings</strong> and set <strong>Release grades</strong> to <strong>Later, after manual review</strong>.</li>
<li>Don't release the scores.</li>
</ol>

<p>New submissions stop showing a score straight away.</p>

<p>To keep the total but hide the working, leave the release mode alone and switch off the respondent toggles for missed questions and correct answers. People still get a number, and your question bank stays unpublished.</p>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_s9_respondent_toggles_off_6a38d03e40.jpg" alt="Google Forms Respondent settings with Missed questions and Correct answers switched off while Point values stays on" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<p>One thing this won't do: scores you already emailed can't be recalled. Changing the setting affects what happens next, not what already went out.</p>

<h2>Where Google Forms scoring runs out</h2>

<p>For a quick knowledge check, quiz mode is genuinely enough. The gaps show up when the quiz carries weight, like a certification, a hiring screen or an exam with a pass mark.</p>

<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>What you need</th><th>Google Forms</th></tr>
</thead>

<tr><td>Pass or fail threshold</td><td>Not available, you compare totals yourself</td></tr>
<tr><td>Negative marking for wrong answers</td><td>Not available</td></tr>

<tr><td>Half marks or decimal points</td><td>Not available, the field takes 0.5 then saves 0</td></tr>

<tr><td>Quiz timer</td><td>No built-in timer, add-ons only</td></tr>

Scores you already emailed can't be pulled back.</p></details>
<details><summary>What is view result summary in Google Forms?</summary><p>It's the Summary tab under Responses. For a quiz it shows the average, median and range of scores, plus a chart of how many people scored what. It's the fastest read on how a group performed.</p></details>
<details><summary>How do I view my score in a Google Form as a student?</summary><p>That depends on how your teacher set the quiz up. If grades release immediately, your score appears right after you submit, usually behind a "View score" link.</p><p>If the quiz is set to manual review, you get an email once your teacher releases it. There's no page you can log into to check later, so keep that email.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can I see the results of a Google Form that isn't mine?</summary><p>No. Responses are only visible to the form owner and anyone they add as an editor. If you need the results, ask the owner to share editing access or send you a summary. No add-on or workaround gets around this.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can I view Google Forms scores on my phone?</summary><p>Yes. Open the form in a mobile browser and the Responses tab works the same way, including all three views. There's no dedicated Google Forms app for iPhone or Android.</p><p>Grading long written answers on a phone is slow going. The Question view is the least painful of the three.</p></details>
