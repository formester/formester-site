## Types of CRMs
There are three main types of CRMs designed for specific needs.

#### 1. Operational CRM
This type focuses on managing workflows and tracking leads efficiently.

**Purpose:** Automates repetitive tasks, organizes sales pipelines, and streamlines customer service.

**Example:** A small business tracking sales progress and managing customer interactions.

**In Notion:** Use CRM templates for lead tracking, kanban boards, and sales progress tracking.

#### 2. Analytical CRM
These CRMs analyze customer data to improve decision-making.
**Purpose:** Tracks trends, measures ROI, and predicts sales outcomes.

**Example:** A marketing agency analyzing campaign performance.

**In Notion:** Leverage databases with deep filtering options, charts, and advanced reporting.

#### 3. Collaborative CRM
Collaborative CRMs enhance communication within teams and with customers. **<a href="https://www.listalpha.com/guides/private-equity-crm-software-guide">Private equity CRM software</a>** often includes advanced collaboration features to help investment teams stay aligned and manage client relationships effectively.

**Purpose:** Shares relevant information across departments for a unified customer experience.

**Example:** A team managing shared client information and tasks.

**In Notion:** Build shared workspaces with custom permissions to manage client data.
Notion’s flexibility lets you build CRMs tailored to any of these categories.

### How to Make a CRM in Notion
Building a CRM in Notion is simple and requires only a few steps.

#### Step 1: Set Up Your Database

* Open a Notion page and select “Database Table” as your base.

* Add columns for customer name, email, phone number, status, next steps, and notes.

#### Step 2: Customize Your Views

* Use Kanban View to visualize sales pipelines and move leads through different stages.

* Switch to Calendar View for follow-up scheduling.

* Explore other database views like lists and galleries for specific tasks.

#### Step 3: Add Tags and Filters

* Use tags such as “Lead,” “In Progress,” or “Closed” to track progress.

* Apply deep filtering options to sort by priority, deadlines, or team assignments.

#### Step 4: Leverage CRM Templates

* Use Notion CRM templates to save time on repetitive tasks like onboarding or follow-ups.

* Customize templates to include deal size, preferred contact methods, and other metrics.

#### Step 5: Integrate Additional Features

* Add widgets for charts, summaries, and metrics visualization.

* Use Notion databases to link tasks, projects, and sales metrics for a full overview.

### How to Automate Data Entry
Now let's see how to automate the data entry using online forms:

#### Step 1: Set Up a Notion Database
Start by creating a database in Notion where your form responses will be stored. If you already have one, skip this step.

* In Notion, click + Add a page and choose Database > Table.

* Name your database and add columns for each question in your form (like Name, Email, or Feedback).

* Set the right column types, like Text or Multi-select, and add choices for dropdowns if needed.
This will be your central hub for storing data.

#### Step 2: Create a Form in Formester
Log in to Formester and click + New Form. Use the drag-and-drop builder to design your form with fields that match your Notion columns. Customize it with themes, conditional logic (e.g., hide questions based on answers), or autoresponders to thank users.

Once your form is ready, it’s time to connect it with Notion!

#### Step 3: Connect Formester to Notion
Use **[Zapier](https://zapier.com/)** to link your form to your Notion database.

* Go to Formester’s Automation tab and select Notion.

* Set Formester as the trigger app and choose New Form Submission.

* Select your form and test it with a sample response.

* Choose Notion as the action app and map your form fields to your Notion columns.

Now, every form submission will automatically update your Notion database!

#### Step 4: Test and Share

* Submit a test form and check if the data appears in your Notion database. Once it works perfectly, publish your form.

* Share the form link via email, social media, or embed it on your website to start collecting data automatically.

With these simple steps, you’ll collect data effortlessly and keep everything organized in Notion!
