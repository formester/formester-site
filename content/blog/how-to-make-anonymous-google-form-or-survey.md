<p style="font-size: inherit;">An anonymous Google Form is one where nothing in the response ties back to the person who filled it in: no email, no name, no employee ID. It takes about a minute to set up, and the setting people miss is not the obvious one. What trips teams up is a second option elsewhere in Settings that quietly re-identifies everyone.</p>

<div class="tldr">
<p><strong>Key facts</strong></p>
<p>Open <strong>Settings</strong>, expand <strong>Responses</strong>, set <strong>Collect email addresses</strong> to <strong>Do not collect</strong>, then delete any question asking for a name or an ID. Submit the form yourself and check the Responses tab: if there is no Email column, it is anonymous.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Google Forms does not collect emails by default</strong>, so a fresh form is anonymous until someone changes it</li>
<li><strong>The setting that breaks it: Limit to 1 response.</strong> It requires every respondent to sign in to a Google Account (<a href="https://support.google.com/docs/answer/2839588" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google Docs Editors Help</a>)</li>
<li><strong>So does quiz grading set to "Later, after manual review"</strong>, which turns email collection back on</li>
<li><strong>IP addresses are not shown to the form owner.</strong> Google's own abuse logs are a separate matter</li>
<li><strong>How to verify: 1 test submission.</strong> No Email column in the Responses tab or the linked Sheet means it is clean (<a href="https://support.google.com/docs/answer/139706" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google Docs Editors Help</a>)</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 id="how">How do you make a Google Form anonymous?</h2>

<p>Three steps, and only the first one is a setting. The other two are the reason forms that look anonymous often are not.</p>

<h3>1. Turn off email collection</h3>
<p>Open the form and click <strong>Settings</strong>, expand <strong>Responses</strong>, then set <strong>Collect email addresses</strong> to <strong>Do not collect</strong>. On a new form this is already the default, so the job is usually confirming it rather than changing it.</p>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/46ee6f6816ea9e6c_anon-01-do-not-collect.jpg" alt="Google Forms Settings with Collect email addresses set to Do not collect, marked with a red arrow" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:790px;height:auto;border-radius:10px;border:1px solid #d8dce3;box-shadow:0 1px 3px rgba(16,24,40,0.08);margin:18px 0;" /></p>

<h3>2. Remove identifying questions</h3>
<p>A form with email collection off is still not anonymous if the first question asks for a name. Delete anything that asks for name, employee ID, phone number, department in a small team, or anything else that narrows the response to one person. On a team of eight, "which department are you in" is an identifier.</p>

<h3>3. Verify with a test submission</h3>
<p>Fill the form in yourself and open the <strong>Responses</strong> tab. If there is no Email column there and none in the linked Google Sheet, the form is genuinely collecting nothing. This takes thirty seconds and is the only way to be sure, because the settings panel tells you what is configured rather than what is recorded.</p>

<h2 id="breaks">Which settings quietly break anonymity?</h2>

<p>Two, and neither of them is on the Responses panel where you would look for them.</p>
<p><strong>Limit to 1 response.</strong> This is the big one. It sits under a <strong>REQUIRES SIGN IN</strong> label, and Google's documentation is explicit that respondents must sign in to a Google Account to fill the form in. You cannot have duplicate-blocking and anonymity at the same time.</p>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/e6d94b1fa71e5957_anon-02-requires-sign-in.jpg" alt="The Limit to 1 response setting in Google Forms sitting under a REQUIRES SIGN IN label, marked in red" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:790px;height:auto;border-radius:10px;border:1px solid #d8dce3;box-shadow:0 1px 3px rgba(16,24,40,0.08);margin:18px 0;" /></p>
<p><strong>Quiz grades released after manual review.</strong> If the form is a quiz and you set <strong>Release grades</strong> to "Later, after manual review", Google turns email collection back on so it has somewhere to send the score. The option says so in small grey text under the radio button, and it is easy to miss.</p>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/e90d133ba1164749_quiz-02-release-grades.jpg" alt="Google Forms Release grades options, with Later after manual review marked in red because it turns email collection back on" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:720px;height:auto;border-radius:10px;border:1px solid #d8dce3;box-shadow:0 1px 3px rgba(16,24,40,0.08);margin:18px 0;" /></p>

<h2 id="tracked">What does Google still track on an anonymous form?</h2>

<p>This is the question the setting does not answer, and it is worth being precise about rather than reassuring.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The form owner sees no IP address.</strong> Google Forms does not log or display respondent IPs to you, and there is no column for it in the responses or the Sheet.</li>
<li><strong>Google's own systems are separate.</strong> Its internal logs may retain data for spam and abuse handling, which is Google's business rather than yours, but it means "anonymous" describes what you can see rather than what exists.</li>
<li><strong>Timestamps are always recorded.</strong> On a small team, a submission time can identify someone as effectively as a name.</li>
<li><strong>Free-text answers identify people constantly.</strong> Someone describing their own project in a comment box has told you exactly who they are, whatever the settings say.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="best-practice">How do you run an anonymous survey people actually trust?</h2>

<p>The settings are the easy half. Getting honest answers depends on the respondent believing the form, and a form that demands a login before it will take feedback about a manager does not get honest feedback whatever the privacy notice says.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Say so on the form.</strong> Put a line in the description: this survey is anonymous, we do not collect your name, email or any identifying information.</li>
<li><strong>Keep the questions broad.</strong> Every demographic field narrows the pool, and three of them on a team of twenty identifies almost everyone.</li>
<li><strong>Do not promise what the tool cannot do.</strong> If you have turned on Limit to 1 response, the form is not anonymous and saying otherwise is worse than saying nothing.</li>
<li><strong>Share the results.</strong> People answer the next survey honestly based on what happened after the last one.</li>
</ul>
