<h2>Which HTML form builder should you choose?</h2>

<p>An HTML form builder solves an old problem: your site needs a form, and you'd rather not build and maintain the backend that catches the submissions. The tools that solve it fall into two camps.</p>

<p>Hosted builders (Formester, Jotform, Google Forms) give you a visual editor and an embed snippet. Form endpoints (Formspree, Basin, Getform, Netlify Forms) let you write the HTML yourself and point it at a URL that does the rest.</p>

<p>Which camp you belong to decides most of this comparison, so settle that first: do you want to write your own markup, or would you rather never see it?</p>

<p>The quick picks are below and the full reviews follow.</p>

<div class="tldr">
<p><strong>The 60-second TL;DR</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Best all-around for hosted forms + HTML embed:</strong> Formester, unlimited responses free, one-line embed</li>
<li><strong>Best pure form endpoint for developers:</strong> Formspree, POST to a URL, from $10/mo</li>
<li><strong>Best if you already host on Netlify:</strong> Netlify Forms, free and unlimited on current plans</li>
<li><strong>Best endpoint with team features:</strong> Basin, from $15/mo</li>
<li><strong>Best endpoint for file uploads:</strong> Getform, 100 free submissions/mo, paid from $15.83/mo billed yearly</li>
<li><strong>Best hosted builder for template depth:</strong> Jotform, 20,000+ templates, from $39/mo</li>
<li><strong>Best for WordPress-hosted forms:</strong> Gravity Forms, annual licenses from $59/year</li>
<li><strong>Best free workspace embed:</strong> Google Forms, iframe embed with any Google account</li>
</ul>

<h2>1. Formester, best all-around</h2>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/formester_99e2a99609.png" alt="Formester homepage hero screenshot" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" loading="lazy" /></p>

<p>Formester is a hosted builder, so you design the form in a visual editor and drop one line of embed code into your HTML.</p>

<p>What makes it unusual in this list is the metering, or rather the lack of it: unlimited forms and unlimited responses on every plan, including the free one. Every endpoint below counts your submissions. Formester doesn't.</p>

<p>The editor is current and comfortable to live in. Changes preview live, the output is mobile-responsive by default, and the AI generator builds a form from a prompt, a PDF, or a pasted list of questions, all on the free plan.</p>

<p>The embed works the same on hand-coded HTML, Astro, Webflow, or WordPress.</p>

<p>Paid plans add features rather than volume. Personal at $13 a month brings conditional logic, and Business at $49 adds a custom domain and 25 team seats. On yearly billing those drop to $12 and $45.</p>

<h2>2. Formspree, best pure form endpoint for developers</h2>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/shot_formspree_2dbff89c83.png" alt="Formspree homepage hero screenshot" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" loading="lazy" /></p>

<p>Formspree is the classic form endpoint: write your own HTML, set the form's action to your Formspree URL, and submissions arrive in your dashboard and inbox.</p>

<p>There's no builder to learn because there's no builder at all, which for many developers is exactly the appeal.</p>

<p>The workflow stays simple as you scale, and the plan steps are mostly about volume. The free tier covers 50 submissions a month, Personal at $10 covers 200, Professional at $20 covers 2,000 and adds a team member, and Business at $60 covers 20,000.</p>

<p>Spam protection pairs a honeypot with reCAPTCHA integration.</p>

<p>One billing note: these are the prices on Formspree's monthly toggle, and the yearly toggle showed the same rates when we checked, so we can't quote you a yearly discount.</p>

<h2>3. Netlify Forms, best for sites hosted on Netlify</h2>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/shot_netlify_0887212adc.png" alt="Netlify Forms homepage hero screenshot" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" loading="lazy" /></p>

<p>Netlify Forms is the zero-integration option when your site already deploys on Netlify: add a data-netlify attribute to your form tag and the platform detects and collects submissions at build time. No endpoint URL, no external service, no extra account.</p>

<p>The pricing recently changed in your favor, and most comparison pages haven't caught up. On Netlify's current credit-based plans, forms are free and unlimited, according to Netlify's own documentation.</p>

<p>The plans themselves run Free at $0, Personal at $9 a month, and Pro at $20 a month with unlimited members; the old per-level forms pricing now applies only to legacy accounts.</p>

<p>The limitation is the flip side of the convenience: it only exists inside Netlify. Move your hosting and the forms don't move with you.</p>

<h2>6. Jotform, best for template depth + HTML embed flexibility</h2>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/jotform_52dafaf96f.png" alt="Jotform homepage hero screenshot" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" loading="lazy" /></p>

<p>Jotform comes at HTML sites from the hosted side, like Formester, but with scale as the pitch: 20,000+ templates, an embed that works anywhere you can paste a snippet, and a custom-HTML question type for injecting your own markup inside a form.</p>

<p>The free plan covers 5 forms and 100 submissions a month. On monthly billing, Bronze costs $39 (1,000 submissions), Silver $49 (2,500), and Gold $129 (10,000, and the tier where HIPAA features become available).</p>

<p>The prices Jotform advertises are the annual-billing rates, which run lower: $34, $39, and $99.</p>

<p>The depth cuts both ways. Whatever form you need probably has a template, and whatever setting you need is probably three menus deep. Budget some onboarding time.</p>

<h2>Common questions about HTML form builders</h2>

<details><summary>What is an HTML form builder?</summary><div>An HTML form builder is a tool that gives your HTML page a working form without you building the backend that receives the submissions. They come in two shapes: hosted builders (Formester, Jotform, Google Forms), where you design the form visually and embed it with a snippet, and form endpoints (Formspree, Basin, Getform, Netlify Forms), where you write the markup yourself and point the form's action attribute at a URL that stores what comes in.</div></details>
<details><summary>What is the best free HTML form builder?</summary><div>The best free HTML form builder depends on which camp you're in. Hosted: Formester, with unlimited forms and responses on its free plan. Endpoint: Getform's 100 free submissions a month is the largest allowance, and Netlify Forms is free and unlimited if your site already runs on Netlify's current plans. Formspree and Basin both cap free use at 50 submissions a month.</div></details>
<details><summary>Do I need a server for HTML forms?</summary><div>No, you don't need a server for HTML forms if you use any tool in this list. Endpoints exist precisely to replace the server: your HTML posts to their URL and they handle storage and notifications. Hosted builders skip the question entirely by hosting the form for you.</div></details>
<details><summary>Can I use HTML forms with Astro, Hugo, or Jekyll?</summary><div>Yes, you can use HTML forms with Astro, Hugo, or Jekyll. A static site can POST to Formspree, Basin, or Getform like any other HTML page, or embed a hosted form from Formester, Jotform, or Google Forms with a snippet.</div></details>
<details><summary>Which HTML form builder has the best spam protection?</summary><div>The best spam protection among these tools comes from layering. Formester and Basin ship honeypots with rate limiting and keyword blocklists on their free tiers; Formspree and Getform pair honeypots with reCAPTCHA integration; Netlify Forms filters spam within its own hosting.</div></details>
<details><summary>Can HTML form endpoints handle file uploads?</summary><div>Yes, HTML form endpoints can handle file uploads, and Getform is the strongest fit because it meters storage alongside submissions: 1 GB on Pro and 10 GB on Volume. Basin supports uploads on paid plans, and Formspree on its upper tiers. The hosted builders, Formester and Jotform, include file-upload fields as standard.</div></details>
<details><summary>What's the difference between Formspree and Formester?</summary><div>The difference between Formspree and Formester is who writes the HTML. With Formspree, you write the markup and POST it to their URL. With Formester, you design the form in a visual editor and embed the result with a snippet. Both end with a working form on an HTML site; they start from opposite ends.</div></details>
<details><summary>Is Google Forms a proper HTML form builder?</summary><div>Google Forms is not a proper HTML form builder in the strict sense, because you never touch the markup; it embeds as an iframe you can't customize. It earns its place on this list because that iframe drops onto any HTML page and costs nothing.</div></details>
<details><summary>Do any of these support GDPR compliance?</summary><div>Yes, all of these tools support GDPR-compliant data handling. Formester additionally states UK GDPR and CCPA compliance as its baseline.</div></details>
