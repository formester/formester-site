<p style="font-size: inherit;">Google has no standalone poll tool, so a Google poll is a Google Form with one Multiple choice question. Open a blank form, type the question and its options, switch on Required and Limit to 1 response, then publish and send the link. Votes show up as a live chart in the Responses tab. It is free and takes about two minutes.</p>

<p>The five steps below build that poll, then cover the two settings that decide whether it is fair, how voters see the results and how to share it to a phone or a group chat.</p>

<div class="tldr">
<p><strong>Quick answer</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The poll:</strong> blank form, one Multiple choice question for a single vote or Checkboxes for several, options typed one per line, Required on.</li>
<li><strong>One vote per person:</strong> Limit to 1 response in Settings, which also forces voters to sign in to Google. Leave it off for an open, anonymous poll and accept repeat votes.</li>
<li><strong>Results:</strong> you see a live chart in Responses. Voters only see it if you switch on View results summary, which shows every chart to every voter.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 id="tool">Does Google have a poll tool?</h2>

<p>Not as a product you can open. Google Surveys, the paid market-research tool, shut down in 2022. There is no app called Google Polls.</p>

<p>What Google has are polls inside other things: a poll panel for hosts during a Google Meet call, community polls on a YouTube channel, and Google Forms, which is the one that works for a link you can send anywhere.</p>

<p>So when someone says "make a Google poll", they mean a Google Form with one question. Everything below is that.</p>

<h2 id="build">How do you make a poll on Google Forms?</h2>

<ol class="steps">
<li>
<h3>Open a blank form and name it</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/e9cbee0799e48bb2_gforms-poll-step1-blank-title.gif" alt="Opening a blank form at forms.google.com and typing Team offsite: which Friday? as the title with a one-line description" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Go to <a href="https://forms.google.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener">forms.google.com</a> and click <strong>Blank form</strong>. Type the poll question as the title, so it shows in the browser tab and in the link preview, and use the description for the deadline and how many people should vote.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Add the question and the options</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/fa349503ad72adee_gforms-poll-step2-question-options.gif" alt="Typing the poll question into the first Google Forms question, confirming the type is Multiple choice and adding three date options" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>The first question is already there. Type the question, check the type says <strong>Multiple choice</strong> for one vote per person, and type each option, pressing Enter to add the next. Switch to <strong>Checkboxes</strong> if people may pick more than one. Delete the extra "Other" option unless you want write-ins.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Shuffle the options and make it required</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/b873ea5d8a6bf39e_gforms-poll-step3-shuffle-required.gif" alt="Opening the three-dot menu on the poll question in Google Forms, ticking Shuffle option order and switching Required on" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Switch <strong>Required</strong> on so a blank vote cannot be submitted. From the question's three-dot menu tick <strong>Shuffle option order</strong>, which shows the options in a different order to each voter and stops the first option winning because it was first.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Set one vote per person and what voters see</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/7ba8656eaa1a7556_gforms-poll-step4-one-vote-results-summary.gif" alt="The Google Forms Settings tab with Limit to 1 response switched on under Responses and View results summary switched on under Presentation" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>In <strong>Settings</strong>, switch on <strong>Limit to 1 response</strong>. It stops repeat votes, and it also requires every voter to sign in to a Google account, which is the trade. Under <strong>Presentation</strong>, <strong>View results summary</strong> lets voters see the chart after they vote. Leave it off if the result should stay with you until the poll closes.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Publish, copy the link and set a close date</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/49bc902b6fbfde4b_gforms-poll-step5-publish-copy-link.gif" alt="Clicking Publish on the Google Forms poll, confirming who can respond, then copying the shortened responder link" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Click <strong>Publish</strong>, check who can respond and copy the responder link with Shorten URL ticked. Then click the <strong>Published</strong> button again and choose <strong>Set close date or response limit</strong> so the poll stops taking votes on the deadline rather than when you remember to close it.</p>
</li>
</ol>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/4fdb9de13ef33321_gforms-poll-close-date.gif" alt="Opening the Published button menu in Google Forms, choosing Set close date or response limit and picking a date and time" loading="lazy"></p>

<h2 id="types">Which question type for which poll?</h2>

<div class="table-wrap">
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Poll</th><th>Google Forms type</th><th>What the chart shows</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Pick one (yes or no, a date, a winner)</td><td>Multiple choice</td><td>A pie chart of votes</td></tr>
<tr><td>Pick all you like</td><td>Checkboxes</td><td>A bar per option</td></tr>
<tr><td>Pick one from a long list</td><td>Dropdown</td><td>A pie chart</td></tr>
<tr><td>Rate something</td><td>Linear scale or Rating</td><td>A bar per level, no average</td></tr>
<tr><td>Rank the options</td><td>Multiple choice grid, one row per option</td><td>A grid of counts, no ranked-choice tally</td></tr>
<tr><td>Vote on pictures</td><td>Multiple choice with an image on each option</td><td>A pie chart</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

<p>The ranked version needs a hand count or a spreadsheet, because Google Forms does not run the elimination rounds. <a href="/blog/how-to-make-a-google-form-for-rank-choice-voting/">Ranked choice voting in Google Forms</a> covers the grid setup and the Sheets formula.</p>

<h2 id="anonymous">How do you make a Google poll anonymous?</h2>

<p>Anonymous and one-vote-per-person pull in opposite directions in Google Forms, because the only way it recognises a repeat voter is a Google sign-in. Pick the combination that matches what the poll is for.</p>

<div class="table-wrap">
<table>
<thead><tr><th>You want</th><th>Collect email addresses</th><th>Limit to 1 response</th><th>What you get</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Fully anonymous, open to anyone</td><td>Do not collect</td><td>Off</td><td>No sign-in, no names, repeat votes possible</td></tr>
<tr><td>Anonymous, one vote each</td><td>Do not collect</td><td>On</td><td>Sign-in required, but no address is stored</td></tr>
<tr><td>Named vote</td><td>Verified</td><td>On</td><td>Sign-in required, each vote tied to an address</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

<p>Say which one it is in the description. People vote differently when they think their name is attached, and "Limit to 1 response" on its own does not record who voted. <a href="/blog/how-to-make-anonymous-google-form-or-survey/">Making a Google Form anonymous</a> goes through the settings in detail.</p>

<h2 id="share">How do you share a Google poll?</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>A link:</strong> the responder link from Publish works in Slack, Teams, WhatsApp, a group text or an email. Shorten URL keeps it tidy in a chat.</li>
<li><strong>A QR code:</strong> for a meeting room screen or a printed notice, make one from the same link. <a href="/blog/how-to-make-a-qr-code-for-a-google-form/">QR codes for Google Forms</a> covers it.</li>
<li><strong>Embedded on a page:</strong> the Share panel has an Embed HTML tab for a site or intranet page. <a href="/blog/how-to-share-google-form/">Sharing a Google Form</a> covers email and embedding.</li>
<li><strong>On a phone:</strong> voters open the link in any browser. There is no Google Forms app to install, and the poll works the same on iPhone and Android. <a href="/blog/google-forms-app/">The Google Forms app</a> explains what that means for you as the owner.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="results">How do you see the results?</h2>

<p>Open the form and click the <strong>Responses</strong> tab. <strong>Summary</strong> shows one chart per question that updates as votes arrive, which for a one-question poll is the whole result. <strong>Individual</strong> shows one vote at a time. <strong>Link to Sheets</strong> gives you every vote as a row with a timestamp, which is how you see when the votes came in.</p>

<p>Voters see the same summary chart after submitting only if View results summary is on. There is no way to show a live result to someone before they vote, or to show one chart and hide another, so a two-question poll with a name field shows the names too.</p>

<h2 id="limits">What Google Forms cannot do for a poll</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Stop repeat votes without a sign-in:</strong> Limit to 1 response is the only guard, and it requires a Google account.</li>
<li><strong>Show voters a live result before they vote:</strong> the summary appears after submitting, or not at all.</li>
<li><strong>Cap one option:</strong> the response limit closes the whole poll. A "first ten get the slot" poll needs a different tool or the <a href="/blog/how-to-create-signup-sheets-in-google-forms/">signup sheet workaround</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Count a ranked vote:</strong> the grid collects ranks, and the tally happens in Sheets.</li>
<li><strong>Look like yours:</strong> a header image and a theme colour, with the Google Forms footer under the vote button.</li>
</ul>
