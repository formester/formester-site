![Cover art for a guide to creating an order form in Google Forms](https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/968f5cdd237f60ce_how-to-create-an-order-form-in-google-form-cover.png)

<p style="font-size: inherit;">To create an order form in Google Forms, open the Order Form template from the template gallery, replace its product question with your own items and prices, add a Short answer quantity question with number validation, and publish. Orders arrive in the Responses tab and in a linked Google Sheet.</p>

<p>What Google Forms cannot do is take the payment, so the money is a link you send afterwards or a form builder with checkout.</p>

<p>The seven steps below build the form from the template, with the question types that keep orders clean, the settings that stop the order window at a date, and the honest answer on payment.</p>

<div class="tldr">
<p><strong>Quick answer</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Start from the template:</strong> the gallery's Order Form already has a product question, a colour question, a product options field and a contact section. Rename and swap the items.</li>
<li><strong>Quantity and variants:</strong> quantity is a Short answer with Response validation set to a number greater than 0. Sizes or flavours are Checkboxes with the price in the option text, or their own section reached with Go to section based on answer.</li>
<li><strong>Payment:</strong> there is no payment field. Send a payment link in the confirmation, put a QR code in it, or use a form builder such as Formester that has Stripe and PayPal on the form.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 id="build">How do you make an order form in Google Forms?</h2>

<ol class="steps">
<li>
<h3>Open the Order Form template</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/4abb47dbd0334cfd_gforms-order-step1-template.gif" alt="The Google Forms template gallery with Order Form in the Work row, opening to an Order Request form with a new or existing customer question, an item question and a contact section" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Go to forms.google.com, open the <strong>Template gallery</strong> and pick <strong>Order Form</strong> from the Work row. It opens as Order Request with a customer question, an item question, a colour question, a Product options paragraph and a Contact info section for name, phone and email. Change the title and the description, which is where pickup days and cut-off times belong.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Replace the product question with your items and prices</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/4fd17e0dcec2bb3d_gforms-order-step2-checkboxes-type.gif" alt="Changing the item question to Checkboxes in Google Forms and typing three options with prices: Sourdough loaf $6, Croissants box of 4 $10, Birthday cake 8 inch $35" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>The template asks for the item as a Short answer, which gives you fifty spellings of the same loaf. Change the type to <strong>Checkboxes</strong> if customers can order several items, or <strong>Multiple choice</strong> if it is one, and type each item with its price as an option. Switch <strong>Required</strong> on.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Add a quantity question with number validation</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/327c773c6ecaeee2_gforms-order-step3-quantity-validation.gif" alt="Adding a Quantity Short answer question in Google Forms, opening Response validation from the three-dot menu and setting Number, Greater than, 0 with a custom error message" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Add a <strong>Short answer</strong> question called Quantity. From its three-dot menu choose <strong>Response validation</strong>, then Number, Greater than, 0, and type an error message such as "Enter a whole number". For a fixed range, a <strong>Dropdown</strong> with 1 to 10 removes typos entirely. One quantity question per item if quantities differ.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Handle sizes, flavours and other variants</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/6d4907a709eeb20c_gforms-order-step2b-product-options.gif" alt="The Product options paragraph question in the Google Forms order template, with its description asking customers to choose size and number per colour" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>The template's Product options field is a Paragraph, which means customers type their sizes in free text. For a few variants, add them to the option text instead: "Birthday cake, 8 inch, $35" and "Birthday cake, 10 inch, $48".</p>
<p>For many, make one section per product and use <strong>Go to section based on answer</strong> on the item question, so a cake order sees cake questions only. <a href="/blog/how-to-add-conditional-questions-in-google-forms/">Conditional questions in Google Forms</a> covers the branching.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Check the contact and delivery fields</h3>
<p>The Contact info section already has name, phone number, email and preferred contact method. Make name and one contact method required. Add a <strong>Date</strong> question for the pickup or delivery date and a <strong>Multiple choice</strong> for pickup or delivery, and set the email question's validation to Text, Email address so a typo cannot lose the order.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Collect email addresses and send a copy</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/a588eaca83536016_gforms-order-step4-collect-email-copy.gif" alt="The Settings tab in Google Forms with Collect email addresses set to Verified and Send responders a copy of their response switched to Always" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>In <strong>Settings</strong>, set <strong>Collect email addresses</strong> to Verified or Responder input, then switch <strong>Send responders a copy of their response</strong> to Always. That copy is the only confirmation Google Forms sends, and it is where your payment instructions go, so write them into the confirmation message under Presentation as well.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Publish, share and link to Sheets</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/da0b7aec12edb519_gforms-order-step5-publish-link-sheets.gif" alt="Publishing the order form in Google Forms, copying the responder link, then opening the Responses tab and clicking Link to Sheets" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Click <strong>Publish</strong>, confirm who can respond and copy the responder link. In the <strong>Responses</strong> tab click <strong>Link to Sheets</strong> so every order becomes a timestamped row you can sort by pickup date. When the order window ends, <strong>Set close date or response limit</strong> under the Published button closes it on a date or at a number of orders.</p>
</li>
</ol>

<p>Two habits keep the Sheet readable. Never let a customer type a product name, because you will be matching "sourdough", "Sourdough loaf" and "the big bread" by hand. And keep prices in the option text, because Google Forms will not show a running total, so the option is the only place the customer sees the cost.</p>

<h2 id="payment">How do customers pay on a Google Forms order form?</h2>

<p>They do not, at least not inside the form. Google Forms has no payment field and no add-on from Google that takes cards. Every working setup collects the order in Forms and the money somewhere else. There are three honest routes.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>A payment link after they submit:</strong> put a Stripe Payment Link, PayPal.Me or bank details in the confirmation message and in the email copy. You reconcile the payment against the order row by name.</li>
<li><strong>A QR code in the confirmation:</strong> the same link as an image, useful for market stalls and pickup counters where people pay from a phone.</li>
<li><strong>A checkout add-on:</strong> Marketplace add-ons such as Payable and neartail bolt a checkout onto a Google Form, for a fee on top of the processor's rate.</li>
</ul>

<p>All three are covered, with the fees, in <a href="/blog/how-to-add-payment-option-in-google-forms/">how to add a payment option in Google Forms</a>. The short version is that for a bake sale the link works, and for anything you sell every week the reconciliation becomes the job.</p>

<h2 id="limits">What Google Forms cannot do for orders</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Take payment:</strong> no card field, no PayPal button, no receipt.</li>
<li><strong>Show a total:</strong> there is no calculation, so the customer never sees what the order costs before submitting.</li>
<li><strong>Track stock:</strong> the form takes the hundredth cake order after the tenth one sold out. The response limit closes the whole form, not one option.</li>
<li><strong>Handle variants at scale:</strong> five products with three sizes each is fifteen options or five sections, maintained by hand.</li>
<li><strong>Send a real order confirmation:</strong> the copy of responses is a plain list of answers, with no order number, no total and no payment status.</li>
<li><strong>Carry your brand:</strong> a header image and a theme colour, with Google's footer on every page.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="template">The Google Forms order form template</h2>

<p>The gallery's Order Form template is a customer request form rather than a shop. It asks whether the customer is new or existing, what item they want as a Short answer, which colours as Checkboxes, and product options as a Paragraph, then a Contact info section with name, phone, email, preferred contact method and comments.</p>

<p>Use it for the structure and the contact section, and replace the item, colour and options questions with the Checkboxes and quantity pattern from the steps above.</p>

<p>Once your version is right, keep it as your own template: in Drive, right-click the form and choose <strong>Make a copy</strong> for each new order window, so the questions and settings carry over and the responses start from zero.</p>
