## What Is RSVP?
RSVP means "**please respond**" in French. It’s just a way to ask people if they are coming to your event or not. This helps you get ready without any guesswork. 

Some RSVP forms are simple. They only ask **Yes or No**. Others ask more questions. They may ask if you are bringing someone, what food you want, or if you are not sure yet. 

You can also add space for notes or anything else you need to know before the event.

### Why RSVP Matters

- **Accurate headcounts:** You need to know how many people will show up so you can plan food, seats, and space.

- **Saves time, money, and effort:** When you know the exact number of guests, you won’t waste money on extras or run short.

- **Helps plan food, seating, materials:** You’ll know how much food to order, how many chairs to set up, and what materials to prepare.

- **Reduces event-day surprises:** Last-minute surprises can ruin events. RSVP helps you stay in control and well-prepared.

## Types of RSVP Forms

### Basic RSVP
This is the most common type. It asks guests if they’re attending or not. It's quick and perfect for simple events where you just need a clear Yes or No to plan ahead.

### RSVP with additional questions
Use this to collect more details like food preferences, allergies, or travel needs. It helps you personalize the event experience for every guest..

### RSVP with guest count
Ask how many people are coming with the invitee. This is great for family events or weddings where guests might bring a +1 or kids along.

### RSVP with deadlines
Set a last date to respond. It adds urgency and helps you close your list in time to book vendors, food, and other services without stress.

### RSVP with payment options
For paid events, add payment links directly in the form. It makes the process easy for guests and helps you track who paid and who didn’t.

### Digital RSVP forms
Online forms are easy to share, fast to fill, and simple to track. You get real-time responses and avoid manual work with spreadsheets or paper invites.

### Paper RSVP forms
These are printed cards sent by mail. While they feel personal, they take more time, are easy to lose, and hard to track compared to digital tools like Formester.

## What to Include in an RSVP Form

### Event name, date, location
Always start with the basics. Clearly mention the name of the event, the date it’s happening, and the full location. This helps people know exactly what they are responding to.

### Attendee name
Ask for the guest’s full name. It helps you track responses and create personalized experiences like name tags or seating charts.

### Email or phone
Collect at least one contact detail, either an email or phone number. This makes it easy to send updates, reminders, or last-minute changes.

### Response options
Give simple choices like Yes, No, or Maybe. You can also allow them to select how many guests they are bringing. Keep it short and easy to answer.

### Additional questions
Depending on the event, you might want to ask for food choices, allergies, or special needs. You can also add an open field for notes or comments.

### Confirmation message or follow-up
After someone submits the form, show a thank-you message or send an email confirmation. This reassures the guest that we recorded their response.

### Best Practices for Sending RSVP Invitations

- Send invites at least 3-4 weeks before the event

- Send one or two reminders, one week and one day before the event

- Ensure the form is mobile-friendly for easy access

- Keep your invitation copy short and clear
