<h2>Which free form builder should you choose?</h2>

<p>Free form builders fall into three groups, and the word free means something different in each. Some place no meaningful cap at all. Some allow a usable monthly allowance. Some offer a trial dressed as a plan.</p>

<p>Knowing which group a tool belongs to matters more than comparing features, because the group decides whether you will still be using it in six months or shopping again. The 17 tools below are sorted on exactly that.</p>

<div class="tldr">
<p><strong>The 60-second TL;DR</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Best genuinely unlimited free plan:</strong> Formester, unlimited forms and responses</li>
<li><strong>Best free plan for unlimited submissions:</strong> Tally, unlimited forms and submissions</li>
<li><strong>Best free option in Google Workspace:</strong> Google Forms</li>
<li><strong>Best free option in Microsoft 365:</strong> Microsoft Forms, 200 responses per form</li>
<li><strong>Best free plan with real logic:</strong> Cognito Forms, 100 entries/mo</li>
<li><strong>Best free plan for templates:</strong> Jotform, 5 forms and 100 submissions/mo</li>
<li><strong>Best free plan with unlimited seats:</strong> Fillout, 1,000 responses/mo</li>
<li><strong>Best free plan for marketing:</strong> HubSpot Forms, unlimited submissions</li>
</ul>
</div>

<p>Paid plans add capability rather than volume, so upgrading is a decision about conditional logic at $13 or a custom domain at $49, never about running out of room.</p>

<p>There is no paid Forms plan. It comes with Google Workspace, where Business Starter is $7 per user a month on an annual basis.</p>

<h2>How to pick the right free form builder</h2>

<p>Work out first whether your volume is predictable. If it is not, only the genuinely uncapped plans will do, which means Formester, Tally, or Google Forms. Everything else will interrupt you at some point.</p>

<p>If volume is predictable and modest, the choice opens up considerably. Cognito Forms gives you real logic free, Fillout gives you unlimited seats, and Jotform gives you the biggest template library.</p>

<p>Then check what your free plan needs to do beyond collecting. Payments free means Formester or Cognito Forms. A CRM behind the form means HubSpot. An email list means MailerLite.</p>

<h2>Common questions about free form builders</h2>

<details><summary>What is the best free form builder?</summary><div>The best free form builder is Formester if you want no caps at all, since its free plan includes unlimited forms, responses and fields along with AI generation and payments. Tally and Google Forms also offer effectively unlimited free submissions.</div></details>

<details><summary>Which free form builders have no submission limit?</summary><div>Three tools here place no meaningful cap on free submissions: Formester, which allows unlimited forms and responses, Tally, which allows unlimited forms and submissions, and Google Forms, which has no practical limit. HubSpot Forms also allows unlimited submissions with its branding.</div></details>

<details><summary>Can I collect payments on a free form?</summary><div>Yes, two tools include payment collection on their free plans. Formester supports Stripe and PayPal free, and Cognito Forms includes native payment collection on its free Individual plan, capped at 100 entries a month.</div></details>

<details><summary>Do free form builders show their own branding?</summary><div>Most do. Formester, Tally, Jotform, Wufoo, Fillout, HubSpot and MailerLite all display vendor branding on free forms. Removing it generally requires the first paid tier, which ranges from $13 to $39 a month across this list.</div></details>

<details><summary>Which free form builder has conditional logic?</summary><div>Cognito Forms offers the most capable free logic, including calculations and repeating sections. Tally and forms.app both include conditional logic free. Formester places conditional logic on its Personal plan at $13 a month.</div></details>

<details><summary>Is a free form builder enough for a business?</summary><div>A free form builder is enough for many businesses, particularly with an uncapped plan like Formester, Tally, or Google Forms. Businesses usually upgrade for branding removal, a custom domain, or team seats rather than because they run out of submissions.</div></details>

<details><summary>What is the catch with free form builders?</summary><div>The catch is usually one of three things: a monthly submission cap, vendor branding on the form, or a feature you need sitting on a paid tier. Reading which of those applies matters more than comparing free-plan feature lists.</div></details>

<details><summary>Which free form builder works best for teams?</summary><div>Fillout works best for teams on a free plan because it allows unlimited seats, which most free tiers do not. Google Forms and Microsoft Forms also support multiple editors through Workspace and Microsoft 365 permissions.</div></details>
