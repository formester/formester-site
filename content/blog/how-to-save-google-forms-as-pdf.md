<p>Google Forms has no Download as PDF button, so every Google Form to PDF route goes through a print dialog or an extra tool. Which one you need depends on what should be in the file: the blank questions, one person's answers, every response, or a fresh PDF each time someone submits.</p>

<div class="tldr">
<p>Google Form to PDF: quick answer</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Blank form:</strong> in the form editor, open the three-dot menu, choose <strong>Print</strong>, then <strong>Save as PDF</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>One response:</strong> open <strong>Responses</strong>, then <strong>Individual</strong>, and click the print icon.</li>
<li><strong>All responses:</strong> open the <strong>Responses</strong> three-dot menu and choose <strong>Print all responses</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Responses as a table:</strong> link the form to Google Sheets, then choose <strong>File</strong>, <strong>Download</strong>, <strong>PDF</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>A PDF for every new response:</strong> Google Forms can't do this alone. Use an add-on, a Zapier workflow, or a form builder that creates PDFs itself.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 id="blank-form">1. How to save a blank Google Form as a PDF</h2>

<p>Use this when you want a paper version to hand out, or a record of the questions for your files.</p>

<ol class="steps">
<li><h3>Open the menu</h3><p>Open the form in the editor and click the three-dot menu at the top right.</p><img src="/images/blog/google-forms-to-pdf/print-blank-form.webp" alt="The Google Forms editor three-dot menu open, with Print listed below Embed HTML" width="722" height="552" loading="lazy" /></li>
<li><h3>Choose Print</h3><p>Click <strong>Print</strong>. Google Forms opens your browser's print dialog.</p></li>
<li><h3>Save as PDF</h3><p>Set the destination to <strong>Save as PDF</strong> and click <strong>Save</strong>.</p></li>
</ol>

<h2 id="one-response">2. How to save one Google Form response as a PDF</h2>

<p>Use this to send someone a copy of what they submitted, or to file a single application.</p>

<ol class="steps">
<li><h3>Open the Individual view</h3><p>Open the <strong>Responses</strong> tab, then click <strong>Individual</strong>.</p></li>
<li><h3>Find the response</h3><p>Use the arrows or the dropdown to reach the response you want.</p></li>
<li><h3>Print it</h3><p>Click the print icon next to the arrows, then choose <strong>Save as PDF</strong>.</p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/65c44ea4b8cdae50_03-print-one-response.jpg" alt="The Google Forms Individual view showing response 1 of 3, with the print icon on the right" width="791" height="215" loading="lazy" /></li>
</ol>

<div class="note"><p>On forms with more than 10,000 responses, Google hides the Individual view, so this method stops working. See <a href="https://support.google.com/docs/answer/139706" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google's help on managing responses</a>.</p></div>

<h2 id="all-responses">3. How to save all Google Form responses as a PDF</h2>

<p>Use this when you want every answer in a single file, for an archive or an audit.</p>

<ol class="steps">
<li><h3>Open the Responses menu</h3><p>Open the <strong>Responses</strong> tab and click its three-dot menu.</p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/5ae83f09b41875dc_02-print-all-responses.jpg" alt="The Google Forms Responses three-dot menu open, showing Print all responses" width="791" height="367" loading="lazy" /></li>
<li><h3>Print all responses</h3><p>Choose <strong>Print all responses</strong>.</p></li>
<li><h3>Save as PDF</h3><p>Set the destination to <strong>Save as PDF</strong> and save the file.</p></li>
</ol>

<p>Every response goes into one long document, and you can't pick which ones are included. If you want the charts instead, stay on the <strong>Summary</strong> view of the Responses tab and print the page with Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on a Mac). The <strong>Question</strong> view works the same way for the answers to a single question. Google stops showing the Summary view above 50,000 responses.</p>

<img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/e20c3a1159318b8e_04-summary-tab.jpg" alt="The Google Forms Summary view with a chart for each question, ready to print as a PDF" width="791" height="454" loading="lazy" />

<h2 id="google-sheets">4. How to download Google Form responses as a PDF from Google Sheets</h2>

<p>Use this when you want the answers as a table, with columns you can trim first. It's the cleanest option for a long list of sign-ups or orders.</p>

<ol class="steps">
<li><h3>Link a spreadsheet</h3><p>In the <strong>Responses</strong> tab, click <strong>Link to Sheets</strong> and create a new spreadsheet or choose an existing one.</p></li>
<li><h3>Hide what you don't need</h3><p>Hide columns such as the timestamp or email so the table fits the page.</p></li>
<li><h3>Download as PDF</h3><p>Click <strong>File</strong>, <strong>Download</strong>, then <strong>PDF</strong>. Choose landscape and <strong>Fit to width</strong> for wide forms, then click <strong>Export</strong>.</p></li>
</ol>

<p>You get one row per response, not one page per person. If you only need the data for Excel, <strong>Download responses (.csv)</strong> in the Responses menu is quicker. Our guide to <a href="/blog/google-forms-to-google-sheets/">sending Google Forms responses to Google Sheets</a> covers the linking options.</p>

<h2 id="automatic">5. How to create a PDF for every new Google Form response</h2>

<p>Every method above is manual. If each submission needs its own document, like a receipt, certificate or signed application, you need a PDF created automatically. Google Forms can't do that alone, so you add a tool.</p>

<h3>Use a Google Workspace add-on</h3>

<p>These add-ons fill a Google Docs template with each response and save it as a PDF:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Form Publisher</strong> creates a document every time the form is submitted, as a PDF or another format. Our <a href="/blog/form-publisher-for-google-forms/">Form Publisher guide</a> covers the setup.</li>
<li><strong>Autocrat</strong> merges each row of the response sheet into a template, runs on form submit, and can email the PDF.</li>
<li><strong>Document Studio</strong> generates documents such as invoices and certificates from Forms and Sheets.</li>
<li><strong>Portant</strong> connects a form to a Docs or Slides template and creates a new PDF each time someone submits.</li>
</ul>

<p>Each one installs from the Google Workspace Marketplace. Check its listing for pricing and monthly limits before you depend on it.</p>

<h3>Use Zapier</h3>

<p>Zapier can start a workflow on each <strong>New Form Response</strong> and run the Google Docs action <strong>Create Document From Template</strong>, which fills the placeholders in your template and can export a PDF. It suits teams that also need the response sent on to another app, like a CRM.</p>

<h2 id="why-it-looks-different">Why your Google Form PDF looks different from the form</h2>

<p>A Google Forms PDF is a printout of a web page, not an exported document. That causes a few common problems:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Colors and images change.</strong> Theme colors and the header image can print lighter or in a different place.</li>
<li><strong>Page breaks split questions.</strong> A long list of options can run onto the next page.</li>
<li><strong>No branding.</strong> You can't add your own logo, header or footer.</li>
</ul>

<p>Print from a computer for the most control. In Chrome, open <strong>More settings</strong> in the print dialog and turn on <strong>Background graphics</strong> to keep more of the color.</p>

<h2 id="respondent-copy">Can respondents get a PDF of their answers?</h2>

<p>Not as a PDF. Google Forms can email respondents a copy of their answers: in <strong>Settings</strong>, turn on email collection, then set <strong>Send responders a copy of their response</strong> to <strong>Always</strong> or <strong>When requested</strong>. The copy arrives as an email, and <a href="https://support.google.com/docs/answer/139706" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google notes</a> it can be caught by spam filters. To send a PDF instead, use one of the tools in <a href="#automatic">method 5</a>. Our guide on <a href="/blog/google-form-send-copy-of-response/">sending respondents a copy of their response</a> has the full setup.</p>
