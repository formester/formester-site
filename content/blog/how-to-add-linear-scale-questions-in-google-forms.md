One popular question type in Google Forms is the linear scale question. It lets you ask people to rate something on a number scale, like from 1 to 5. 

This is perfect for <strong><a href="/blog/best-customer-feedback-collection-tools/">gathering quick feedback</a></strong>, checking satisfaction levels, or measuring opinions.

In this guide, you’ll learn what <strong>linear scale questions</strong> are, when to use them, how to add them in Google Forms, and how to do it more easily using <strong>Formester– a <a href="/google-forms-alternative/">free Google Forms alternative</a></strong>.

## What Is a Linear Scale Question?
A linear scale question allows someone to answer using a number range.

 For example, if you ask, “How happy are you with our service?”, the person can choose a number between 1 (not happy) and 5 (very happy).

You can think of it like a mini rating system. It’s simple for people to understand, and it gives you quick data you can measure.

### When Should You Use a Linear Scale?
Use a linear scale when you want a quick opinion. It works well for:

- Customer feedback

- Course or event ratings

- Product satisfaction

- Measuring how likely someone is to do something (like recommend your product)

Don’t use it for detailed answers. If you want reasons or explanations, combine it with a short text question.

### How to Add a Linear Scale Question in Google Forms
Follow these steps below to add a linear scale question:

<strong>1. Go to Google Forms:</strong> 
Visit forms.google.com and sign in with your Google account.

<strong>2. Create a new form:</strong> 
Click on the blank form option to start fresh.

<strong>3. Add a question:</strong> 
Click the plus (+) button on the right to add a new question.

<strong>4. Change the question type:</strong> 
Click on the dropdown menu that says "Multiple choice" and select "Linear scale".

<strong>5. Set the range:</strong> 
Choose your starting number (usually 1) and your ending number (like 5 or 10).

<strong>6. Label the ends of the scale:</strong> 
Add a label for the lowest number (example: "Very Bad") and the highest number (example: "Excellent").

<strong>7. Write your question:</strong> 
For example: "How satisfied are you with our service?"

<strong> 8. Make it required (optional):</strong> 
Click the “Required” toggle if you want people to answer before submitting.

That’s it. Your linear scale question is ready.

### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Don’t forget to label your scale clearly

- Don’t switch scales too often in one form

- Don’t ask emotional or vague questions without context

- Keep it simple and consistent.

<h2 class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__h2" id="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq-h2">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<p class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__intro">Common questions about linear scale questions in Google Forms and when to use them.</p>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__list">

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>How do I add a linear scale question in Google Forms?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Open your form, click the plus icon to add a new question, click the question-type dropdown, select Linear scale, set the range (default 1-5), and add optional labels for the low and high ends. The question is ready to use.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>What is a linear scale question?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">A linear scale question asks respondents to rate something on a numeric scale (typically 1-5 or 1-10) where the low number means one thing (e.g., poor) and the high number means another (e.g., excellent).</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>What scale should I use for a linear scale question?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">1-5 is the most common and easiest for respondents. 1-10 gives more granularity but increases cognitive load. Use 1-5 for quick feedback (NPS uses 0-10; CSAT uses 1-5).</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>Can I customize the labels on a Google Forms linear scale?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Yes. The Linear scale question type lets you label the low and high endpoints (e.g., Not at all likely / Extremely likely). You cannot label the middle values in Google Forms; for that, use a custom rating scale field in Formester.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>What's the difference between a linear scale and a rating scale?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">A linear scale uses numbers (1-5, 1-10). A rating scale typically uses stars, smiley faces, or thumbs. Linear scale is better for analysis (numeric data); rating scale is better for emotional response (consumer feedback).</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>Can I require a linear scale question in Google Forms?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Yes. Toggle Required at the bottom of the question. Respondents must select a value before submitting the form.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>Can a Google Forms linear scale start at 0?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Yes. Google Forms supports starting the scale at 0 or 1, ending at any value up to 10. Common scales: 0-10 (NPS), 1-5 (Likert), 1-10 (general rating).</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>How do I create an NPS question in Google Forms?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Use a linear scale with range 0-10, label the low end as Not at all likely and the high end as Extremely likely, and ask the question How likely is it that you would recommend us to a friend or colleague?</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>Can I add labels for every value on a linear scale?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Not in Google Forms (only endpoints are labeled). For per-value labels, use a multiple-choice question with descriptive options, or use Formester's custom rating-scale field.</div>
</details>

<details class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__item">
<summary>Is there a better tool than Google Forms for advanced rating scales?</summary>
<div class="fmstr-cmp-tmpl-faq__answer">Formester offers customizable rating scales with star, thumb, and slider variants, per-value labels, conditional logic on the answer, and NPS-specific analytics. Worth it when Google Forms hits its rating-scale limits.</div>
</details>
