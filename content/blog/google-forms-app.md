<p style="font-size: inherit;">There is no Google Forms app. Google has never released one for iPhone or Android, and every "Google Forms" app you find in the App Store or Google Play is made by somebody else and works by signing in with your Google account.</p>

<p>The official way to use Forms on a phone is the browser: open forms.google.com in Safari or Chrome, sign in and you get the same editor as on a computer, with one documented gap.</p>

<p>That is fine for most people, and the rest of this page shows how to make the browser behave like an app, what the store apps are actually asking for, and where a phone hits the limits.</p>

<div class="tldr">
<p><strong>Quick answer</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>No official app:</strong> Google's own help page says to open a mobile browser and go to Google Forms. Existing forms are also in the Google Drive app, which opens them in the browser editor.</li>
<li><strong>The store apps:</strong> Forms for Google Docs, Forms+, FormsApp and the one called "Google Forms App" are all third-party. Each signs in with your Google account and reads your forms through that access, free with in-app purchases.</li>
<li><strong>Make the browser the app:</strong> open forms.google.com, then Share and Add to Home Screen on iPhone, or the menu and Add to Home screen in Chrome on Android. It opens full-screen from an icon like anything else on the phone.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 id="app">Is there a Google Forms app?</h2>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/2029e4fe4b78254c_google-play-search-google-forms.gif" alt="Searching Google Play for google forms: the first result is a third-party FormsApp, above Google's own Docs and Sheets apps" loading="lazy"></p>

<p>No. Google publishes phone apps for Docs, Sheets, Slides, Drive, Keep and Calendar. It does not publish one for Forms. Its help page for iPhone, iPad and Android starts with "open a mobile web browser, like Safari" and never mentions installing anything, because there is nothing to install. The confusion comes from the stores.</p>

<p>Search "google forms" in Google Play and the first result is FormsApp, sitting above Google Docs and Google Sheets. Search the App Store and one of the results is literally called "Google Forms App". None of them are from Google, and the search results do not say so until you open the listing and scroll to the disclaimer.</p>

<p>If you already have a Google account and a phone, you have the whole product. Signed out, forms.google.com lands on the Google sign-in page. Signed in, it opens the form editor with the same question types, the same Publish button and the same Responses tab as the desktop.</p>

<h2 id="store">What are the "Google Forms" apps in the App Store and Google Play?</h2>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/99525b026a126fbe_app-store-third-party-forms-app.gif" alt="The App Store listing for Forms for Google Docs, a free third-party app with in-app purchases, with its description expanded" loading="lazy"></p>

<p>They are third-party clients. Each one asks you to sign in with your Google account, and with that permission it reads, edits and creates the forms in your Drive through its own interface. Some are well made, and the ratings are high.</p>

<p>The thing to understand before tapping Install is that you are handing a company you have not heard of the same access to your forms that you have, in exchange for a wrapper around a website that already works on your phone.</p>

<p>Nothing these apps do is impossible in the browser. What they sell is a native-feeling layout, a response list that fits a phone screen and, on the paid tiers, notifications when a response arrives.</p>

<p>If that is worth the account access to you, pick one whose listing carries the disclaimer, check the data safety section, and remove its access at myaccount.google.com under Security when you stop using it. If it is not, the browser route below costs nothing and asks for nothing.</p>

<h2 id="phone">How do you use Google Forms on a phone?</h2>

<p>The same way as on a computer, in a smaller window. Google's help page gives the same steps for iPhone, iPad and Android. They hold in Safari, Chrome and Samsung Internet alike.</p>

<ol class="steps">
<li>
<h3>Open forms.google.com and sign in</h3>
<p>Type <strong>forms.google.com</strong> into the browser, not the search box. Signed out, you land on the Google sign-in page; signed in, you land on the Forms home with your recent forms and the template gallery.</p>

<p>If the phone is signed in to a work account and a personal one, check the account avatar in the top right before you build anything, because a form lives in whichever Drive you were in when you made it.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Start a form, or open one from Drive</h3>
<p>Tap the <strong>Blank form</strong> tile to start from nothing or pick a template. For a form that already exists, the Google Drive app is often quicker: find the form there and tap it, and it opens in the browser editor. Either route lands in the same place.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Build it</h3>
<p>The editor is the desktop editor, so the plus button, the question type dropdown, sections, images and the Settings tab are all there. Two things are different on a phone. Drag-and-drop reordering is fiddly, so use the three-dot menu on a question to move it.</p>

<p>And Google's help page states that the <strong>date and time picker is not currently supported for iPhone and iPad</strong>, so a Date question added from an iPhone should be checked on a computer before you rely on it.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Publish and copy the responder link</h3>
<p>Tap <strong>Publish</strong> in the top right, confirm who can respond, then use the link icon to copy the responder link. Tick <strong>Shorten URL</strong> if you are about to paste it into a message. <a href="/blog/how-to-share-google-form/">Sharing a Google Form</a> covers the other routes, and <a href="/blog/how-to-make-a-qr-code-for-a-google-form/">making a QR code for it</a> is a one-minute job from the same link.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Add it to the home screen</h3>
<p>This is the step that makes the app question go away. On iPhone, with forms.google.com open in Safari, tap the Share button and choose <strong>Add to Home Screen</strong>. In Chrome on Android, open the menu and choose <strong>Add to Home screen</strong>.</p>

<p>You get a Forms icon that opens the site full screen without the address bar and stays signed in. It is not an app in the store sense, and it behaves like one for everything that matters.</p>
</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="answer">How do people answer a Google Form on a phone?</h2>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/dc3ba61180c4da82_qr-step5-phone-check.gif" alt="A Google Form opening on a phone screen from the responder link, with a value tapped on the scale" loading="lazy"></p>

<p>Without any app, and usually without an account. The responder link opens in whatever browser the phone has, the form stacks into a single column, and a submit takes one tap. Most of the responses to most forms arrive this way, which is why it is worth opening Preview on your own phone before you share a link.</p>

<p>Respondents only meet a sign-in wall if you made them. <a href="/blog/how-to-create-a-google-form-with-photo-upload/">A File upload question</a>, the Limit to 1 response setting, or restricting responders to your organisation all require a Google account, and on a phone that means a login screen before the first question. Leave those off for anything public.</p>

<h2 id="limits">What can you not do from a phone?</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Pick a date on an iPhone or iPad:</strong> the date and time picker is unsupported there, in Google's own words. Build date questions on a computer, or use a Short answer with response validation instead.</li>
<li><strong>Work offline:</strong> the editor and the responder page both need a connection to load and to submit. A form opened on a train stops at the first tunnel, with no queued submission to send later.</li>
<li><strong>Use add-ons or Apps Script:</strong> the add-ons menu and the script editor are desktop features. Response limits, PDF generation and notifications from add-ons are set up on a computer and then run on their own.</li>
<li><strong>Read the results comfortably:</strong> the Responses tab renders its charts and the individual view on a phone, but a form with twenty questions is a lot of scrolling. Link to Sheets and open the Sheets app, which is built for exactly that.</li>
<li><strong>Print or export:</strong> printing a form or saving a response as a PDF goes through the browser print dialog, which works and is awkward. <a href="/blog/how-to-save-google-forms-as-pdf/">Saving a Google Form as a PDF</a> is easier on a computer.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="why">Why is there no Google Forms app?</h2>

<p>Google has not said, and people have been asking on its help forum for years. The practical answer is that Forms is delivered as part of Drive and Workspace rather than as a product on its own, and a form editor is one of the few Google tools that works well enough in a mobile browser that a native app would mostly duplicate it.</p>

<p>Docs and Sheets got apps because typing and editing cells in a browser on a phone is painful. Building a form is mostly tapping, which the browser handles. Whether Google ever ships one is not something anyone outside Google can answer, so plan around the browser and the home-screen icon.</p>
