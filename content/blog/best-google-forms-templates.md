<p style="font-size: inherit;">Google Forms has a built-in template gallery. Most articles about it either undercount what is in there or quietly list templates from a different product, so here is the actual contents, section by section, with what each one is good for and where it falls down.</p>

<blockquote><p><strong>Quick answer.</strong> Google Forms ships <strong>17 templates</strong> in its native gallery, split across Work (6), Personal (6) and Education (5). Open them at <a href="https://docs.google.com/forms/u/0/?ftv=1">forms.google.com</a> and click one. It opens as a new form in your Drive straight away. There is no separate "copy" step and no cost.</p></blockquote>

<h2>The 17 templates Google Forms actually ships</h2>

<p>The gallery lives on the Google Forms home screen. Click <strong>Template gallery</strong> at the top and you get three sections. Clicking any tile creates a working copy in your own Drive straight away.</p>

<h3>Work (6 templates)</h3>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_template_gallery_work_fcd75d04b6.jpg" alt="Google Forms template gallery Work section showing Event Feedback, Order Form, Job Application, Time Off Request, Work Request and Customer Feedback" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Event Feedback.</strong> Post-event ratings and open comments. Linear scales for the numbers, one paragraph field for the things people actually remember.</li>
<li><strong>Order Form.</strong> Item picker, quantity, delivery details. Worth knowing before you commit: it cannot take payment, and there is no field that multiplies quantity by price, so the total is typed by hand or worked out later.</li>
<li><strong>Job Application.</strong> Name, contact, position, file upload for a CV. The upload counts against your Drive quota, which matters once a role gets a few hundred applicants.</li>
<li><strong>Time Off Request.</strong> Dates, type of leave, cover arrangements. No approval routing, so the manager finds out by watching the response sheet.</li>
<li><strong>Work Request.</strong> Internal ticket intake: requester, category, priority, description.</li>
<li><strong>Customer Feedback.</strong> Feedback type, rating, free text. The closest thing in the gallery to an NPS survey, though it does not calculate a score.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Personal (6 templates)</h3>

<ul>
<li><strong>Contact Information.</strong> Name, email, address, phone. The plainest template in the gallery and the most copied.</li>
<li><strong>Find a Time.</strong> A checkbox grid of days against time slots. Useful for scheduling a group without a scheduling tool, and it does not check anyone's calendar.</li>
<li><strong>RSVP.</strong> Attending yes or no, guest count, dietary notes.</li>
<li><strong>Party Invite.</strong> RSVP with a friendlier header image and a what-are-you-bringing question.</li>
<li><strong>T-Shirt Sign Up.</strong> Size picker plus name. No stock control, so two people can claim the last large.</li>
<li><strong>Event Registration.</strong> Attendee details, session choice, contact. The one to start from for anything ticketed, keeping in mind there is no payment field and no capacity cap on a question.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Education (5 templates)</h3>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/gforms_template_gallery_education_19e9a2f22f.jpg" alt="Google Forms template gallery Education section showing Blank Quiz, Exit Ticket, Assessment, Worksheet and Course Evaluation" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" /></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Blank Quiz.</strong> The only template with <strong>Make this a quiz</strong> already switched on, so answer keys and point values are available from the first question. Start here for anything graded rather than converting a form later.</li>
<li><strong>Exit Ticket.</strong> Three short questions for the end of a lesson. Short enough that it gets answered on the way out.</li>
<li><strong>Assessment.</strong> A longer graded set with name and email capture at the top.</li>
<li><strong>Worksheet.</strong> Structured practice questions with an image field per question.</li>
<li><strong>Course Evaluation.</strong> End-of-course ratings across teaching, materials and workload.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How to open a Google Forms template</h2>

<ol>
<li>Go to <a href="https://docs.google.com/forms/">forms.google.com</a> signed in to the Google account you want to own the form.</li>
<li>Click <strong>Template gallery</strong> in the top bar. If you are on a Workspace account you will also see a tab named after your organisation, which holds templates your admin published.</li>
<li>Click a tile. Google creates a copy in your Drive immediately and opens it in the editor.</li>
</ol>

<p>That last point catches people out. The gallery has no preview: clicking a template is the same as taking it. If you were only looking, delete the new form afterwards or your Drive fills with half-opened templates.</p>

<h2>How to customise a template after you open it</h2>

<p>Everything in a template is editable. What you can change divides cleanly into content and appearance.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Questions.</strong> Click any question to edit it. The type dropdown covers short answer, paragraph, multiple choice, checkboxes, dropdown, file upload, linear scale, multiple choice grid, checkbox grid, date and time. Toggle the asterisk to make one required.</li>
<li><strong>Form settings.</strong> Open the <strong>Settings</strong> tab beside Questions and Responses. Collecting email addresses, response receipts, editing after submit and the confirmation message all live there.</li>
<li><strong>Appearance.</strong> The palette icon changes theme colour, header image and font. There are four font choices and no way to load your own, so a template can look tidy but never quite on brand.</li>
</ul>

<p>Two limits are worth knowing before you build a process on a template. Branching works at section level, driven by a multiple choice answer, so you cannot show or hide an individual field based on a previous answer. And there is no calculation field anywhere, so any total on an order form is manual.</p>

<h2>Google Forms templates for students and teachers</h2>

<p>Classroom use is the most common reason people go looking, and the needs are narrow.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Collecting work.</strong> Start from Assessment or Worksheet and add a file upload field. Switch on Collect email addresses so submissions match to students without anyone typing an address.</li>
<li><strong>Quick checks.</strong> Exit Ticket, unchanged. Three questions and one screen is the whole point.</li>
<li><strong>Graded tests.</strong> Blank Quiz, because the quiz setting is already on. Answer keys work on six question types, and a short answer near miss is left ungraded rather than marked wrong, so plan for a manual pass.</li>
<li><strong>Parent consent.</strong> Build from Contact Information, add a required checkbox for the permission itself, and keep it to one page so it survives being filled in on a phone.</li>
</ul>

<p>File uploads consume your Drive quota, so a class of thirty submitting photos eats space faster than expected.</p>

<h2>Google Forms templates for business use</h2>

<p>Business use pushes on the thinnest parts of Google Forms.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Client intake.</strong> Contact Information plus your own scope questions. Fine until you need different follow-ups per service line.</li>
<li><strong>Purchase requests.</strong> Work Request with cost centre and line items added. The total cannot be calculated or validated.</li>
<li><strong>Employee feedback.</strong> Customer Feedback with Collect email addresses off and no name field. Say so in the form description, because people will assume otherwise.</li>
<li><strong>Vendor onboarding.</strong> Do not collect bank or tax identifiers in a Google Form. There is no field-level encryption and the response sheet inherits your Drive sharing.</li>
</ul>

<p>That last one is the honest limit rather than a pitch. For anything regulated, the form is the wrong tool no matter which template you start from.</p>

<h2>Are Google Forms templates free, and can you export to PDF?</h2>

<p>Every template in the gallery is free, on personal and Workspace accounts alike. There is no paid tier for the gallery and no cap on how many forms you build.</p>

<p>PDF is the awkward part. Google Forms has no export-as-PDF for a form or for a single response. The options are printing the form from your browser to PDF, or opening the linked response sheet and exporting that. Neither gives you a per-response document that looks designed. <h2>Google Forms templates FAQ</h2>

<details><summary>How many templates does Google Forms have?</summary><p>Seventeen in the native gallery: six under Work, six under Personal and five under Education. Workspace accounts may see an extra tab holding templates published by their own organisation, which sits on top of those 17.</p></details>

<details><summary>How do I copy a Google Forms template?</summary><p>Click the tile in the template gallery. Google creates a copy in your Drive and opens it straight away, so there is no separate copy step. There is also no preview, which means clicking a template you were only curious about leaves a new form behind.</p></details>

<details><summary>Are Google Forms templates really free?</summary><p>Yes, on both personal and Workspace accounts, with no limit on how many forms you create from them. The practical limit is storage: file upload answers consume your Drive quota, and a full Drive stops a form accepting uploads.</p></details>

<details><summary>Can I customise a Google Forms template after I copy it?</summary><p>Yes, every question and setting is editable. Click a question to change its type or wording, toggle the asterisk to make it required, and use the Settings tab for form-level options such as collecting email addresses. Appearance is limited to theme colour, header image and four fonts.</p></details>

<details><summary>How do I create my own template in Google Forms?</summary><p>There is no save-as-template button on a personal account. Build the form once, then use Make a copy from the three-dot menu each time you need a fresh one. Workspace admins can publish a form into a shared organisation gallery, which is the only true template feature Google offers.</p></details>

<details><summary>Can a Google Forms template collect payments?</summary><p>No. Google Forms has no payment field and no processor integration, so order forms and ticketed events need a separate payment step after submission. Anything that has to take money on the form itself needs a builder with native payment fields.</p></details>

<details><summary>Can I add conditional logic to a Google Forms template?</summary><p>Only at section level. A multiple choice answer can send someone to a different section, but you cannot show or hide an individual question based on a previous answer. Per-question logic needs a different builder.</p></details>

<details><summary>Can ChatGPT create a Google Form for me?</summary><p>Not directly, because it has no write access to Google Forms. It can draft your questions and answer options for you to paste in. Tools built for this generate the form itself from a prompt.</p></details>

<details><summary>What is the best Google Forms template to start from?</summary><p>Contact Information for anything that just gathers details, Event Registration for anything ticketed, and Blank Quiz for anything graded, because it is the only one with the quiz setting already switched on. Everything else in the gallery is a variation on those three shapes.</p></details>
