Open them at <a href="https://docs.google.com/forms/u/0/?ftv=1">forms.google.com</a> and click one. It opens as a new form in your Drive straight away. There is no separate "copy" step and no cost.</p></blockquote>

<h2>How to open a Google Forms template</h2>

<ol>
<li>Go to <a href="https://docs.google.com/forms/">forms.google.com</a> signed in to the Google account you want to own the form.</li>
<li>Click <strong>Template gallery</strong> in the top bar. If you are on a Workspace account you will also see a tab named after your organisation, which holds templates your admin published.</li>
<li>Click a tile. Google creates a copy in your Drive immediately and opens it in the editor.</li>
</ol>

<p>That last point catches people out. The gallery has no preview: clicking a template is the same as taking it. If you were only looking, delete the new form afterwards or your Drive fills with half-opened templates.</p>

<h2>How to customise a template after you open it</h2>

<p>Everything in a template is editable. What you can change divides cleanly into content and appearance.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Questions.</strong> Click any question to edit it. The type dropdown covers short answer, paragraph, multiple choice, checkboxes, dropdown, file upload, linear scale, multiple choice grid, checkbox grid, date and time. Toggle the asterisk to make one required.</li>
<li><strong>Form settings.</strong> Open the <strong>Settings</strong> tab beside Questions and Responses. Collecting email addresses, response receipts, editing after submit and the confirmation message all live there.</li>
<li><strong>Appearance.</strong> The palette icon changes theme colour, header image and font. There are four font choices and no way to load your own, so a template can look tidy but never quite on brand.</li>
</ul>

<p>Two limits are worth knowing before you build a process on a template. Branching works at section level, driven by a multiple choice answer, so you cannot show or hide an individual field based on a previous answer. And there is no calculation field anywhere, so any total on an order form is manual.</p>

<h2>Google Forms templates for students and teachers</h2>

<p>Classroom use is the most common reason people go looking, and the needs are narrow.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Collecting work.</strong> Start from Assessment or Worksheet and add a file upload field. Switch on Collect email addresses so submissions match to students without anyone typing an address.</li>
<li><strong>Quick checks.</strong> Exit Ticket, unchanged. Three questions and one screen is the whole point.</li>
<li><strong>Graded tests.</strong> Blank Quiz, because the quiz setting is already on. Answer keys work on six question types, and a short answer near miss is left ungraded rather than marked wrong, so plan for a manual pass.</li>
<li><strong>Parent consent.</strong> Build from Contact Information, add a required checkbox for the permission itself, and keep it to one page so it survives being filled in on a phone.</li>
</ul>

<p>File uploads consume your Drive quota, so a class of thirty submitting photos eats space faster than expected.</p>

<h2>Google Forms templates for business use</h2>

<p>Business use pushes on the thinnest parts of Google Forms.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Client intake.</strong> Contact Information plus your own scope questions. Fine until you need different follow-ups per service line.</li>
<li><strong>Purchase requests.</strong> Work Request with cost centre and line items added. The total cannot be calculated or validated.</li>
<li><strong>Employee feedback.</strong> Customer Feedback with Collect email addresses off and no name field. Say so in the form description, because people will assume otherwise.</li>
<li><strong>Vendor onboarding.</strong> Do not collect bank or tax identifiers in a Google Form. There is no field-level encryption and the response sheet inherits your Drive sharing.</li>
</ul>

<p>That last one is the honest limit rather than a pitch. For anything regulated, the form is the wrong tool no matter which template you start from.</p>

<details><summary>Are Google Forms templates really free?</summary><p>Yes, on both personal and Workspace accounts, with no limit on how many forms you create from them. <details><summary>Can I customise a Google Forms template after I copy it?</summary><p>Yes, every question and setting is editable.
