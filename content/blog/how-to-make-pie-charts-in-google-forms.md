![Cover art for a guide to making a pie chart from Google Forms responses](https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/c76adc09db53d976_how-to-make-pie-charts-in-google-forms-cover.png)

<p style="font-size: inherit;">Google Forms draws a pie chart for you. Open the <strong>Responses</strong> tab and every multiple choice and dropdown question already has one, as soon as one person has answered. You cannot change that chart inside Google Forms. To make a Google Forms pie chart with your own colors and labels, or to save it as a file, you build it in Google Sheets from the linked responses.</p>

<div class="tldr">
<p><strong>Quick answer</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>You want to see the chart Google already made:</strong> click <strong>Responses</strong>, then <strong>Summary</strong>. Multiple choice and dropdown questions get a pie chart. Checkboxes and scales get a bar chart.</li>
<li><strong>You want to copy or download it:</strong> click the copy icon on the chart card and paste it into Docs or Slides. For a file you can attach, rebuild it in Google Sheets. Sheets downloads a chart as an image or a PDF.</li>
<li><strong>You want different colors or a different chart type:</strong> link the responses to Google Sheets and count the answers with COUNTIF. Insert a pie chart from that table.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 id="where">Where is the Google Forms pie chart?</h2>

<p>The chart lives in the Responses tab, not in the form itself. <a href="https://support.google.com/docs/answer/139706" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google's documentation</a> notes that the Summary view only appears once the form has at least one response, so a brand new form shows nothing until you submit a test answer.</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>
<h3>Open the Responses tab</h3>
<p>Open the form in the editor and click <strong>Responses</strong> at the top, next to Questions and Settings.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Stay on Summary</h3>
<p>Summary is the first of the three views and the one with the charts. Question groups answers by question, and Individual shows one response at a time.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Scroll to the question</h3>
<p>Each question has its own card. The chart type depends on the question type, and you cannot change it here.</p>
<!-- IMAGE: Responses > Summary on a test form with 5+ responses, a multiple choice pie chart card directly above a checkbox bar chart card | ALT: The Google Forms Responses Summary view showing a pie chart for a multiple choice question next to a bar chart for a checkbox question -->
</li>
</ol>

<h3>Which questions get a pie chart</h3>

<p>Google picks the chart from the question type. A pie chart only works when each person gives one answer, so only single-answer questions get one.</p>
<ul>
<li>Multiple choice and dropdown questions: pie chart.</li>
<li>Checkbox questions: horizontal bar chart, because one person can tick several options.</li>
<li>Linear scale and rating questions: bar chart, one bar per point on the scale.</li>
<li>Multiple choice grid and checkbox grid questions: stacked bar chart, one bar per row.</li>
<li>Short answer and paragraph questions: a list of the answers, no chart.</li>
</ul>
<p>If a question shows a bar chart and you need a pie chart, change its type to multiple choice in the Questions tab. If you can, change the type before you share the form.</p>

<h2 id="copy">How do you copy or download a Google Forms pie chart?</h2>

<p>The Summary view has no download button. It does have a copy control. Click the copy icon at the top right of the chart card and paste the chart into a Google Doc or a slide. Google's help page says a chart pasted into Docs or Slides can be updated later from inside that file, as long as you can still edit the form.</p>
<!-- IMAGE: one Summary chart card, cursor hovering, the copy icon in the card's top right highlighted | ALT: A pie chart card in the Google Forms Summary view with the copy icon in the top right corner highlighted -->
<p>A screenshot works too, and it is the only option on a phone. For a file you can attach or print, such as a PNG or a PDF, rebuild the chart in Google Sheets using the steps below.</p>

<h2 id="share">How do you show respondents the pie chart?</h2>

<p>You can let the people who fill in the form see the same charts, which is useful for a class poll or a team vote. Open <strong>Settings</strong> and expand <strong>Presentation</strong>. Turn on <strong>View results summary</strong>. After someone submits, the confirmation page links to a summary with every chart.</p>
<p>The summary shows everything, not only the charts. <a href="https://support.google.com/docs/answer/2839588" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google warns</a> that full text answers are visible to anyone who can respond. Leave the setting off on a form that asks for names or comments. One respondent should not be able to read what another wrote.</p>

<h2 id="sheets">How do you make a custom pie chart in Google Sheets from form responses?</h2>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/cee663ab9edd7a05_google-sheets-pie-chart.gif" alt="Selecting the answer column in the linked response sheet and inserting a pie chart in Google Sheets" loading="lazy"></p>

<p>Google Sheets is where you change colors and labels, and where you download the chart as a file. Chart a small count table instead of the raw response column. A chart built on a fixed range such as C2:C50 stops at row 50. A COUNTIF over the whole column keeps counting as responses arrive.</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>
<h3>Link the responses to a spreadsheet</h3>
<p>In the Responses tab, click <strong>Link to Sheets</strong> in the top right and choose <strong>Create a new spreadsheet</strong>. Every response, including the ones you already have, appears as a row on a tab called Form Responses 1. Our guide on <a href="/blog/how-to-link-google-sheets-to-google-forms/">linking Google Sheets to Google Forms</a> covers the options if you already have a spreadsheet.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Add a count table on a new tab</h3>
<p>Add a second tab so your formulas never sit in the response rows. In column A, type each answer option exactly as it appears in the form. Column B holds the count for each one. If the question is in column C of the response tab, cell B2 is:</p>
<p><code>=COUNTIF('Form Responses 1'!C:C, A2)</code></p>
<p>Fill the formula down. Counting the whole column means new responses are included without touching the formula again.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Insert the pie chart</h3>
<p>Select the two columns of the count table, then click <strong>Insert</strong> and <strong>Chart</strong>. In the Chart editor, set <strong>Chart type</strong> to <strong>Pie chart</strong>. <a href="https://support.google.com/docs/answer/9143036" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Sheets expects</a> labels in the first column and positive numbers in the second, which is what the count table gives it. An option with zero picks is left out of the chart.</p>
<!-- IMAGE: Sheets Chart editor open, chart type dropdown showing Pie chart, data range pointing at the two-column count table | ALT: The Google Sheets Chart editor with Pie chart selected and a COUNTIF count table as the data range -->
</li>
<li>
<h3>Customize it</h3>
<p>Open the <strong>Customize</strong> tab of the Chart editor. The options you need:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pie slice</strong>: the color of each slice.</li>
<li><strong>Pie chart</strong>: slice labels as a value or a percentage, and the doughnut hole if you prefer that style.</li>
<li><strong>Chart &amp; axis titles</strong>: the heading.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Download or publish it</h3>
<p>Click the chart and open its three-dot menu. Choose <strong>Download</strong> and pick PNG or SVG for an image. Pick PDF for print. The same menu has <strong>Publish chart</strong>, which gives you a link or embed code that shows the current chart.</p>
</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="quiz">How do you see the pie chart for a Google Forms quiz?</h2>

<p>A quiz uses the same Responses tab and the same Summary view. Multiple choice and dropdown questions still get a pie chart, with the correct answer marked so you can see how many people got it right. The score overview at the top of the Summary is a bar chart of total points, and Google also lists the questions people missed most often.</p>
<p>For a class, turn on <strong>View results summary</strong> only if you want every student to see the whole class's answers. To send each student only their own score, use the quiz's release grades setting instead. Our guide on <a href="/blog/how-to-view-scores-in-google-forms/">viewing scores in Google Forms</a> covers that.</p>

<h2 id="looker">How do you build a live chart in Looker Studio?</h2>

<p>Looker Studio, which used to be called Google Data Studio, is for a dashboard that other people open, rather than a chart you paste somewhere. It reads the linked response sheet and updates on its own.</p>
<ol>
<li>Open lookerstudio.google.com and click <strong>Create</strong>, then <strong>Report</strong>.</li>
<li>Choose <strong>Google Sheets</strong> as the data source and pick the response spreadsheet and the Form Responses 1 tab.</li>
<li>Click <strong>Add a chart</strong> and choose a pie chart. Set the dimension to the question column and the metric to Record Count.</li>
<li>Share the report the way you share a Google Doc.</li>
</ol>
<p>It takes longer to learn than Sheets, so use it when several people need to look at the results over time rather than once.</p>

<h2 id="limits">What can Google Forms charts not do?</h2>

<ul>
<li>Change how a chart looks or switch its type. The Summary view is fixed.</li>
<li>Export a chart as a file. Copying and screenshots are the only ways out of Forms.</li>
<li>Filter a chart by date or by another answer. Every chart covers every response.</li>
<li>Share one chart from inside Forms. View results summary shares all of them, text answers included. To share one chart, copy it into a Doc and share that.</li>
</ul>
