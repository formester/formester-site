![Cover art for a guide to creating a contact form in Google Forms](https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/800e59024af8feed_how-to-create-a-contact-form-in-google-forms-cover.png)

<p style="font-size: inherit;">To create a contact form in Google Forms, open the Contact Information template from the template gallery, rename it Contact Us, cut the fields down to name, email and a message, then publish.</p>

<p>Then do the two things the template leaves off: switch off Limit to 1 response, which would force every visitor to sign in, and turn on Get email notifications for new responses in the Responses tab, or you will never know a message arrived.</p>

<p>The eight steps below build that form, put it on a website with the embed code, and cover what Google Forms cannot do for a contact form, which matters more here than for most forms.</p>

<div class="tldr">
<p><strong>Quick answer</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Start from the template:</strong> Template gallery, Personal row, Contact Information. It ships with name, email, address, phone and comments, with email validation already set.</li>
<li><strong>Two settings decide whether it works:</strong> Limit to 1 response off, so strangers can write to you without a Google account, and Get email notifications on, so you hear about each message.</li>
<li><strong>On your site:</strong> the three-dot menu has Embed HTML, an iframe you paste into any page. Or share the link from a Contact button.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 id="build">How do you make a contact form in Google Forms?</h2>

<ol class="steps">
<li>
<h3>Open the Contact Information template and rename it</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/08bd2a8cad74f76b_gforms-cf-step1-template-rename.gif" alt="The Google Forms template gallery scrolled to the Personal row, opening Contact Information and renaming the form title to Contact Us with a one-line description" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Go to <a href="https://forms.google.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener">forms.google.com</a>, open the <strong>Template gallery</strong> and pick <strong>Contact Information</strong> from the Personal row. Change the title to Contact Us and use the description for the one thing visitors want to know: how fast you reply.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Cut the fields to what you will act on</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/e651f7d81435348a_gforms-cf-step2-email-validation-fields.gif" alt="The Email question in Google Forms showing its Text, Email address response validation, then deleting the Address question and renaming Comments to How can we help" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>The template's Email question already has <strong>Response validation</strong> set to Text, Email address, so a typo gets an error before submission. Delete the Address question unless you post things to people. Rename Comments to <strong>How can we help?</strong> and switch <strong>Required</strong> on, since a contact form with no message is a mystery.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Add a topic question so you can sort messages</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/94d32b390c8a076d_gforms-cf-step2b-topic-question.gif" alt="Adding a Multiple choice question called What is this about with the options Sales, Support and Something else in Google Forms" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Add a <strong>Multiple choice</strong> question, What is this about?, with three or four options such as Sales, Support and Something else. Google Forms cannot send each answer to a different inbox, but the answer lands in your Sheet, and it lets you filter sales enquiries out of support questions at a glance.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Collect the email address and turn off the sign-in</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/190fb2a9c52b8980_gforms-cf-step3-collect-email-limit-off.gif" alt="The Google Forms Settings tab: Collect email addresses set to Responder input, Send responders a copy set to Always and Limit to 1 response switched off" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>In <strong>Settings</strong>, set <strong>Collect email addresses</strong> to Responder input and <strong>Send responders a copy of their response</strong> to Always, so the visitor has a record of what they sent. Then switch <strong>Limit to 1 response</strong> off. The template ships with it on, and on means every visitor must sign in to a Google account before they can write to you.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Write the confirmation message</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/6efc37e031743d8d_gforms-cf-step4-confirmation-message.gif" alt="Editing the confirmation message under Presentation settings in Google Forms to say a reply will come within one business day" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Under <strong>Presentation</strong>, edit the <strong>Confirmation message</strong>. This is the only thing the visitor sees after they click Submit, so say that the message arrived and when they will hear back. The default "Thanks for submitting your contact info" reads like nobody is home.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Turn on email notifications for new responses</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/1b98f59684575c95_gforms-cf-step5-email-notifications.gif" alt="Opening the three-dot menu in the Google Forms Responses tab and ticking Get email notifications for new responses" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Open the <strong>Responses</strong> tab, click the three-dot menu next to Link to Sheets and tick <strong>Get email notifications for new responses</strong>. Without this, messages pile up silently inside the form. Google sends a short email to the account that owns the form, with a link to the response.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Publish and copy the link</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/f9ef4d791786e6f1_gforms-cf-step6-publish-copy-link.gif" alt="Clicking Publish in Google Forms, confirming Anyone with the link can respond, then copying the shortened responder link" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>Click <strong>Publish</strong>, confirm the responder setting says Anyone with the link, and copy the responder link with Shorten URL ticked. That link is the Contact button on your site, the line in your email signature and the reply you send when someone asks how to reach you.</p>
</li>
<li>
<h3>Embed it on your website and link the Sheet</h3>
<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/2f6dfd24523d3b87_gforms-cf-step7-embed-html-link-sheets.gif" alt="Opening Embed HTML from the three-dot menu in Google Forms to copy the iframe code, then clicking Link to Sheets in the Responses tab" loading="lazy"></p>
<p>The three-dot menu at the top right has <strong>Embed HTML</strong>: set a width and height, copy the iframe and paste it into an HTML block on your page. Then click <strong>Link to Sheets</strong> in the Responses tab so every message becomes a timestamped row you can mark as answered.</p>
</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="fields">Which fields belong on a contact form?</h2>

<p>Fewer than you think. Every extra field loses some of the people who were about to write to you, and a contact form is the one place you want no friction at all.</p>

<h2 id="email">How do you get contact form submissions by email?</h2>

<p>Google Forms does not email you a submission by default, which is the question that fills Google's own support forum. The switch is in the Responses tab, under the three-dot menu, called <strong>Get email notifications for new responses</strong>. It applies to your account only, so a colleague who also edits the form has to tick it for themselves.</p>

<p>The notification is a plain email from Google Forms saying a new response arrived, with a link to it. It does not contain the message, and you cannot change the wording, the sender or the recipient. To reply, you open the response, copy the visitor's address and write a normal email.</p>

<p>If you need the message itself in the email, or different people for different topics, that takes a Marketplace add-on such as Email Notifications for Google Forms or a Zapier step from the linked Sheet. Both work, and both are another moving part on a form that was supposed to be simple.</p>

<h2 id="website">How do you add a Google Form to your website?</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Embed HTML:</strong> the three-dot menu at the top right gives you an iframe. Paste it into an HTML or Custom Code block. The frame does not resize, so set the height tall enough for the whole form or visitors get a scrollbar inside the page.</li>
<li><strong>Google Sites:</strong> Insert, then Forms, then pick the form. It embeds without code.</li>
<li><strong>WordPress:</strong> a Custom HTML block takes the iframe. <a href="/blog/how-to-add-a-wordpress-contact-form-without-a-plugin/">WordPress contact forms without a plugin</a> covers the alternatives.</li>
<li><strong>Canva, Wix and Squarespace:</strong> each has an embed code element. <a href="/blog/how-to-add-a-contact-us-form-to-canva-website/">Adding a contact form to a Canva website</a> shows the pattern.</li>
<li><strong>A link or button:</strong> point your Contact button at the responder link. The form opens on Google's page, which is the simplest option and the one that looks least like your site.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="limits">What Google Forms cannot do for a contact form</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Filter spam:</strong> there is no CAPTCHA and no spam check. The only guard is requiring a sign-in, which also turns away the people you want.</li>
<li><strong>Route messages:</strong> every notification goes to the form owner. Sales, support and press all land in one place.</li>
<li><strong>Email you the message:</strong> the notification says a response exists. Reading it means opening the form or the Sheet.</li>
<li><strong>Reply from the form:</strong> there is no reply button and no autoresponder beyond a copy of their own answers.</li>
<li><strong>Look like your site:</strong> a header image and one theme colour, with the Google Forms footer and a "Never submit passwords" warning under every embed.</li>
<li><strong>Take attachments from strangers:</strong> File upload requires the visitor to sign in to Google.</li>
</ul>
