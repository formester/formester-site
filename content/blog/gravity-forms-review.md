<h2>The 60-second verdict</h2>

<p style="font-size: inherit;"><strong>What Gravity Forms is:</strong> A premium WordPress form plugin, $59 to $259 per year, with 30+ official add-ons and a mature conditional-logic engine. WordPress-only. Self-hosted, no cloud version.</p>

<p><strong>What it gets right:</strong> The most powerful conditional logic and calculation engine of any WordPress form plugin, first-party add-ons for every major workflow (Stripe, PayPal, MailChimp, Salesforce, Zapier), and lifetime feature parity within your license tier.</p>

<p><strong>What it gets wrong:</strong> WordPress-only (no cloud, no hosted version), add-on complexity (Elite license needed for the useful ones), editor UX has not modernized in years, and if you're not on WordPress it's not a choice.</p>

<p><strong>Bottom line:</strong> If your site is WordPress and you want the most powerful form plugin in the ecosystem, Gravity Forms is the pick, especially at the Elite tier. If your site is not WordPress, or you want a hosted form-builder without maintaining the plugin, look at <a href="https://formester.com/">Formester</a> or Fillout.</p>

<p><strong>G2 rating:</strong> <a href="https://www.g2.com/products/gravity-forms/reviews">4.7/5 (248 reviews)</a></p>


<h2>What Gravity Forms is (and isn't)</h2>

<p><p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/shot_gf_home_660b83407d.png" alt="Gravity Forms homepage hero screenshot" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" loading="lazy" /></p></p>

<p>Gravity Forms is a <strong>WordPress plugin</strong>. Not a SaaS. Not a hosted form service. You install it on your own WordPress site, you host the forms, you own the data.</p>

<p>That distinction matters more than any feature comparison. If you're on Webflow, Framer, Wix, Squarespace, Shopify, or any non-WordPress CMS, this review is not for you. Gravity Forms will not install on those platforms. Move on to Formester, Fillout, or a native form for your CMS.</p>

<p>If you are on WordPress, keep reading. Gravity Forms has been in the market since 2008 and it's still the most powerful form plugin the WordPress ecosystem has produced.</p>


<h2>Pricing: three tiers, all annual</h2>

<p><p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/shot_gf_pricing_169595e5b3.png" alt="Gravity Forms pricing page screenshot" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" loading="lazy" /></p></p>

<p>The <strong>Basic license</strong> is a real fit for a marketing site. Conditional logic, Mailchimp, HubSpot, ActiveCampaign, and Campaign Monitor are all included, so newsletter capture and CRM sync work on day one. Where Basic runs out of room is payments: Stripe, PayPal Checkout, and Square all sit behind Pro.</p>

<p><strong>Pro at $159/year</strong> is the pick the moment you take payments, use Zapier for wider integrations, or sync to Zoho CRM. Three sites cover most small agencies.</p>

<p><strong>Elite at $259/year</strong> is the value tier for agencies and anyone building serious workflows. Unlimited sites, priority support, WordPress Multisite, and the deep add-ons (User Registration, Salesforce, Signature, Survey, Polls, Conversational Forms, Partial Entries, Webhooks, Quiz, Advanced Post Creation). Cheaper than a $39/month Jotform Silver seat for a mid-sized team.</p>


<h2>Editor and builder experience</h2>

<p><p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/shot_gf_features_7dfc4c755b.png" alt="Gravity Forms features page screenshot" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" loading="lazy" /></p></p>

<p>The form editor is a three-panel layout: field library on the left, form canvas in the middle, field settings on the right. Drag a field type from the left onto the canvas, click it to edit settings on the right.</p>

<p><strong>What it does well:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Field library covers 30+ types out of the box (name, email, phone, address, file upload, signature via add-on, price, product, quantity, HTML, section break, page break)</li>
<li>Conditional logic is the deepest in the WordPress form plugin space: any field can conditionally show, hide, require, or auto-populate based on any other field</li>
<li>Calculations engine handles multi-step math (subtotal + tax + shipping, tiered pricing, quantity discounts) with a formula field</li>
<li>Multi-page forms with progress bar built in, no add-on needed</li>
<li>Save-and-continue-later ships as a free add-on</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>What feels dated:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>UI has not changed meaningfully in five years; feels 2019-era compared to Fillout or Formester</li>
<li>No drag-to-reorder between form pages (you have to move fields via up/down arrows)</li>
<li>No live-preview split; you toggle to Preview to see how it renders</li>
<li>Mobile preview is inside a modal, not a live side-by-side</li>
</ul>

<h2>Real-world use cases where it wins</h2>

<p><strong>1. WordPress-based membership sites.</strong> Elite's User Registration add-on plus Stripe subscription plus conditional profile fields, all inside WordPress. Gravity Forms Elite plus WooMemberships is the reference stack for a self-hosted membership site.</p>

<p><strong>2. Multi-step application forms with conditional logic.</strong> Job applications, insurance intake, mortgage pre-qualification, university admissions. The conditional-logic engine plus multi-page form plus save-and-continue is the tightest DIY solution for a 20-field intake form.</p>

<p><strong>3. Quote calculators with variable pricing.</strong> Custom pricing forms (freelance quotes, contractor bids, subscription tiers) benefit from the calculations engine. Multi-variable pricing (quantity + tier + region + discount code) works without a plugin.</p>

<p><strong>4. WooCommerce order flows with custom fields.</strong> Gravity Forms + WooCommerce Gravity Forms Product Add-Ons pairs cleanly for product-configurator flows (custom cake orders, engraving requests, made-to-measure clothing).</p>

<p><strong>5. Salesforce lead capture on a WordPress marketing site.</strong> The Salesforce add-on is first-party and Elite-only, so field mapping and update-vs-create logic are built in, not stitched together via Zapier. HubSpot sync ships on Basic and up if you don't need Salesforce.</p>


<h2>Where Gravity Forms falls short</h2>

<p><strong>It's WordPress-only.</strong> If your site isn't WordPress, this whole review is moot. There is no cloud version, no Shopify plugin, no Webflow embed. Forms live on your WordPress install or nowhere.</p>

<p><strong>It's self-hosted.</strong> You're responsible for keeping the plugin updated, keeping WordPress core updated, keeping PHP updated, and dealing with security patches. A cloud form service like Formester or Fillout takes all of that off your plate.</p>

<p><strong>No hosted version.</strong> Even if you'd pay for one, it doesn't exist. Every submission lives in your WordPress database.</p>

<p><strong>Add-on management is fragmented.</strong> Each add-on has its own settings page, its own version, its own update flow. On a large site with 15 add-ons active, keeping them all in sync is real ops overhead.</p>

<p><strong>Editor UX has not modernized.</strong> Compared to Fillout, Typeform, or Formester (built in the last five years), the Gravity Forms editor feels like 2019. Functional, not delightful.</p>

<p><strong>Response management is basic.</strong> Entries dashboard shows a WordPress admin table. No card view, no drag-to-status. Export to CSV works, but the analytics view is minimal.</p>

<p><strong>Front-end styling requires CSS.</strong> Gravity Forms 2.5+ has better default styling than the pre-2.5 versions, but matching your theme still requires custom CSS in most cases.</p>
