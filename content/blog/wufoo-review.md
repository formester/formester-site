<h2>The 60-second verdict</h2>

<p><strong>What Wufoo is:</strong> An online form builder owned by SurveyMonkey, priced from $16.25 per month yearly. Wufoo built its reputation on ease of use in the 2010s and still ships a functional form editor with report generation and a decent template library.

<p style="font-size: inherit;"><strong>What it does well:</strong> Simple forms with reports built in. The classic form-builder feel that made Wufoo popular. Reasonable pricing on the Starter tier compared to Jotform Bronze or Formstack. Templates cover the common cases.</p>

<p><strong>What is annoying:</strong> Wufoo has not modernized meaningfully in years. There's no marquee AI form generator, no offline capture, no native custom domain on lower tiers, and file uploads cap at 25 MB. Entry-per-form and form-per-plan caps are strict. Owned by SurveyMonkey since 2011, product development has slowed compared to newer builders.</p>

<p><strong>Bottom line:</strong> If you want a simple, reliable form builder with reports and don't need modern features (AI generation, offline PWA, native payments beyond basic), Wufoo works and Starter at $16.25 per month yearly is priced honestly. If you want a modern feature set at a similar or lower price, <a href="https://formester.com/">Formester</a> or Fillout is a better fit in 2026.</p>

<h2>What Wufoo is (and isn't)</h2>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/shot_home_71b6fdace8.png" alt="Wufoo homepage screenshot" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" loading="lazy" /></p>

<p>Wufoo is a <strong>hosted online form builder</strong>, owned by SurveyMonkey since 2011. It sits in the same category as Jotform, Formester, Fillout, and Formstack: build a form on a web canvas, embed or share it, collect responses in a dashboard.</p>

<p>The distinction from SurveyMonkey (its parent company) is important. SurveyMonkey is a survey platform for measurement programs at scale. Wufoo is a form builder for signups, contact forms, order forms, event registrations, and general data collection. Same parent, different products, different buyers.</p>

<p>Wufoo's original identity was ease-of-use plus reports. Signing up, building a form, and getting a chart of responses took minutes. That was a real differentiator in 2010. In 2026, most modern form builders match that ease and add features Wufoo hasn't added (AI generation, offline capture, native custom domains on lower tiers, deeper conditional logic).</p>

<p>If you're already comfortable in Wufoo and your workflow is stable, there's no urgent reason to move. If you're evaluating fresh in 2026, you're likely to find a better fit elsewhere.</p>

<h2>Pricing: five tiers, all yearly-billed</h2>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/shot_pricing_c11f9626f8.png" alt="Wufoo pricing page screenshot" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" loading="lazy" /></p>

<p>Wufoo prices yearly on all paid tiers. There's a Free plan with tight caps, and each paid tier adds forms, entries per month, and a few features. Overage on entries is charged at $0.05 per extra entry.</p>

<p>A few practical notes:</p>

<p><strong>The Free plan is actually usable for very small use cases.</strong> 5 forms, 100 entries per month, 3 users. Enough for a tiny team's contact form or an internal signup. Compared to Typeform Free (10 responses) or Jotform Starter (100 submissions on 5 forms), Wufoo Free holds up.</p>

<p><strong>Starter at $16.25 per month yearly is Wufoo's honest entry tier.</strong> 10 forms, 1,000 entries per month. This is where most new Wufoo buyers land. It's reasonable pricing for a small operation.</p>

<p><strong>Professional at $33.25 per month yearly ($399/yr) is the mainstream mid-tier.</strong> Unlimited forms, 5,000 entries per month. If you're running multiple forms across a team or site, this is the tier that fits.</p>

<p><strong>Advanced at $83.25 per month yearly and Ultimate at $210.25 per month yearly</strong> scale the entry cap dramatically (25,000 and 200,000 respectively). Ultimate at $2,523 per year is a strange offering for a product that hasn't modernized: you're paying enterprise-tier money for a mid-tier feature set.</p>

<p><strong>Overage is $0.05 per extra entry</strong> on all paid tiers. If you consistently exceed your cap, upgrade instead of paying the overage. Otherwise it's a fair per-entry price for occasional spikes.</p>

<p><strong>Max file upload is 25 MB</strong> across all plans. That's tight for anything file-heavy (portfolios, video submissions, engineering drawings).</p>

<h2>Editor and features</h2>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/shot_tour_384d7bde10.png" alt="Wufoo product tour screenshot" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" loading="lazy" /></p>

<p>The editor is a straightforward drag-and-drop canvas. Field library on the left, canvas in the middle, field settings on the right. Standard form-builder pattern.</p>

<p>What it does well:</p></p>

<ul>
<li>Setup speed is fast. New users build a working form in ten minutes without documentation.</li>
<li>The template gallery covers the common use cases (contact, event registration, order, application, feedback, quiz).</li>
<li>Reports (charts and tables generated from responses) are still one of Wufoo's real strengths. They're not fancy, but they're one click and readable.</li>
<li>Notifications and confirmations are easy to configure. Autoresponders, admin alerts, customized thank-you pages.</li>
<li>Rules (Wufoo's word for conditional logic) work for simple show/hide branching.</li>
<li>Integrations exist for the common cases: Zapier, Salesforce, Mailchimp, PayPal, Stripe.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>What has fallen behind:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>No AI form generator. Every modern form builder (Formester, Jotform, Typeform, Fillout) ships AI generation from a prompt. Wufoo does not.</li>
<li>Offline capture is unavailable. Field teams cannot use Wufoo without a live internet connection.</li>
<li>Custom domain is not shipped natively on lower tiers.</li>
<li>Advanced conditional logic (multi-condition branches, complex piping) is thinner than Jotform, Formstack, or Formester.</li>
<li>File upload cap at 25 MB is tight for many workflows.</li>
<li>The design language of the forms looks 2018-era. Themes and customization exist but the default aesthetic reads dated.</li>
<li>Product velocity has slowed. Post-SurveyMonkey acquisition, Wufoo has not added the marquee features newer competitors ship regularly.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Templates and integrations</h2>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/shot_templates_2bfca21ee9.png" alt="Wufoo templates gallery screenshot" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" loading="lazy" /></p>

<p>Wufoo's template library is decent but not deep. Categories cover contact forms, event registrations, order forms, employment applications, surveys, quizzes, and feedback forms. The templates are functional and clearly written, if visually plain.</p>

<p><strong>Integrations</strong> cover the essentials via Zapier (5,000+ downstream apps) and native connectors:

<ul>
<li><strong>Payments:</strong> PayPal, Stripe (basic single-item payment)</li>
<li><strong>CRM and email:</strong> Salesforce, Mailchimp, HubSpot (via Zapier)</li>
<li><strong>Productivity:</strong> Google Sheets, Dropbox, Box</li>
<li><strong>Automation:</strong> Zapier, native webhooks (Professional and above)</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>The catch:</strong> integrations that ship natively on Formester Personal ($12/mo) or Jotform Silver ($39/mo) sometimes require Wufoo Professional at $33.25 per month yearly or Advanced at $83.25. Feature gating is stricter than newer competitors.</p>

<h2>Real-world use cases where Wufoo works well</h2>

<p><img src="https://formester-strapi.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/shot_features_7923bda68f.png" alt="Wufoo features screenshot" style="width:100%;max-width:900px;height:auto;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;" loading="lazy" /></p>

<p>1. Simple contact and inquiry forms with reports.</p> Small business, single admin, occasional lead capture. Wufoo Starter at $16.25 per month yearly handles this cleanly with the reports feature built in.</p>

<p><strong>Event registrations for small events.</strong> Non-profit fundraisers, community events, small conferences. Under 1,000 entries per month fits Starter. Under 5,000 fits Professional. Payment via PayPal or Stripe if needed.</p>
<p><strong>Internal team forms for small companies.</strong> IT ticket intake, HR requests, expense submissions. The Free plan often works for teams under 3 users with light volume.</p>
<p><strong>Order forms for single-product or small-catalog sellers.</strong> The Stripe and PayPal integrations handle basic one-item purchase forms. Not for large catalogs with variants.</p>
<p><strong>Feedback and satisfaction surveys.</strong> Post-purchase feedback, event follow-up, quick pulse surveys. Reports render results in one click, which is still Wufoo's strongest workflow.</p>
<h2>Where Wufoo falls short</h2>

<p><strong>No AI form generator.</strong> Every meaningful competitor (Formester, Jotform, Typeform, Fillout) ships AI generation from a prompt in 2026. Wufoo does not. If AI generation is on your requirements list, Wufoo is out on paper.</p>
<p><strong>No offline capture.</strong> Field data collection, in-store surveys, event booths without wi-fi. Wufoo requires a live connection. Formester and Jotform ship offline PWAs; Wufoo does not.</p>
<p><strong>Advanced logic and piping is thin.</strong> Simple show/hide rules work fine. Multi-condition branches, question piping across many fields, calculation-based routing require workarounds. Newer competitors do this cleanly.</p>
<p><strong>File upload cap at 25 MB is limiting.</strong> Anyone collecting portfolios, videos, engineering drawings, or medical images runs into the cap. Formester Business ships 50 GB per submission; Wufoo Ultimate still caps at 25 MB.</p>
<p><strong>Custom domain and white-labeling is gated hard.</strong> Wufoo's default URL structure and branding sit on lower tiers. Full white-labeling requires higher plans and even then falls short of what Formester Personal ($12/mo) offers.</p>
<p><strong>Product velocity has slowed under SurveyMonkey ownership.</strong> Wufoo has not shipped a marquee new feature in years. Meanwhile Formester, Jotform, Fillout, and Typeform ship regularly. If long-term platform evolution matters, Wufoo is a defensive pick.</p>
<p><strong>Ultimate at $2,523/yr is not a fair price for what you get.</strong> Ultimate scales entry cap to 200,000 but does not add marquee features. Paying enterprise-tier money for a feature set that reads mid-2010s is hard to justify.</p>
<p><strong>Multi-user support is thin.</strong> The Free plan lists 3 users, but paid tiers are structured around a single admin. Team collaboration is not a Wufoo strength.</p>

<p><strong>You want AI form generation from prompt, question list, or PDF.</strong> <a href="https://formester.com/ai-form-generator/">Formester's AI form generator</a> builds a full form from any of three inputs. Wufoo has no comparable feature. If you're building forms regularly, the AI shortcut alone saves hours per month.</p>
<p><strong>You need offline capture for field teams.</strong> <a href="https://formester.com/features/offline-forms/">Formester's offline forms</a> ship as PWAs that queue responses on the device and sync when connectivity returns. Wufoo cannot do this at any tier.</p>
<p><strong>You want a modern editor and design system.</strong> <a href="https://formester.com/">Formester's editor</a> ships a modern drag-and-drop canvas, live preview, custom themes, and typography options that feel current. Wufoo's design language has not moved much since the mid-2010s.</p>

<p><strong>You want a product that ships new features.</strong> Formester ships new capabilities regularly. If long-term platform evolution matters, this is a defensive advantage over Wufoo's slower velocity.</p>
<h2>When Wufoo is the better pick</h2>

<p><strong>You're already on Wufoo and your workflow is stable.</strong> Switching costs (migrating forms, retraining teammates, rebuilding integrations) are real. If Wufoo works for you and you don't need modern features, staying is defensible.</p>
<p><strong>You want a simple form with reports and nothing more.</strong> Wufoo's reports feature is still one of the cleanest one-click implementations. For a single admin who wants forms plus dashboards without much fuss, Wufoo is fine.</p>
<p><strong>You're on the Wufoo Free plan and it fits your entire use case.</strong> 5 forms, 100 entries per month, 3 users is enough for a tiny team's contact form or internal signup. Formester Free is more generous, but if you don't need the extra headroom, Wufoo works.</p>
<p><strong>You value the SurveyMonkey ecosystem tie-in.</strong> If your team also uses SurveyMonkey and wants forms adjacent to that ecosystem, Wufoo has parent-company integration. That's a specific fit, not a common one.</p>
<h2>The final verdict</h2>

<p>Wufoo is a <strong>simple, functional form builder that made its reputation in the 2010s and has coasted since</strong>. If you already use it and it works, there's no urgent reason to switch. If you're evaluating fresh, you'll find a more modern feature set at similar or better price with almost any current competitor.</p>
