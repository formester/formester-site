<p style="font-size: inherit;">Three different jobs share this one search, which is why half the results for it are forum threads. Find the row that matches what you are actually trying to do, because the answers have nothing in common.</p>

<blockquote><p>The setting is <strong>Settings</strong> then <strong>Responses</strong> then <strong>Send responders a copy of their response</strong>, and it is greyed out until <strong>Collect email addresses</strong> is on. It only applies going forward: it cannot send copies for submissions you already have.</p></blockquote>

<h2 id="own-copy">1. You filled in a form and want your own answers</h2>

<p>This is the most common version of the search and the one with the least satisfying answer. Whether you can get your answers back was decided by the form owner before you hit submit.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Check your inbox first.</strong> If the owner enabled receipts, Google emailed your answers at submission time. Search your mail for the form's title rather than the word Google.</li>
<li><strong>Look for an edit link.</strong> If the owner also allowed response editing, that same email carries an <strong>Edit your response</strong> link, which reopens your submission with your answers still filled in.</li>
<li><strong>If there is no email, there is no self-service route.</strong> Google Forms keeps no list of forms you have submitted. Ask whoever owns the form to look you up.</li>
</ul>
<p>Taking a screenshot before you submit is the unglamorous fix, and it is the only one that does not depend on someone else's settings.</p>

<h2 id="auto-send">2. You own the form and want respondents to get a copy</h2>

<p>This is the setting people are usually looking for, and it has one prerequisite that trips everyone up.</p>
<ol>
<li>Open the form and click <strong>Settings</strong>.</li>
<li>Expand <strong>Responses</strong>.</li>
<li>Set <strong>Collect email addresses</strong> to a collecting option. Until you do, the next control stays greyed out.</li>
<li>Set <strong>Send responders a copy of their response</strong> to <strong>Always</strong>, or to <strong>When requested</strong> if you would rather each person opt in with a tickbox.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Where it stops.</strong> The copy is a plain-text email of the answers, not a document. There is no template, no branding and no attachment, and the setting is not retroactive: everyone who submitted before you switched it on gets nothing.</p>

<h2 id="copy-form">3. You want to duplicate the form itself</h2>

<p>A smaller share of this search is people wanting a second copy of the form, usually to reuse it next term or next cycle.</p>
<p>Open the form, use the three-dot menu and choose <strong>Make a copy</strong>. You get a fresh form with the same questions and settings.</p>
<p><strong>What does not come with it.</strong> The responses. A copied form starts empty, and it gets its own response sheet. That is usually what you want for a new cycle and a nasty surprise if you assumed the data travelled. To move the data instead, export the CSV or copy the linked Sheet.</p>

<h2 id="why-plain">Why the confirmation email looks so plain</h2>

<p>Google's receipt is a rendering of your answers, not a document you control.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>No branding.</strong> No logo, no colours, no sender name of your choosing.</li>
<li><strong>No attachment.</strong> Nothing the recipient can file, forward as a record, or sign.</li>
<li><strong>No custom wording.</strong> You cannot add a thank-you note, next steps or a link.</li>
</ul>
<p>For a class exit ticket that is fine. For an order, a booking or a consent form, a plain list of answers in an email body is not a receipt anyone would keep.</p>

<section class="faq">
<h2>Google Forms response copy FAQ</h2>
<details><summary>Does Google Forms send you a copy of your response?</summary><p>Only if the form owner turned it on. The form must collect email addresses and the owner must enable Send responders a copy of their response. Without both, nothing is emailed and there is no way to retrieve your answers yourself.</p></details>
<details><summary>What does "Send me a copy of my responses" mean in Google Forms?</summary><p>It is the respondent-facing version of the setting. When the owner chooses When requested, each person gets a tickbox at the end of the form; ticking it emails them their answers. Choosing Always sends the copy without asking.</p></details>
<details><summary>How do I get a copy of my Google Form response after submitting?</summary><p>Search your inbox for the form's title. If a receipt was sent, it is there. If not, there is no self-service option, because Google Forms keeps no record of forms you have submitted. Ask the form owner to look up your response.</p></details>
<details><summary>How do I turn on response receipts in Google Forms?</summary><p>Open the form, click Settings, expand Responses, set Collect email addresses to a collecting option, then set Send responders a copy of their response to Always or When requested.</p></details>
<details><summary>Why is "Send responders a copy" greyed out?</summary><p>Because Collect email addresses is off. Google has no address to send to until that is enabled, so the control stays disabled.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can I send copies for responses already submitted?</summary><p>No. The setting only applies to submissions made after you switch it on. Earlier respondents get nothing, and there is no way to send retroactively from within Google Forms.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can I make a copy of a Google Form with its responses?</summary><p>No. Make a copy duplicates the questions and settings only, and the new form starts with an empty response set and its own sheet. To keep the data, export the CSV or copy the linked Google Sheet separately.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can the confirmation email be branded or include a PDF?</summary><p>Not in Google Forms. The receipt is a plain-text list of the answers with no logo, custom wording or attachment. Sending a branded document per response needs a builder that generates one.</p></details>
